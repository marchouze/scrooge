## 1. Role purpose and regulatory anchor

### 1.1 Why this seat exists

The Hoz bank-entity (Hoz Bank Limited) and the Hoz securities-entity (Hoz Securities Limited) are both **accountable institutions** under FIC Act 38 of 2001 Schedule 1 — banks under item 6, persons authorised to deal in securities under item 13. Each accountable institution must, by statute, designate:

- **a Money Laundering Reporting Officer (MLRO)** under FIC Act s.43A — single point of contact for Suspicious Transaction Reports (STRs), Cash Threshold Reports (CTRs), Suspicious Activity Reports (SARs), Terrorist-Property Reports (TPRs); the named submitter to the FIC's gO!AML system; and
- **a Compliance Officer for the FIC obligations** under FIC Act s.43B — accountable for the Risk Management and Compliance Programme (RMCP) end-to-end under FIC ss.42–42A; runs CDD/EDD (FIC ss.21–28A), record-keeping, sanctions-screening operations.

Separately, every responsible-party body that processes personal information must designate, under POPIA Act 4 of 2013:

- **a POPIA Information Officer (IO)** under POPIA s.56 — primary contact for the Information Regulator, manages PAIA requests, handles s.22 breach notifications, oversees the lawful-processing register and cross-border-transfer governance under POPIA s.72.

POPIA s.56(b) **defaults the IO to the CEO** if no separate designation is made; SARB Prudential Authority and Information Regulator published guidance both expect a regulated financial institution to designate an IO **away from the CEO**. The expectation is precedent / supervisory practice, not statute (per Mira+Zara Q3) — but it is binding in practice.

The seat is **mandatory separate from the CEO** because:

- **CEO-as-MLRO is treated as a structural defect** under FIC's published RMCP guidance — the second-line check on STR escalation cannot be the CEO whose institution the STR concerns. (Mira+Zara Q3 confirmed this is published-guidance / supervisory-precedent binding, not statute.)
- **CEO-as-IO** is the POPIA s.56(b) statutory default but is supervisory-precedent-overridden for regulated financial institutions.

### 1.2 The triple-hat — workload-concentration, not statutory-defect

The MLRO + FIC CO + POPIA IO triple-hat is **statutorily compliant**: each merger is contemplated by the regulatory regime and routinely practised at SA Tier-2 / boutique banks and asset managers. Mira (Compliance / RegTech engineer) + Zara (Chief Compliance Officer) Q1 (`Owner Inbox/2026-05-09_mira-zara_concentration-risk-conduct-confirmation.md`) confirmed:

> "Each statutory merger is contemplated; SA practice routinely co-locates MLRO + FIC CO; POPIA s.56 explicitly permits IO designation. The triple-hat is a workload concentration, not a statutory defect."

The licence-application narrative defends the triple-hat on three grounds:

1. **Engineering substrate carries the operational load.** Mira (Compliance / RegTech engineer; reports to Zara) runs the screening engine, transaction-monitoring pipeline, FIC-submission draft pipeline, and obligations-register curator function. Iris (Information Officer governance — agent fleet) runs the POPIA programme substrate (lawful-processing register, retention schedule, consent and notice governance, breach-notification workflow). The human triple-hatted Lead is the regulator-facing designation those agents' outputs flow through — not the operational executor.
2. **Alternates are split** (Mira+Zara §4.2; Owen final composition paper §5): Deputy POPIA IO = Company Secretary (seat #4); MLRO-alternate = AC-Chair NED (seat #1). This avoids a single-point-of-failure at the triple-hat seat and at any one alternate.
3. **Institutional-only counterparty book** (no retail customers pre-licence-day; small institutional book at commencement-of-trading) keeps the FIC volume low at licence-day. The triple-hat is operationally manageable for the bank's posture.

## 2. Mandate scope

### 2.1 In scope (the human triple-hat)

- **MLRO duties under FIC s.43A** — receives internal STR escalations (Zara's `STRCandidate` events; Mira's transaction-monitoring outputs); exercises MLRO judgement on the FIC s.29 reportability test; signs STR / SAR / CTR / TPR submissions to the FIC's gO!AML system; manages tipping-off discipline under FIC s.29(3); sole point of contact with FIC supervisory staff on STR matters.
- **FIC Compliance Officer duties under FIC s.43B** — accountable for the bank's RMCP end-to-end (FIC ss.42–42A); RMCP custodian; signs RMCP version cycles within Board-approved framework; runs the bank's CDD/EDD (FIC ss.21–28A) and record-keeping discipline; sanctions-screening operational accountability (UNSC, OFAC, EU, UK HMT, DTI POCDATARA lists); FIC annual return; FIC liaison on RMCP and supervisory inspections.
- **POPIA Information Officer duties under POPIA s.56** — designation lodgment with the Information Regulator (CEO act, IO drafts the form); manages PAIA requests under PAIA Act 2 of 2000; handles POPIA s.22 breach notifications to the Information Regulator and data subjects; oversees lawful-processing register (Iris-substrate-curated); cross-border-transfer governance under POPIA s.72; data-subject-rights workflow (access, correction, deletion, objection); annual POPIA submissions to the Information Regulator.

- **Regulator-engagement** on conduct and AML/CFT — day-to-day relationship with FIC, the Information Regulator, and (for Hoz Securities Limited under FAIS) FSCA conduct supervision.
- **Multi-entity application** — same human serves MLRO + FIC CO for both Hoz Bank Limited and Hoz Securities Limited (both accountable institutions under FIC Schedule 1; Owen final composition §7A.3); same human serves IO for Hoz Bank Limited and Hoz Securities Limited (Iris's per-entity scoping); the group-entity (Hoz Group Limited) is **not** an accountable institution and does not require these designations at the group level.

## 4. Fit-and-proper considerations

The seat is a **designated officer** under multiple regulatory regimes, each with its own fit-and-proper assessment:

### 4.1 Banks Act fit-and-proper

Banks Act 94 of 1990 s.60 + SARB Prudential Authority fit-and-proper standards apply because the Compliance Lead is a designated officer of Hoz Bank Limited. The PA's expectations cover honesty/integrity, competence, financial soundness, and absence of conflicts. Sade's PROC-FAIS-KI-FAP-01 substrate template (PR #69, merged) provides the file structure — extended for the multi-entity bank / securities / group triangulation per Owen final composition §7A.2.

### 4.2 FIC fit-and-proper

FIC Act + FIC published RMCP guidance applies for the MLRO and FIC Compliance Officer designations. Practical assessment items: clean FIC supervisory record (no prior fit-and-proper findings); CISA professional standing in good order; demonstrable independence from the first line; demonstrable independence from the CEO.

### 4.3 POPIA Information Officer suitability

POPIA s.56 + Information Regulator's October 2021 guidance recommends designation of an IO with appropriate authority, resources, and seniority. Practical assessment items: demonstrable POPIA-programme authority at prior employer; clean Information Regulator supervisory record (no prior breach-notification failures); seniority sufficient to carry s.22 breach-notification authority without escalation friction.

### 4.4 FAIS fit-and-proper (if FSP-licensed slice carries Compliance Officer designation)

Per D-FSP-LICENCE-NECESSITY confirm-A, the bank pursues an FSP licence. The FAIS Compliance Officer under FAIS s.17 is a separate designation from the FAIS Key Individual (Saskia steady-state). If the Compliance Lead carries the FAIS s.17 designation, FAIS Determination of Fit and Proper Requirements 2017 applies — RE1 qualification, integrity, competence, operational ability.

### 4.5 SARB PA + Information Regulator non-objection

The licence-application file declares the named human in the SARB application; SARB PA reviews fit-and-proper as part of the licence-application assessment. The Information Regulator does not formally pre-clear IO designations but receives the lodgment after CEO designation; the licence-application narrative pre-engages the Information Regulator if the bank's pre-application strategy includes early IR notice.

### 4.6 Automatic disqualifiers (per Nolan recruitment scope §7)

- Concurrent compliance-officer seat at a competitor SA bank.
- Prior FIC fit-and-proper findings or supervisory action against the candidate.
- Prior POPIA breach-notification failure under the candidate's accountability.
- Cannot demonstrate substantive independence from the CEO.
- Family / close personal relationship with Marc (CEO).

## 5. Workload concentration analysis

The triple-hat is a **workload concentration**, not a statutory defect (Mira+Zara Q1). This section analyses the operational-manageability question — *given the bank's posture, is the workload manageable?* — and surfaces the split criteria for franchise growth.

### 5.1 Why the triple-hat is operationally manageable at licence-day

- **Institutional-only counterparty book.** The bank's strategic foundation (`project_strategic_foundation.md`) is institutional-only global-markets trading: no retail customers, no consumer credit, no transactional banking for individuals. The FIC volumetric load (CDD onboarding, transaction monitoring, STR throughput) at an institutional-only desk is materially lower than at a retail bank.
- **Small counterparty book pre-licence-day.** Niko (Sales / CRM engineer) lifecycle activates at licence-day; the institutional-onboarding pipeline is staged for first-onboarding waves of ~10–30 institutional counterparties pre-commencement. CDD/EDD volume at this scale is single-digit-cases-per-week, not hundreds-per-day.
- **Engineering substrate carries the operational load.** Mira's screening engine, transaction-monitoring pipeline, FIC-submission draft pipeline, and obligations-register curator function are autonomous-by-default Principle-7 agents. The human Lead exercises judgement on edge cases and signs final dispositions; the volume-handling is engineered.
- **Iris's POPIA programme substrate.** The lawful-processing register, retention schedule, consent and notice governance, and breach-notification draft pipeline are Iris-curated. The human Lead carries the s.56 designation and signs s.22 notifications; the programme operation is engineered.
- **Alternates split.** Deputy POPIA IO = Company Secretary (seat #4) absorbs PAIA-request load and IO continuity; MLRO-alternate = AC-Chair NED (seat #1) absorbs MLRO continuity and out-of-hours STR-decision back-up.

### 5.2 When to split

The triple-hat splits when **any one** of the following thresholds is crossed:

| Threshold | Slice that splits first | Successor seat shape |
|---|---|---|
| Counterparty book exceeds ~150 institutional clients OR the bank adds a retail or SME slice | Split POPIA IO from MLRO + FIC CO; designate a separate full-time IO | Separate IO seat reporting to Iris (governance) |
| FIC supervisory cadence intensifies (annual on-site inspection becomes biannual; thematic-review participation becomes quarterly) | Split MLRO from FIC CO; named MLRO becomes a full-time seat carrying STR throughput | Separate MLRO seat reporting to Zara (CCO) |
| FSCA conduct-supervision intensifies (TCF on-site assessment + FAIS thematic + advice-record review concurrent) | Split FAIS s.17 Compliance Officer from FIC slice; named FAIS CO becomes a full-time seat | Separate FAIS Compliance Officer seat |
| POPIA enforcement matures (Information Regulator's enforcement-direction cadence picks up; class-action POPIA litigation lands in SA) | Same as first row — split IO first | Separate IO seat |
| Notifiable POPIA breach OR named-individual STR pattern indicates capacity strain | Immediate split — substrate flag | Triggered review by Vera + Thandiwe |

The licence-application file declares the triple-hat **with the explicit expansion commitment** that any one of the above thresholds triggers a split, with named timelines per threshold.
