## Purpose of BA 400

Form BA 400 is the prudential return through which the bank reports its **leverage ratio** — the **non-risk-based backstop** to the risk-based capital-adequacy ratios on BA 100. The leverage ratio answers a deliberately simpler question than the CAR: *how much gross balance-sheet-and-off-balance-sheet exposure is the bank running against its highest-quality (Tier-1) capital, irrespective of how risky each exposure is judged to be?* It catches the failure mode where a bank's RWA-denominated ratios look healthy because of low risk-weight buckets while **gross leverage quietly builds** (the canonical pre-2008 failure). BCBS LEV20.2 states its dual intent verbatim: *"(1) restrict the build-up of leverage in the banking sector to avoid destabilising deleveraging processes …; and (2) reinforce the risk-based capital requirements with a simple, non-risk-based 'backstop' measure."*

The headline leverage identity (BCBS LEV20.3–20.7; Reg 38; `ba-400-leverage-ratio.ts` `generateLeverageRatio()`):

```
leverageRatio = Tier 1 capital / total leverage exposure measure   ≥ 3%   [LEV20.7]

where  Tier 1 capital  =  CET1 + Additional Tier 1   (the SAME numerator as the BA 100
                          risk-based ratios — LEV20.4: "the capital measure used for the
                          leverage ratio … is the Tier 1 capital measure applicable at
                          that time under the risk-based framework")

total exposure measure =  on-balance-sheet exposures                       [LEV20.6(1)]
                        + derivative exposures (SA-CCR: RC + α·PFE)         [LEV20.6(2); LEV30.14–.17]
                        + securities-financing-transaction (SFT) exposures [LEV20.6(3)]
                        + off-balance-sheet items, post credit-conversion-factor (CCF) [LEV20.6(4)]
```

The crucial contrast with BA 100: the leverage exposure measure is **not risk-weighted** and (for on-balance-sheet exposures) **does not net long against short** (LEV30.6–.8) — it is closer to a gross-accounting view, complemented by specific treatments for derivatives, SFTs, and off-balance-sheet items (LEV30.1). A misstated *numerator* (Tier 1) or a misstated *denominator* (any of the four exposure components) misstates the backstop — which is why BA 400 and BA 100 share the **same Tier-1 source of truth** (`leverage-ratio-metrics.ts` re-uses `computeCapitalMetrics`).

**The bank's actual leverage position today.** In the build phase the bank holds **no real capital** and has **no real positions** (CLAUDE.md "build phase vs licence-day" — the R300m is a *licence-day target*, not a present balance). The leverage substrate runs against the **ICAAP v1 confirmed build-phase baseline** (`D-MARKETS-CAPITAL-TIME-SHAPE`, CEO-approved 2026-05-12): a R300,000,000 Tier-1 envelope (`BUILD_PHASE_LEVERAGE_BASELINE_TIER1_MINOR = 30_000_000_000` cents) against a **zero exposure measure** (`BUILD_PHASE_LEVERAGE_BASELINE_EXPOSURE_MINOR = 0` — no positions, no derivatives, no commitments yet). With zero exposure the ratio is `Infinity` (regulator-floor compliant by convention — same divide-by-zero treatment as the BA 100 case). When the first rehearsal trade settles or the first commitment is booked, the live exposure-measure projection takes over and produces a real number; until then the build-phase baseline is the floor while the substrate comes up (`leverage-ratio-metrics.ts` `readLiveExposureDecomposition` returns `null` at v0 — the substrate gap surfaced below).

## The capital measure — the Tier-1 numerator (shared with BA 100)

The leverage-ratio numerator is **Tier 1 capital** — Common Equity Tier 1 (CET1) + Additional Tier 1 (AT1), as defined for the risk-based framework (BCBS LEV20.4 verbatim: *"The capital measure for the leverage ratio is Tier 1 capital — comprising Common Equity Tier 1 and/or Additional Tier 1 instruments — as defined in CAP10 … the Tier 1 capital measure applicable at that time under the risk-based framework"*). This is the **same number** the BA 100 Tier-1 ratio is computed against — the leverage and risk-based views deliberately share one Tier-1 source of truth so they cannot drift. In the substrate this is enforced structurally: `leverage-ratio-metrics.ts` calls `computeCapitalMetrics()` and reads `availableCapitalMinor` as the Tier-1 numerator; the BA 400 generator's `tier1CapitalMinor` input is documented as *"typically the `netTier1Minor` field of a `Ba100Output.capitalStack` — pass the same number the BA 100 ratios are computed against to guarantee consistency."*

**Tier-1 deductions interaction (no double-count).** Any item deducted from Tier 1 capital under the risk-based framework (goodwill, intangibles, DTAs, etc.) is **also deducted from the leverage exposure measure** so it is not counted twice (BCBS LEV30.3: *"any item deducted from Tier 1 capital … may be deducted from the exposure measure"*; LEV30.4). The `ba-400-leverage-ratio.ts` `onBalanceSheetExposureMinor` field documents this verbatim treatment: *"gross carrying value of all assets net of specific provisions, less the same Tier-1-deductions that have already been subtracted from the capital numerator (BCBS §149 — avoids double-counting)."*

## The exposure measure — the non-risk-based denominator

The denominator is the **total leverage exposure measure** — the sum of four components (BCBS LEV20.5–20.6; the `LeverageExposureDecomposition` interface; `ba-400-leverage-ratio.ts`):

| Component | BCBS LEV | Treatment | In-repo field |
|---|---|---|---|
| **On-balance-sheet exposures** | LEV20.6(1); LEV30.6–.10 | Gross accounting carrying value of all assets (cash, securities, loans, repo receivables), net of specific provisions, less Tier-1 deductions (no double-count); **no netting of long vs short** (LEV30.6); excludes on-BS derivative + SFT exposures (counted in their own components). | `onBalanceSheetExposureMinor` |
| **Derivative exposures** | LEV20.6(2); LEV30.14–.17 | Replacement cost (RC) + potential future exposure (PFE), measured via **SA-CCR** (α = 1.4 per LEV30.17(1): *"alpha is 1.4"*). Bilateral netting only under a legally valid master-netting agreement (LEV30.18–.20); collateral grossed up where balance-sheet-netted (LEV30.23). | `derivativeExposureMinor` |
| **SFT exposures** | LEV20.6(3); LEV30 (SFT section) | Repos, reverse repos, securities lending / borrowing, margin lending — computed gross of cash payable / receivable; bilateral netting permitted only where the master-netting-agreement conditions are met. | `sftExposureMinor` |
| **Off-balance-sheet items** | LEV20.6(4); LEV30 (OBS section) | Undrawn commitments, trade-finance, guarantees, note-issuance facilities — converted to a credit-equivalent by a **credit-conversion factor (CCF)**, banded 10%–100% by commitment type (10% unconditionally cancellable; 40% other commitments; 20% trade LCs; 100% guarantees). | `offBalanceSheetExposurePostCcfMinor` |

`totalExposureMeasureMinor = onBalanceSheetExposureMinor + derivativeExposureMinor + sftExposureMinor + offBalanceSheetExposurePostCcfMinor` (the generator sums the four; every component is reported as a **non-negative magnitude** — the generator rejects negative or non-finite inputs and does not net beyond the framework's allowance).

**The bank's leverage exposure today is dominated by the global-markets desks.** When live, the on-balance-sheet component is driven by correspondent-bank nostro balances and cash positions; the derivative component by the desks' FX-forward / -swap and OTC-IRD counterparty exposure (SA-CCR EAD); the SFT component by repo / reverse-repo; and the off-balance-sheet component by undrawn commitments. This is the **same exposure universe** that drives the credit-risk return (BA 200) — but the leverage return measures it **gross and un-risk-weighted**, which is exactly the point of the backstop.

## The derivative-exposure component — SA-CCR (D7/2020)

For the **derivative-exposure** leg of the leverage exposure measure, the EAD is measured under the **Standardised Approach for Counterparty Credit Risk (SA-CCR)** — the same methodology used for the BA 200 / BA 210 counterparty-credit-RWA computation, transposed via **Directive D7/2020** ("Calculation of derivative exposure amount for the purposes of determining the leverage ratio", `ORG-PR-66`):

```
derivative exposure  =  α × ( RC + PFE )        with  α = 1.4          [LEV30.17(1); D7/2020; BCBS d317 §10]
PFE  =  multiplier × Σ AddOn    (over-collateralisation shrinks the multiplier to a 5% floor)
```

BCBS LEV30.17(1) verbatim confirms the alpha: *"alpha is 1.4"*. D7/2020 prescribes SA-CCR **replacing the Current Exposure Method (CEM)** for the leverage-ratio derivative exposure. This is **built** (`prototype/platform/risk/sa-ccr/` — `ead.ts` `computeEad`, `pfe-addon.ts` `ALPHA_SA_CCR = 1.4`, `replacement-cost.ts`) and is the live measurement path for the markets desks' derivative counterparty exposure. The BA 400 generator's `derivativeExposureMinor` field is the seam: at v0.1 the caller passes the SA-CCR EAD value (summed across netting sets); the optional `saccrEventId` field threads the `SaCcrEadComputed` event_id through to preserve the **Principle-1 chain-of-custody** when the periodic SA-CCR projection lands.

The SA-CCR *engine* is built and clause-cited at the BCBS level (CRE52 §52.5 / d317 §10 inline cites); the D7/2020 *sub-clause* mapping is the gap. ## Minimum requirement and the (non-applicable) G-SIB buffer

The bank must hold a leverage ratio **at or above 3% at all times** (BCBS LEV20.7 verbatim: *"Banks must meet a 3% leverage ratio minimum requirement at all times."*; `BCBS_LEVERAGE_RATIO_REGULATORY_MINIMUM = 0.03`). SARB adopts the Basel 3% floor unchanged through Reg 38 (the `ba-400-leverage-ratio.ts` `regulatoryMinimumRatio` defaults to 0.03; the field is present so a future Pillar-2-style add-on can be absorbed without a schema change).

| Layer | Leverage minimum | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| **Base regulatory minimum** | **3.0%** | BCBS LEV20.7; Reg 38; Banks Act s.70 |
| **+ G-SIB leverage buffer** | +50% of the G-SIB higher-loss-absorbency requirement | BCBS LEV40.2 — **NOT_APPLICABLE** (Hoz Bank is **not** a G-SIB at build-phase; the LEV40 buffer is a no-op) |
| **+ RAS management buffer (appetite)** | green ≥ 4.5% / amber 4.0–4.5% / red < 3.5% (critical < 3.5%) | Internal RAS gold-plate (`D-RAS`); `leverage.threshold.{green,amber,red}` |

The **G-SIB leverage buffer** (BCBS LEV40 — set at 50% of the G-SIB higher-loss-absorbency requirement, with the same five-bucket capital-conservation / distribution-constraint mechanics as the risk-based buffers) is a **no-op for this bank** — Hoz Bank is not a G-SIB, so LEV40 never binds (consistent with the D-SIB/G-SIB no-op posture on [`ba-100.md`](ba-100.md)).

**The RAS gold-plate (the canonical GOLD_PLATES case).** The bank's Risk Appetite Statement operates a **stricter** band than the 3% Basel floor — green ≥ 4.5%, amber 4.0–4.5%, red < 4.0% with a critical sub-status < 3.5% (`leverage-ratio-metrics.ts`; `leverage.threshold.green = 0.045`, `amber = 0.04`, `red = 0.035`). This is the canonical GOLD_PLATES case in the Basel-adoption layer: `resolveApplicableRule("za", "urn:reg:bcbs:lev:20.7", asOf)` returns `adoptionType: "GOLD_PLATES", stricter: true` — the 3% baseline is preserved; the local tightening is the delta. The `belowRegulatoryFloor` flag fires separately when the ratio drops below the hard 3% floor (distinct from the RAS amber / red appetite bands).

## Binding obligations on the bank (summary)

1. **Maintain a leverage ratio of not less than 3% at all times** — Tier 1 capital ÷ total leverage exposure measure ≥ 3% (BCBS LEV20.7; Reg 38; Banks Act s.70; `ORG-PR-05`). The non-risk-based backstop, reported on BA 400, **not folded into the CAR**.
2. **Use the risk-based-framework Tier-1 capital measure as the numerator** — the same CET1 + AT1 figure as the BA 100 risk-based ratios, less the same Tier-1 deductions (no double-count) (BCBS LEV20.4; LEV30.3; `leverage-ratio-metrics.ts` shared `computeCapitalMetrics` source).
3. **Compute the total exposure measure** as the sum of on-balance-sheet + derivative + SFT + off-balance-sheet (post-CCF) exposures, un-risk-weighted, without netting long vs short on-balance-sheet (BCBS LEV20.5–20.6; LEV30; `ba-400-leverage-ratio.ts`).
4. **Measure the derivative-exposure component under SA-CCR** — EAD = 1.4 × (RC + PFE), replacing CEM (BCBS LEV30.14–.17; D7/2020; `ORG-PR-66`; `ORG-PR-SACCR-001..010`; built `platform/risk/sa-ccr/`).
5. **Apply credit-conversion factors to off-balance-sheet items** before adding them to the exposure measure (BCBS LEV20.6(4); LEV30; CCF banding 10–100% — engine gap, caller pre-applies at v0.1).
6. **Maintain the leverage management buffer above the regulatory floor** — RAS §B3 appetite (green ≥ 4.5%; `D-RAS`; internal stricter overlay; calibration pending).
7. **Submit form BA 400** to the PA per Annexures 17A/17B of D5/2025 and the Regulations, on the prescribed (monthly) cadence, accompanied by a certified BA 099; the return content is confidential (D5/2025 §2.1.18; Reg 38(1); `ORG-PR-RETURNS-017`; `ORG-PR-RETURNS-001`; exact cadence + Annexure 17B line schema counsel-gated).
8. **Submit form BA 700** (PVA + additional leverage-ratio disclosure) per D5/2025 §2.1.23 — the supplementary leverage-disclosure return distinct from the BA 400 primary computation (`ORG-PR-RETURNS-022`).

**No-silent-zeros posture (D-TRUSTED-FIGURES-PROGRAM-V1).** The generator rejects negative or non-finite exposure components and negative Tier-1 capital (throws — *"Negative Tier 1 capital is a special case that requires Board attention before reporting; the generator does not silently render a negative-numerator ratio"*), validates the ISO-4217 functional currency, and renders a **divide-by-zero ratio as `Number.POSITIVE_INFINITY`** (no exposure ⇒ no leverage ⇒ infinite ratio, surfaced loudly, treated as compliant) rather than a silent zero. The generator surfaces explicit `[citation: TBC]` placeholders into the output `placeholders` array (fixture-grade exposure inputs; derivative-exposure not chained to a `SaCcrEadComputed` event_id) so the recon pipeline tracks placeholder density. The `leverage-ratio-metrics` projection distinguishes the **build-phase baseline** from a **live exposure read** in its `note`. When real positions + real capital land, the generator computes the live leverage ratio over real events.
