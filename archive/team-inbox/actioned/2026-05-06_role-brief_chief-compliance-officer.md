# Role brief — Chief Compliance Officer (CCO)

**Author:** PAX
**Date:** 2026-05-06
**For:** Nolan

## 1. Role title and one-line purpose

**Chief Compliance Officer (CCO)** — governance owner of regulatory and conduct compliance across the bank; named **Money Laundering Reporting Officer (MLRO)** and **Compliance Officer** under the FIC Act; second-line counterpart to the CRO on conduct, financial-crime, and regulatory matters.

## 2. Why this role exists

Mira is the compliance / RegTech *engineer* — she translates rule-text into running controls and curates the obligations register. The named regulatory designations (MLRO, FIC Compliance Officer, accountable institution-level compliance officer under FAIS) are governance accountabilities, not engineering ones. Conflating them with Mira's seat would collapse the second line. SARB Prudential Authority and FSCA expect to engage with a named CCO at executive level. The FIC Act expects the MLRO to be a sufficiently senior person with direct access to the board. King IV expects compliance oversight as a board-reported function.

The CEO has confirmed (2026-05-06) that this is to be a separate hire, distinct from Mira.

## 3. Scope of work (priority order)

1. **Compliance programme governance.** Owns the bank's compliance universe; signs the compliance plan; presents to the BRC and Audit Committee; engages the regulators on conduct, AML/CFT, and FAIS.
2. **MLRO function under FIC Act.** Named MLRO; receives internal STRs from Mira's pipeline, exercises judgement, files to the FIC under sections 28, 28A, 29; maintains the FIC liaison.
3. **FIC Act Compliance Officer.** Named accountable-institution compliance officer; signs the Risk Management and Compliance Programme (RMCP) under section 42; oversees CDD, EDD, screening, and monitoring at the policy level.
4. **FAIS conduct governance.** As the bank carries an FSP licence (when applicable), oversees the FAIS programme — Key Individual designations, representative register, fit-and-proper, complaints, advice records, fee disclosure.
5. **Sanctions and PEP governance.** Sets policy on sanctions exposure (UN, OFAC, EU, UK HMT, DTI Targeted Financial Sanctions); governance over match handling; escalation path for true positives; coordinates with Mira on engineering, with Helena on appetite.
6. **Conduct risk and TCF.** Treating Customers Fairly programme governance; conduct-risk taxonomy; complaints oversight; conduct-related KRIs reported into the BRC.
7. **POPIA programme governance (alongside Iris).** Coordinates with the Information Officer (Iris) on POPIA — the CCO holds the regulatory-compliance dimension; Iris holds the privacy-officer dimension.
8. **Regulatory change management at the policy level.** Mira ingests rule changes operationally; the CCO governs the policy response and the board reporting on regulatory change.
9. **Compliance monitoring plan.** Annual plan, themed reviews, exception reporting; outputs are queries (P3) over the event log produced by Mira's controls.
10. **Whistleblowing oversight (compliance dimension).** Coordinates with the Company Secretary; CCO investigates compliance-related whistles; preserves third-line independence for Vera.

## 4. Required expertise

- South African financial-services regulation: FIC Act, FAIS Act, Banks Act, FSR Act / Twin Peaks, COFI Bill trajectory, POPIA.
- AML/CFT — FATF Recommendations, SA mutual-evaluation history, FIC Guidance Notes especially GN 7 (RBA).
- Sanctions regimes — UN, OFAC, EU, UK HMT, DTI Targeted Financial Sanctions list under POCDATARA.
- Conduct of Financial Institutions thinking — FSCA conduct standards, TCF outcomes.
- Compliance-programme governance at a regulated bank — RMCP authorship under FIC.
- MLRO operational practice — judgement on STR/SAR/CTR/TPR decisions.
- Senior regulator-engagement experience.

## 5. Desirable expertise

- Prior CCO, head of compliance, or named MLRO at a SA bank, large insurer, or large FSP.
- Compliance Institute of Southern Africa (CISA) practitioner status preferred — likely required for board-facing credibility.
- LLB or LLM in financial-services regulation desirable but not essential.
- Track record of regulator engagement under remediation conditions (a CCO who has been through an enforcement matter elsewhere is an asset, not a liability).

## 6. Regulatory / certification requirements

- **Fit and proper** under PA / FSCA standards. The CCO is a designated person under the regulator's lens.
- **MLRO designation under the FIC Act** — must meet the FIC's seniority and independence expectations.
- **FAIS Key Individual** status if the bank carries an FSP licence (relevant categories).
- CISA practitioner (Compliance Institute of Southern Africa) — strongly preferred.
- Working knowledge of POPIA sufficient to govern the programme alongside Iris.

## 8. Success criteria — first 90 days

- Risk Management and Compliance Programme (RMCP) authored and signed under FIC section 42.
- MLRO file open; STR filing pipeline live in test mode with Mira; first dry-run STR processed end-to-end.
- Compliance plan for the next 12 months presented to interim BRC.
- Sanctions, PEP, and adverse-media policy approved and register-linked.
- Conduct-risk taxonomy and TCF programme outline approved.
- POPIA programme governance interface with Iris documented.
- Compliance-related obligations register entries signed off jointly with Mira.

## 9. Principle alignment

**P1 — Events as source of truth.** Compliance decisions (STR escalations, sanctions hits, CDD risk-rating uplifts, FAIS suitability sign-offs) are events. The CCO's posture at any past as-of date is reproducible.

**P2 — Traceability.** Every policy, every control, every regulator submission cites the obligations-register entry that justifies it. The CCO's policy library is co-curated with Mira and register-linked.

**P3 — Cloud-native, no manual.** RMCP, compliance plan, monitoring outputs, regulator submissions — produced from data, not assembled in spreadsheets. Manual exception handling is itself a tracked event.

**P4 — Security by design.** STR / SAR / TPR data is among the most sensitive in the bank — disclosure of STR existence is itself a criminal offence under FIC. The CCO ensures access discipline and integrity attestation around the MLRO function. Coordinated with Senna.

**P5 — Multi-everything.** Sanctions and PEP screening are multi-list, multi-jurisdictional. FATCA/CRS classifications carry multiple jurisdiction tags. As entities and jurisdictions are added, RMCP and conduct programme replicate per-entity, per-jurisdiction with register entries, not rewrites.
