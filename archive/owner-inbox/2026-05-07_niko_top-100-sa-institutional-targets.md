## 1. Framing

This is a **target list**, not an onboarding queue. My customer-lifecycle is paused under the AI-driven-bank reframe — no live FAIS-regulated activity, no signed agreements, no real clients until licence-day. What I can ship now, and what Saskia's franchise design explicitly authorises, is the institutional pipeline curation that lets us walk into licence-day with a Tier-1 counterparty book already half-warm.

**Fit, in this context.** The bank is an institutional global-markets house running on ~R300m of committed capital, ZAR-domiciled, single SA branch, full SARB Authorised-Dealer status. Product surface is JSE-listed SAGB + select corporate bonds + ZAR repo (market-making), JSE Top-40 equities + curated ETF list (agency at outset), ZARONIA-linked vanilla IRD (market-making to a narrow counterparty set), and the full FX surface to Authorised-Dealer scope. A "fit" counterparty is therefore an institutional principal who (a) operates a credible institutional rates / equities / FX book or hedging programme, (b) is plausibly ISDA-counterparty-grade, (c) has a SA touchpoint or SA-domiciled mandate, and (d) sits inside our credit-counterparty envelope as Helena scales it.

This list is a **research artefact**. The institutions named are public SA institutional counterparties — their reciprocal interest is unknown until soft-franchise touch lands.

## 2. Methodology

I worked top-down by segment, then per-segment by approximate scale, then re-ranked across segments by product-overlap and ISDA-readiness signal.

**Segments covered.** SA pension funds (public-sector via PIC and the major retirement funds; private-sector funds large enough to run segregated mandates); SA asset managers (the Tier-1 institutional houses plus the next layer); SA medical schemes (the largest principal-investing schemes); SA insurers split into long-term (life) and short-term; SA banks (peer-bank counterparties for IRD + FX + bonds); SA SOE treasuries (the active hedgers); SA corporates with regular hedging needs (top JSE-listed by market cap, excluding pure financials); SA listed property funds (REITs); SA hedge / alternative managers (FSCA Cat IIA); foreign banks with SA branches active in ZAR; foreign asset managers running SA-domiciled or SA-mandated books; multilateral / supranational SA touchpoints.

**Public sources.** JSE issuer lists (Top-40, Top-100, REIT board, Debt Market issuer set); FSCA FSP register (Cat I / II / IIA); SARB-licensed banks list and authorised-dealer list; PIC public mandate book and GEPF reports; ASISA membership (asset managers, life insurers); SAIA membership (short-term insurers); Council for Medical Schemes register; SAVCA membership (alts); Trade & Investment SA / DTIC SOE list; SA-domiciled subsidiaries of foreign banks and asset managers per SARB and FSCA listings; multilateral SA-presence per their own published programme books.

**Scoring.** Three axes, equal weight: **size** (AUM or balance-sheet, descriptive only), **product overlap** (does the entity actually transact in our product set, signalled by mandate book / treasury policy / public hedging disclosures), and **ISDA-readiness** (do they already maintain ISDAs with SA banks — proxy: are they routinely named in SA banks' counterparty disclosures and Tier-1-counterparty FAQs). Priority A is the top 30 across all segments; B is the next 50; C is aspirational / size-mismatch / lower fit.

**Exclusions.** Retail. SMME. Individuals. Offshore-only counterparties without SA touchpoint. SA-listed entities that are pure financial holdcos with no operating treasury. Anything where I would need non-public information to score.

## 5. Top-30 (Priority A) callouts

**SA banks (1–5).** All five are obligatory ISDA + GMRA + GMSLA counterparties for an institutional GM franchise. **Standard Bank** and **FirstRand (RMB)** are the deepest ZAR rates and FX market-makers and our most natural reciprocal-flow counterparties from day one. **Absa** brings ZAR repo depth and equity-derivative flow. **Nedbank** is a strong SAGB and corporate-credit counterparty. **Investec** is the premium private-banking-and-markets counterparty whose institutional book we will see reciprocally on the IRD line. Entry product for all five is multi-product; entry workflow is a single ISDA + ZA Schedule + CSA + GMRA package.

**Foreign banks with SA branches (91–93 prioritised into A).** **Citi**, **JPM**, and **HSBC** Johannesburg branches are first-tier ZAR market participants and structurally important reciprocal counterparties for cross-currency and large-ticket bond flow. Entry product is multi-product; entry workflow is the same documentation package routed through their SA branch desks.

**SA asset managers (7–12).** **Allan Gray**, **Coronation**, **Ninety One (SA book)**, **Stanlib**, **Old Mutual Investment Group**, and **Sanlam Investments** together represent the core institutional segregated-mandate flow in ZAR rates and equities. All are routinely on Tier-1 ISDA shelves with SA banks. Entry product mix is bond and equity flow; the IRD line opens once their LDI / overlay mandates engage. Imani's ISDA-template work converts directly into outreach packets here.

**SA long-term insurers (13–17).** **Sanlam**, **Old Mutual**, **Discovery (Life + Invest)**, **Liberty**, **Momentum Metropolitan** — each runs a balance-sheet IRD-hedging programme that is precisely our IRD product set, plus material SAGB and corporate-bond holdings. Discovery's hedging programme on its growing protection book is the cleanest IRD-overlap among the five. Entry product mix: rates + IRS overlay; bond inventory financing via repo as the relationship matures.

**SA short-term insurers (18–19).** **Santam** and **Hollard** carry meaningful liquid-asset portfolios (mainly SAGB and high-grade credit) under the Insurance Act prudential framework. Entry product is ZAR rates / bonds; IRS overlay is light at this scale, but the cash-bond and repo flow is real.

**SA SOE treasuries (20–21).** **Eskom** and **Transnet** are the two largest SOE issuers with active hedging programmes — cross-currency, IRS overlay, and FX-forward needs against their offshore debt. Both maintain ISDA shelves with SA banks. Entry product is cross-currency + IRS hedging; FX is the natural extension under our Authorised-Dealer scope.

**SA listed corporates (22–28).** **Anglo American**, **Sasol**, **MTN**, **Vodacom**, **Naspers / Prosus**, **Bidvest**, **Shoprite** — the active hedgers in the JSE Top-40. Anglo and Sasol are the deepest commodity-exporter FX hedgers; MTN and Vodacom carry chronic cross-currency programmes against their pan-African operations; Naspers / Prosus has structural EUR/USD exposure; Shoprite runs USD-denominated import flows. Entry product is FX (hedging + spot + forward) with IRS overlay where the corporate runs term debt. Bidvest is the diversified-industrial outlier — multi-product, but lighter ticket size.

**SA pension funds (29 + 6 already at #6).** **PIC** (#6) and **GEPF** (#29, where directly mandated outside the PIC umbrella) — the deepest single source of SAGB demand in the country. Entry product is rates / bonds, with selective IRD overlay. Reciprocal flow is enormous.

**SA listed property (30).** **Growthpoint** is the cleanest single REIT IRD counterparty — large enough to maintain ISDA, with a chronic interest-rate hedging programme against its property debt. Entry product is OTC IRS hedging.

## 6. Engagement sequencing

**Phase 1 — pre-licence-day (now → licence-grant).**
Soft-franchise touch on the top 30. **Saskia** opens institutional doors at the head-of-treasury / head-of-trading / CIO level; **I provide** the introductory pack, the franchise summary, the indicative product menu, and the licence-day timeline; **Imani** prepares the ISDA + CSA negotiations-in-principle packet and parks it counterparty-by-counterparty; **Mira** runs sanctions / PEP / FIC-AML pre-screen on every counterparty before any meeting lands. Sequencing within the 30: SA banks (1–5) and foreign-bank SA branches (91–93) first — these are reciprocal-flow conversations, not sales conversations. Then asset managers (7–12) and life insurers (13–17) — they want to know who we are well before they pre-screen us as a new ISDA shelf. Then SOEs and corporates (20–28) — these conversations carry longer cycle times and are best opened at the Group-Treasurer level early.

**Phase 2 — licence-grant + 30 days.**
Convert the negotiations-in-principle to live ISDAs. Saskia's franchise design names "institutional minimum threshold of counterparties signed within four weeks of go-live" as the gating condition — that threshold lands on this list. Mira flips from pre-screen to live KYC under her FIC RMCP; my onboarding hand-off pipeline activates per `kyc-onboarding.md`; the first ZAR rates and FX tickets price on this counterparty set.

**Phase 3 — licence-grant + 90 days.**
Broaden from the top 30 to the full Priority-A set + the top 20 of Priority-B. Phase 3 also opens the ETF / authorised-participant path with the asset managers and the auth-dealer-FX programme with the corporate hedgers in earnest.
