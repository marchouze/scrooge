## §1 — What this packet is and isn't

This packet is the materials kit Saskia uses when she opens an institutional conversation during the build phase. It is a soft-franchise touch: an introduction, not an offer. The bank is in formation; no commercial activity is permitted ahead of licence-grant, and no participant in any conversation should imply otherwise.

I (Niko) curate and update this packet on the weekly Monday cadence. Imani prepares the legal-side ISDA / CSA outreach packet separately. Mira screens each named counterparty for sanctions / PEP / FIC AML risk class before any contact is made — that pre-screen is a hard gate, not a checkbox. Iris handles cross-border data-flow questions when conversations move toward live engagement post-licence-day.

What's NOT in this packet: commercial pricing, indicative quotes, product demonstrations, regulatory-submission paperwork, customer-onboarding documentation, signed agreements of any kind. None of those exist before licence-grant. Saskia's job in these conversations is to invite evaluation and gather signal, not to sell.

The 30 entries in §3 are the Priority-A subset of my top-100 list. The 17 ISDA-ready Y-marked names within those 30 are Imani's first-wave subjects for clause-library and bilateral-template work. Treat this packet as living: it updates as conversations land.

## §2 — The institutional one-pager (cover document)

This is the document Saskia hands a counterparty in the first conversation. It is the definitive single page about the bank.

### Narrative

We are an autonomous AI-run institutional global-markets bank in formation in South Africa. Our trading franchise covers JSE-listed bonds, JSE-listed equities, ZARONIA-linked vanilla OTC interest-rate derivatives (swaps, FRAs, OIS, basis swaps), and the full FX surface — vanilla spot, forward, and swap — under full SARB Authorised-Dealer status. We trade institutionally only. Our counterparties are banks, asset managers, pension funds, life and short-term insurers, SOE treasuries, and listed corporates with active hedging programmes.

We do not trade retail. We do not service SMME or individuals. We do not warehouse structured products at the outset; structured-product capability is an M5 work-stream, deferred until the franchise is established. We are not a CLS-direct member; FX settles via correspondent. We are not a SAMOS-direct participant; ZAR clears via sponsor bank under an indirect-participant arrangement.

Our committed-shareholder capital target is R300 million, sized against the franchise design's working ICAAP. The figure is real capital that lands on licence-day, not present balance — we are honest with counterparties that we are pre-licence. Our SARB Banks Act application is in progress; the licence-day timeline is real but unfixed. FSCA Conduct Standards alignment is engineering-substrate-complete.

Our operating posture is deliberate: capital-light, ops-light, sponsor-routed access to critical market infrastructure. The bet is the trading franchise's day-one franchise-build, not infrastructure ownership. This keeps our cost base disciplined and our counterparty-economics defensible.

We think this should interest you for four reasons. First, reciprocal liquidity: ZAR rates and FX market-making in size, with a counterparty roster designed around mutual flow rather than retail-distribution subsidy. Second, counterparty diversification: a credible non-Big-Four bilateral, ISDA-grade and prudentially supervised. Third, an AI-native execution stack with full event-sourced auditability — every trade, every screening decision, every disclosure, queryable to source. Fourth, a counterparty-centric posture: institutional-only, no retail distraction, no off-book activity, conduct discipline designed in.

We are not asking for a commitment. We are asking for this conversation, and an honest signal whether bilateral counterparty work makes sense at licence-grant. Imani handles ISDA/CSA legal-side; Mira handles AML/KYC; I handle follow-up; Saskia owns the relationship.

**Close (30 seconds).** Concrete follow-up commitment. ## §5 — Reciprocal-relationship framing

The frame Saskia uses across every conversation: this is not a sales pitch. It is an indication-of-interest exploration. The bank is *building* a franchise; counterparties are evaluating whether to *be part of* that franchise. Both sides benefit from a healthy bilateral; both sides are choosing each other.

This frame matters because hard-sell pitching pre-licence is dead-on-arrival. No counterparty's treasury committee will pre-commit; no counterparty's compliance function will let pre-commitment land in writing; no Saskia conversation should put either of them in that awkward position. The productive frame is the opposite: "we are inviting your evaluation, and we are evaluating you in return".

The frame also matters because institutional GM banks succeed when the counterparty roster reflects mutual choice. A roster built by hard-pitching converts a small number of counterparties initially and bleeds counterparty trust over the medium term. A roster built by reciprocal-evaluation converts more slowly initially and compounds counterparty trust as the relationship matures. The latter is what we are designing for.

### Five tonal principles to repeat across all conversations

1. **Honesty about pre-licence-day status.** Saskia never softens the licence-pending-status language. "We are pre-licence; we are not selling; we are introducing." Counterparties who sense softening on this point disengage; counterparties who hear directness lean in.

2. **Reciprocal not transactional.** The frame is "we are exploring whether mutual flow makes sense" — not "we are exploring whether we can sell you something". Every claim about us is paired with a question about them.

3. **Long-cycle not deadline-driven.** Saskia explicitly rejects the urgency frame. "We are talking to you now because twelve months of runway is the right cadence, not because we have a quarter-end target." This signals a counterparty-trust-first culture and removes pressure that would otherwise produce false signals.

4. **Specific not generic.** Every conversation cites the specific reason this counterparty is on our list — drawn from the §3 entry's "Why this counterparty for first-tier" line. Generic "we'd like to introduce ourselves" is a wasted opening; specific "your IRD-overlay programme is the cleanest single-insurer fit on our list" earns engagement.

5. **Asking not pitching.** The conversational ratio Saskia targets is at least 60% counterparty talking. The diagnostic questions in §3 and §4 are designed to surface signal; the temptation to fill silence with product-detail is the easiest way to lose a first-tier conversation. Saskia listens; we follow up in writing.

These five principles are not a script. They are the cultural shape every conversation should fit, regardless of who Saskia is in front of.

## §8 — Build-phase guardrails

What Saskia / Niko / anyone in conversation MUST NOT say or imply during pre-licence-day soft-franchise touch. Treat this as a hard list — not a guideline. Conduct-discipline pre-licence is conduct-discipline that signals what we'll be like post-licence, and counterparties read it that way.

**Forbidden:**

- No commercial pricing or indicative quotes. (No "we'd be at X over benchmark"; no "our IRS curve is currently Y bps inside Big-Four")
- No regulatory commitments or timeline guarantees. ("We'll be live by Q3" is an MTN-grade unforced error.)
- No product demonstrations of live trading systems. (Architecture conversation is fine; "let me show you our pricing screen" is not.)
- No customer-onboarding paperwork — no KYC questionnaires, no client-classification forms, no FAIS suitability questionnaires, no consent forms. None of these activate before licence-day.
- No asks for signed commitments, LOIs, or memoranda of understanding. Soft-franchise is structured-artefact-only; signed bilateral artefacts of any shape are off-limits during build.
- No exclusivity asks. Counterparties asked for exclusivity disengage instantly; we don't ask.
- No competitive-comparison claims. ("We'll be tighter than Standard Bank on IRS" is a deal-killer; if Saskia is tempted to make that claim, the conversation is in the wrong frame.)
- No claims about specific future capital, balance-sheet, or risk-appetite numbers beyond what's in the one-pager (§2).

**Permitted:**
