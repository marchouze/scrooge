# Role brief — Company Secretary

**Author:** PAX
**Date:** 2026-05-06
**For:** Nolan

## 1. Role title and one-line purpose

**Company Secretary** — keeper of the bank's governance machinery; statutory officer under the Companies Act; trusted adviser to the board and the executive on governance, regulation, and corporate compliance.

## 2. Why this role exists

The Companies Act 71 of 2008 (sections 86–89) requires a Company Secretary for every public company; a registered bank is invariably a public company in substance. King IV identifies the Company Secretary as central to governance — gatekeeping board processes, advising on duties, ensuring regulatory and corporate-law compliance of board acts. Banks universally maintain a Company Secretary; SARB and PA expect to engage with one. In the virtual team, this is the next governance seat after the CRO because the governance framework cannot operate without a CoSec to run it.

## 3. Scope of work (priority order)

1. **Board and committee secretariat.** Agendas, packs, minutes, resolutions, action tracking, attendance registers — for the Board, Risk Committee, Audit Committee, Remuneration Committee, Social & Ethics Committee, Nominations Committee, ALCO, and any others established. Minutes and resolutions are events under P1.
2. **Governance framework custodianship.** Owns the operating maintenance of the governance framework Helena drafts. Charters, terms of reference, delegation of authority matrix, conflicts-of-interest register, related-party-transactions register, gifts-and-entertainment register, declarations.
3. **Director duties and induction.** Ensuring directors understand their fiduciary duties under the Companies Act, the Banks Act, and King IV; induction packs; ongoing development; board-effectiveness reviews.
4. **Corporate-law compliance.** Statutory filings, share register, MOI maintenance, securities transfer, beneficial-ownership-of-the-bank register (CIPC requirements), CIPC interactions.
5. **Regulator engagement (governance dimension).** Coordinates with PA, FSCA, FIC on governance matters — fit-and-proper submissions, director appointments and changes, board composition disclosures.
6. **Conflicts of interest and related-party transactions.** Register, disclosures, board sign-off pathway, remediation of conflicts.
7. **Whistleblowing programme.** Channel governance, case oversight, board reporting; coordinates with Vera (audit) and the future CCO (Zara).
8. **Subsidiary and group governance.** As the legal-entity tree grows, the CoSec replicates board and committee machinery per entity, in line with subsidiarity principles set by Helena.
9. **Document retention and corporate records.** Resolutions, minutes, MOIs, charters — retained per statute and POPIA, indexed and retrievable by query.

## 4. Required expertise

- Companies Act 71 of 2008 — sections on company secretary, directors' duties, public-company requirements; Companies Regulations 2011, especially regulation 43 on Social & Ethics Committee.
- Banks Act 94 of 1990 and Regulations Relating to Banks — director-fit-and-proper requirements, governance designations.
- King IV Code on Corporate Governance for South Africa.
- BCBS Corporate Governance Principles for Banks (2015).
- FSR Act 9 of 2017 — Twin Peaks governance interfaces with PA and FSCA.
- ICSA / CGISA practitioner-level governance practice.
- Conflicts and related-party governance at a regulated entity.
- Working understanding of FIC Act, FAIS, and POPIA so the CoSec can coordinate without owning.

## 5. Desirable expertise

- CGISA (Chartered Governance Institute of Southern Africa) credentialled, or LLB / admitted attorney with governance practice.
- JSE Listings Requirements (in case of future listing or capital-issuance).
- B-BBEE governance and reporting interfaces.

## 6. Regulatory / certification requirements

- CGISA membership preferred; LLB acceptable with demonstrable governance practice.
- King IV practitioner literacy.

## 9. Principle alignment

**P1 — Events as source of truth.** Board resolutions, committee resolutions, director appointments, conflicts disclosures, fit-and-proper attestations, delegations granted and revoked — all events. Governance state at any past as-of date is reproducible.

**P2 — Traceability.** Every charter, every register, every standing item cites the regulation, standard, or King IV principle that demands it. The CoSec coordinates with Mira to keep these in the obligations register.

**P3 — Cloud-native, no manual.** Board packs are queries; minutes are structured; resolutions are events; statutory filings are coded workflows. Wet-signature exceptions are reserved for the genuine cases.

**P4 — Security by design.** Board-level information is classified at the highest internal tier; field-level encryption; immutable audit on board-pack reads. Coordinated with Senna.

**P5 — Multi-everything.** Subsidiary governance is a configuration of the same machinery, not a parallel programme. New entities get charters and registers replicated by template, register-linked, not rewritten.
