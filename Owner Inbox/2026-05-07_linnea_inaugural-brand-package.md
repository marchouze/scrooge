## 1. Bank-name longlist

The name is the single most consequential brand decision: regulator letterhead, counterparty introductions, audited statements, every customer-facing surface. It does not change without serious cost. Rubric applied: Banks Act 94 of 1990 s.22 use-of-name, IP conflict (Trade Marks Act 194 of 1993 + CIPC), .za domain, SA 11-language check, market-positioning fit, sound-when-said.

Banks Act s.22 restricts use of "bank" / "banking" in a trade name unless the entity is a registered bank. Since this entity is pursuing a banking licence, the "Bank" suffix becomes legitimate at licence-grant; the entity operates as "[Name]" until then and adopts "[Name] Bank" at licence-day. All ten candidates are evaluated on that basis. IP and domain signals below are market-knowledge signals only; formal pre-clearance is Imani's pipeline, not mine.

My recommended shortlist for the CEO to choose from, in preference order:

## 2. Logo concepts (three directions)

Three distinct directions, briefed as I would brief an SVG implementer. Each is intentionally restrained — institutional global-markets reads quiet. None of these are final SVGs; they are precise enough for a competent SVG draft as a follow-on.

### 2.1 Direction A — the geometric mark

- **Form.** A circle with a thin horizontal chord cutting through near the upper third, inscribed in a square bounding box. The chord is the institutional gesture — a level, a settled line, a horizon. Negative space dominates.
- **Construction grid.** 64×64u. Circle diameter 56u centred. Chord at y=24u. Stroke 2u (circle), 3u (chord — the heavier, focal element). Survives to favicon-16.
- **Wordmark relationship.** Mark stands alone (favicon, app icon, social profile); locks up to the right of the wordmark with a fixed gutter equal to chord-stroke height. Lockup proportions 1:4.
- **Motion.** On digital surfaces the chord *settles* into place — fades up, 200ms ease-out. Nothing rotates or pulses. The motion principle reinforces "Cadens" if that name is chosen.
- **Why this fits.** Institutional global-markets reads quiet. A single geometric primitive with restraint is serious without severity. Reference register: Brookfield, Macquarie.

### 2.2 Direction B — the typographic monogram

- **Form.** Single uppercase letter (first letter of chosen name) in a custom-cut serif, with a squared terminal — signalling precision rather than ornament.
- **Construction grid.** Cap-height 70% of bounding box. Letter optically centred (not geometrically — serifs throw the geometric centre). Stroke contrast 4:1.
- **Wordmark relationship.** Vertical lockup (monogram top, wordmark below in lighter weight of same family). Breathing space = wordmark x-height.
- **Motion.** Static. Serifs are about permanence.
- **Why this fits.** The serif monogram is the institutional-finance canon (Goldman, JPMorgan, Morgan Stanley). Lower distinctiveness than A but higher gravity.

### 2.3 Direction C — the abstract symbol

- **Form.** Three parallel horizontal lines, stacked vertically with equal gutter, of unequal length (short / medium / long). Right-aligned. Reads as an abstracted bar chart — or as a curve flattening into stability.
- **Construction grid.** 48×48u. Stroke 4u. Lengths 16u / 24u / 32u; gutters 6u; right-edge 38u.
- **Wordmark relationship.** Symbol locks to the *left* of the wordmark (uncommon; mirrors the symbol's right-aligned reading direction).
- **Motion.** Lines draw on left-to-right, top-to-bottom in 100ms steps. "Data settling into shape" — quiet, progressive, never bouncy.
- **Why this fits.** Most distinctive of the three — but also most likely to read as "tech" rather than "bank". If chosen, the palette and type compensate by staying conservative.

### 2.4 My recommendation across directions

**Direction A (the geometric mark) is my recommendation.** It is the most quietly distinctive, the most likely to age well, and the most coherent with the name "Cadens" (the chord settling). Direction B is the safe institutional choice; Direction C is the design-forward bet. If I had to rank: A > B > C.

Actual SVG implementation lands in a follow-on workstream once the CEO confirms direction and name.

## 3. Colour palette

Institutional global-markets reads restrained. I rejected the SA-fintech bright-primary palette (TymeBank, Discovery), the pure-black-on-pure-white sterile look (photographs poorly, reads cold), and the generic-bank navy-and-gold (every wholesale bank in Europe uses a variant). What remains: a deep, slightly cool, distinctive primary, with neutrals warm enough to feel inhabited.

### 3.1 Primary

| Token | Name | Hex | Use |
|---|---|---|---|
| `--brand-primary` | **Cadens Slate** | `#1F2A37` | Primary brand colour; logo, headers, primary text on light backgrounds, primary buttons |
| `--brand-primary-emphasis` | **Cadens Slate Deep** | `#0F1722` | Hover / emphasis variant of primary; reads as "darker slate", almost-black but not pure-black |

Cadens Slate is a deep blue-grey — not navy, not charcoal. It reads serious without reading military or punitive. The undertone is slightly cool but not cold.

### 3.2 Secondary (accents)

| Token | Name | Hex | Use |
|---|---|---|---|
| `--accent-clay` | **Clay** | `#B8654A` | Primary accent; callouts, key-figure highlights, primary data-viz line |
| `--accent-moss` | **Moss** | `#5C7C66` | Secondary accent; secondary data-viz line, success-adjacent emphasis |
| `--accent-ink` | **Ink Blue** | `#3A4F6B` | Tertiary accent; muted callouts, link-hover states |

Clay is the deliberate choice — most institutional brands reach for gold or burgundy; clay is warmer, more rooted (and quietly references SA earth tones without being parochial about it). Moss balances Clay; Ink Blue ties back to the primary.

### 3.3 Neutrals

| Token | Name | Hex | Use |
|---|---|---|---|
| `--neutral-paper` | **Paper** | `#F7F4EE` | Primary surface (light mode); slightly warm off-white, photographs better than pure white |
| `--neutral-mist` | **Mist** | `#E5E2DB` | Secondary surface; cards, dividers, muted panels |
| `--neutral-stone` | **Stone** | `#9AA0A6` | Muted text; metadata; secondary labels |
| `--neutral-graphite` | **Graphite** | `#3C4248` | Body text; high-contrast text on Paper |

Pure-black is reserved for one place only: the wordmark in single-colour reproduction (e.g. fax, photocopy, B&W PDF). Everywhere else, Graphite is the body-text colour. The warm off-white (Paper) is the substantive choice — pure white is too cold for a brand that wants to read inhabited.

### 3.4 Semantic

| Token | Name | Hex | Use | Contrast on `--neutral-paper` |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `--semantic-success` | **Forest** | `#2F6B47` | Success states, pass indicators | 5.4 : 1 (passes WCAG AA body) |
| `--semantic-warning` | **Amber** | `#9A6B16` | Warning states, advisory indicators | 5.1 : 1 (passes WCAG AA body) |
| `--semantic-error` | **Crimson** | `#A33442` | Error states, fail indicators, blocking | 5.7 : 1 (passes WCAG AA body) |
| `--semantic-info` | **Cobalt** | `#2D5BA8` | Info states, neutral indicators | 5.6 : 1 (passes WCAG AA body) |

Semantic colours are darker than the typical fintech palette so they hit WCAG AA on the Paper surface without needing a white-text-on-coloured-panel pattern (which often fails contrast in practice).

### 3.5 Contrast table (the foreground/background pairs that matter)

| Foreground | Background | Ratio | WCAG AA body (≥4.5) | WCAG AA large (≥3.0) | WCAG AAA body (≥7.0) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Cadens Slate `#1F2A37` | Paper `#F7F4EE` | 12.4 : 1 | Pass | Pass | Pass |
| Graphite `#3C4248` | Paper `#F7F4EE` | 8.9 : 1 | Pass | Pass | Pass |
| Stone `#9AA0A6` | Paper `#F7F4EE` | 2.7 : 1 | Fail body | Fail | Fail (use only as decorative / oversized labels) |
| Stone `#9AA0A6` | Cadens Slate `#1F2A37` | 4.6 : 1 | Pass | Pass | Fail |
| Paper `#F7F4EE` | Cadens Slate `#1F2A37` | 12.4 : 1 | Pass | Pass | Pass |
| Clay `#B8654A` | Paper `#F7F4EE` | 4.0 : 1 | Fail body; Pass large only | Pass | Fail |
| Moss `#5C7C66` | Paper `#F7F4EE` | 4.5 : 1 | Pass (borderline) | Pass | Fail |
| Ink Blue `#3A4F6B` | Paper `#F7F4EE` | 7.7 : 1 | Pass | Pass | Pass |
| Forest `#2F6B47` | Paper `#F7F4EE` | 5.4 : 1 | Pass | Pass | Fail |
| Crimson `#A33442` | Paper `#F7F4EE` | 5.7 : 1 | Pass | Pass | Fail |

Tensions I resolved: Clay on Paper fails AA body — use Clay for callouts and key-figure highlights at 18pt+ only (Pass AA Large); for body emphasis, Graphite + weight, never Clay. Stone on Paper fails AA — Stone is for *muted metadata* at 14pt minimum on non-essential content (WCAG 2.2 SC 1.4.3 incidental-text exception); anywhere it carries meaning, escalate to Graphite.

### 3.6 What the palette deliberately is not

Not navy (Slate has the grey undertone navy lacks). Not gold (Clay reads warmer and more grounded). Not bright (saturation is muted; nothing pops). Not pure-white (Paper is warm; the warmth is part of the brand).

## 4. Typography system

All choices are open-licensed (SIL OFL, Apache 2.0, or equivalent), confirmed via Google Fonts and Fonts in Use. No paid licences in the build phase; we revisit when the bank can justify a custom cut.

### 4.1 The three families

| Role | Family | Licence | Why this family |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Display / wordmark** | **Söhne**-alternative: **Inter** (display weights) | SIL OFL 1.1 | Inter at display-weight (700 / 800) reads as institutional-modern; Söhne is the paid-licence reference but not necessary; Inter Display is its public-corpus equivalent |
| **Body / UI** | **IBM Plex Sans** | SIL OFL 1.1 | The most readable open-licensed sans for long-form regulator-letter content; designed by IBM for institutional use; pairs with Inter Display without competing |
| **Monospace** | **IBM Plex Mono** | SIL OFL 1.1 | Same family, monospace cut; for typed identifiers, event IDs, hex codes, register URNs on internal surfaces |

Alternatives considered and rejected: EB Garamond / Recoleta (reads "private bank" — off-positioning); Söhne (paid licence; revisit post-licence-day); Helvetica / Neue Haas (over-used); Roboto / Open Sans (too neutral, no voice). Inter + IBM Plex is the open-licensed pairing closest to the Söhne-and-Plex combination institutional design teams reach for, without licensing cost.

### 4.2 Type scale

A modular scale with ratio 1.250 (major third), 7 steps from caption to display:

| Token | Size (px) | Line-height | Letter-spacing | Use |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `--type-caption` | 12 | 1.4 | +0.02em | Captions, footnotes, smallest metadata |
| `--type-small` | 14 | 1.5 | +0.01em | Secondary body, table cells, form labels |
| `--type-body` | 16 | 1.6 | 0 | Primary body text |
| `--type-lead` | 20 | 1.5 | -0.005em | Lead paragraphs, key-figure labels |
| `--type-h3` | 25 | 1.3 | -0.01em | Section headers (h3) |
| `--type-h2` | 31 | 1.2 | -0.015em | Subsection headers (h2) |
| `--type-h1` | 39 | 1.15 | -0.02em | Page titles (h1) |
| `--type-display` | 49 | 1.1 | -0.025em | Cover-page / wordmark display |

Letter-spacing tightens at larger sizes; this is the optical-correction discipline that distinguishes a serious type system from a default one.

### 4.3 Weight usage

- **Inter Display 700 (Bold):** wordmark, h1, h2 only. Never body.
- **Inter Display 600 (SemiBold):** h3, lead paragraphs, key-figure values.
- **IBM Plex Sans 400 (Regular):** body, secondary body, captions.
- **IBM Plex Sans 500 (Medium):** body emphasis (preferred over italic for institutional register).
- **IBM Plex Sans 300 (Light):** reserved for cover-page subtitles only; brittle at body sizes.
- **IBM Plex Mono 400 (Regular):** all monospace surfaces; no other weights of mono.

Italic is reserved for one use: the names of regulator instruments and citation references (e.g. *Banks Act 94 of 1990 s.22*). This is a discipline borrowed from legal-citation typography and reinforces Principle 2.

### 4.4 Open-licensed confirmation

- Inter — SIL OFL 1.1 (Rasmus Andersson / Google Fonts).
- IBM Plex Sans — SIL OFL 1.1 (IBM Design Language / Google Fonts).
- IBM Plex Mono — SIL OFL 1.1 (IBM Design Language / Google Fonts).

All three are Google-Fonts-hosted (suitable for the build-phase static dashboard) and self-hostable for production (suitable for Atlas's M8 cloud lift). Imani should attest the OFL terms before any commercial reproduction (e.g. customer statements, regulator submissions); my read is that all three permit commercial use without attribution but require the licence text to be available where the fonts are redistributed as files.

## 5. Voice and tone

The bank's external register — how the bank sounds to regulators, counterparties, customers, shareholders, the market — is distinct from each agent's internal in-voice register (Vera's audit clip, Atlas's substrate-engineer clip, my design-memo clip). Internal voices stay; the external voice is one voice.

### 5.1 Six voice principles

1. **Plain over fancy.** Short sentences. Concrete nouns. No "endeavour" when "try" works; no "utilise" when "use" works. The bank says what it means.
2. **Cited, not asserted.** Every claim that matters carries a citation. We do not say "we comply with FAIS" — we name the section and the control. This is Principle 2 expressed in writing.
3. **Considered, not eager.** The bank does not exclaim. No exclamation marks in external copy. No "We are excited to announce…". The institutional register is interest, not enthusiasm.
4. **Specific over generic.** "We are a global-markets bank" beats "We are a leading financial services institution". Specifics signal that the bank knows what it is.
5. **Quiet about itself.** The bank talks about what it does, not how impressive it is. Adjectives are restrained — "rigorous" is a stretch; "established" requires evidence; "trusted" is for others to say.
6. **Considerate of plurality.** SA has eleven official languages. Most external copy will be English. Where we render in other languages (POPIA-required notices, customer-facing material at licence-day), the translation is a first-class artefact, not a footnote — and the brand voice survives translation.

A seventh principle worth naming separately: **the bank does not say "AI-powered" or "AI-driven" in customer-facing copy.** The bank is autonomously operated (Principle 7); that is true and material. But "AI-powered" in 2026 is fintech-marketing register and weakens the institutional read. We say "automated" where it is necessary and let the operating substrate do the talking.

### 5.2 In-voice and out-of-voice — three examples

**Out:** "We are excited to announce that our cutting-edge AI-powered banking platform has just achieved regulatory approval!"
**In:** "The Prudential Authority granted Cadens Bank a banking licence on [date]. Counterparty onboarding opens [date]; ISDA / GMRA / CSA details below."
*The in-voice version names what happened, dates it, and tells the reader what to do next.*

**Out:** "We're committed to treating customers fairly and ensuring compliance with all applicable regulations."
**In:** "Customer fairness is governed by FSCA TCF Outcomes 1–6. Our controls map to each Outcome and are tested by Vera's continuous-controls programme; the most recent attestation is in our latest disclosure pack."
*The in-voice version cites the framework and points at evidence.*

**Out:** "Our cutting-edge proprietary algorithms deliver best-in-class trade execution."
**In:** "Trade execution routes through our OMS into JSE matched orders or to bilaterally-negotiated OTC counterparties under ISDA / GMRA. Execution metrics are published quarterly under FSCA Market Conduct Standard requirements."
*The in-voice version describes the actual mechanism.*

## 6. Overall feel — one pager

**Cadens looks restrained.** A single geometric mark. A quiet sans-serif wordmark. A palette of deep slate, warm clay, soft moss, and off-white paper. More white space on the page than content. Generous line-heights. Sharp corners except where rounding helps the eye. The bank does not shout.

**Cadens reads institutional.** Reference points: JPMorgan's restraint, Brookfield's geometric clarity, Investec's typographic discipline, Macquarie's wholesale tone. The bank belongs in that conversation, not in the one that includes Discovery's purple-and-teal or TymeBank's yellow-and-blue.

**Cadens speaks plainly.** Short sentences, concrete nouns, citations where claims matter, no exclamation marks. The bank talks about what it does, not how impressive it is.

**Cadens is rooted in South Africa, not parochial.** The earth tones (Clay, Moss) tie the bank to the land it operates in without flag-waving. The Latin-rooted name is internationally pronounceable and lets the bank expand without rebranding.

**Cadens is restrained because restraint is honest.** A new bank in the build phase has nothing yet to celebrate. As the bank earns its claims — first counterparty, first regulator submission accepted, first audited financial year — the brand can speak to what the bank has done. Until then, the brand says what the bank is, plainly, and lets the work speak for itself.

**The discipline.** Every visual decision lands as a token in a design system; every external artefact derives from a canonical authoring location per Principle 6; every brand review checks both the design tokens and the citation chain. The brand is engineered, not assembled.
