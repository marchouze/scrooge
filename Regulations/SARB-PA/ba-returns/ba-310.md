## Purpose of BA 310

Form BA 310 is the prudential return through which the bank reports its **standardised-approach market-risk capital charge** to the Prudential Authority. Market risk is a **Pillar-1** risk: the bank must hold capital against the risk of loss in on- and off-balance-sheet positions arising from movements in market prices (interest rates, equity prices, foreign-exchange rates, commodity prices). BA 310 carries the **standardised measurement method (SMM)** — the rules-based, building-block approach in which capital is computed per risk class from prescribed risk weights and netting rules, then summed.

This is the **non-FRTB standardised** return. The bank's **live market-risk-capital** return is the recalibrated FRTB standardised approach on **BA 325** ([`ba-325.md`](ba-325.md)); BA 310 carries the **legacy / Basel-2.5 standardised position-risk** treatment that Reg 28 prescribes directly. As FRTB beds in (PC 18/2024 roadmap; capitalisation from 1 July 2025), the PA's market-risk return architecture spans all three forms — BA 310 (standardised), BA 320 (alternative), BA 325 (FRTB) — and the bank tracks which return(s) bind it on the prescribed cadence. The granular question of **which of BA 310 / 320 / 325 a given bank submits, and whether they co-exist or one supersedes the others**, is counsel-gated (see Open items).

The standardised position-risk capital identity (Reg 28(3); BCBS MAR `urn:reg:bcbs:mar:20.1`):

```
Standardised market-risk capital
  = interest-rate position risk (general + specific)
  + equity position risk (general + specific)
  + foreign-exchange (net-open-position) risk
  + commodity position risk
marketRiskRwa = 12.5 × marketRiskCapital
```

The total capital is converted to a market-RWA contribution (× 12.5, the reciprocal of the 8% minimum) and integrated into the BA 700 / BA 100 capital-adequacy aggregate (Reg 38, integrating Reg 28/29).

## The standardised approach for position risk

The SA decomposes the trading-book (and, for FX and commodities, the banking book — Reg 28(3)(c)–(d)) into risk classes, each with its own prescribed building-block charge.

### 1. Interest-rate position risk (general + specific)

For interest-rate-related instruments held in the trading book (Reg 28(3)(a)):

- **General risk** — captures the risk of a parallel / non-parallel move in the yield curve. Computed by the **maturity-method ladder**: positions are slotted into prescribed time bands, weighted by band, and netted with vertical / horizontal disallowances for offsetting within and across bands. The in-repo generator carries a **13 time-band** maturity ladder (`IrMaturityBandRow`; `weightedLongMinor` / `weightedShortMinor` per band; the exact SARB band boundaries are pinned by Mira against the SARB schedule — `[citation: TBC]` for the precise band-edge table).
- **Specific risk** — captures issuer-specific (credit-spread / default) risk on debt instruments, computed as `Σ (gross position × specific-risk weight)` per issuer / rating-grade slice (`IrSpecificRiskRow`; weights e.g. 0.0 sovereign AAA, 0.0025 qualifying, 0.08 high-yield).

### 2. Equity position risk (general + specific)

For equity instruments held in the trading book (Reg 28(3)(b)): the simplified standardised treatment is **8% general (net) + 8% specific (gross)** per market (`EquityRow`; `8% × |netPosition| + 8% × grossPosition`). The bank holds JSE-listed equities only for client facilitation; equity position risk enters as the book grows.

### 3. Foreign-exchange (net-open-position) risk — the live charge

For any foreign-exchange instrument held in the **banking book or trading book** — *i.e. any foreign-currency position held by the bank* (Reg 28(3)(c)) — the SA applies a **net-open-position (NOP)** charge. The standardised shorthand the in-repo generator implements is **8% × max(Σ longs, Σ shorts)** (the BCBS "shorthand method" for FX: capital = 8% of the greater of the aggregate net long or aggregate net short positions across currencies; BCBS D352 §718(xiii); Reg 28(5) FX open-position charge). This is the **live binding market-risk charge** for the bank in the build phase — the trading book runs spot and forward FX as its primary franchise.

> **FX NOP routing across the market-risk returns (per the prior reconciliation).** Reg 28(3)(c) draws the FX NOP into market-risk scope **regardless of book** (banking or trading). The in-repo **BA 310 FX adapter** (`ba-310-fx-adapter.ts`) feeds the FX net-open positions — derived from the live FX-position calculator / `FxTradeExecuted` events — into the **BA 310 FX section** (`Ba310FxSection`), excluding the ZAR functional-currency leg per Reg 28(5). Under the **FRTB** recalibration on BA 325, the *same* FX NOP feeds the **FX risk class** (SBM delta; vega + curvature once FX options trade) plus the **GIRR** discount legs. So the FX NOP is a single economic exposure that is **represented on the standardised BA 310 (8%-NOP shorthand)** and, under FRTB, **re-expressed on BA 325 (SBM/DRC/RRAO)** — *the bank does not double-count it; it is reported on the return architecture that binds it for the period.* The precise allocation rule — whether a given bank-period reports FX on BA 310, BA 320, or BA 325, and how the PA treats the transition — is counsel-gated (see Open items); the source supports only that FX-in-either-book is in market-risk scope (Reg 28(3)(c)) and that the in-repo FX adapter targets the BA 310 standardised FX section.

### 4. Commodity position risk

For any commodity position held in the banking book or trading book (Reg 28(3)(d)): the simplified-method charge (`CommodityPositionRow`; the in-repo generator applies a simplified `15% net + 3% gross`). Out of the bank's primary franchise scope; residual cross-currency only.

## The trading-book / banking-book boundary

The boundary determines which positions attract market-risk capital (trading book) versus credit / IRRBB capital (banking book). Reg 28 carries the boundary rules; critically, Reg 28(3)(c)–(d) draw **FX and commodity positions** into market-risk scope **whether held in the banking book or the trading book**. The presumptive-classification rules and the re-designation gate are operationalised at the New Product Approval gate and in Market Risk Policy.

The *capital* charge under the standardised approach is the 8%-NOP figure (BA 310); under FRTB it is the SBM/DRC/RRAO figure (BA 325).

## Material foreign-currency reporting

Reg 28(3)(c) brings every foreign-currency position into market-risk scope. Because the bank reports in ZAR but trades multi-currency positions, the BA 310 FX adapter excludes the ZAR functional leg and computes the NOP charge on the non-functional-currency positions revalued into ZAR minor units (`netPositionFunctionalMinor`). This is the Principle-5 (multi-currency) hook on the standardised market-risk return — ZAR is the presentation currency; the FX NOP is the live risk.

## Binding obligations on the bank (summary)

1. **Measure trading-book (and in-scope banking-book FX/commodity) market risk per the standardised approach** and hold capital under Reg 28 (`ORG-PR-RETURNS-011`; Reg 28; BCBS MAR 20.1).
2. **Compute the standardised position-risk charge** = IR general + IR specific + equity + FX NOP + commodity, per the prescribed building-block rules (Reg 28(3); BCBS MAR 20.1).
3. **Capitalise FX positions in either book** — the FX NOP attracts market-risk capital whether held in the banking book or the trading book (Reg 28(3)(c)), via the 8% × max(longs, shorts) standardised shorthand (Reg 28(5)).
4. **Integrate the market-risk charge into the capital-adequacy aggregate** — feed the standardised capital (× 12.5 → market RWA) into BA 700 / BA 100 (Reg 38, integrating Reg 28/29).
5. **Submit form BA 310** to the PA per Annexures 11A/11B of D5/2025 and the Regulations, on the prescribed (monthly) cadence (D5/2025 §2.1.12; exact cadence + Annexure 11B line schema counsel-gated).
6. **Operate the market-risk appetite limits above the regulatory floor** — MR-1-FX (1-day 99% VaR) and the per-currency NOP sub-limits (internal RAS; the appetite layer, not this catalogue).
