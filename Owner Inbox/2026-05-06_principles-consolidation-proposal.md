## 1. The observation

P2, P6 and P7 are three rules about the same phenomenon — **traceability**. They were written separately because each addresses a different operational consequence, but conceptually they are one principle:

| Today | What it really says |
|---|---|
| **P2** — Every action traces to a source | *Atomic rule.* At every code path / process step, a citation must exist. |
| **P6** — Single source of truth; presentations derive from data | *Structural rule, downward.* External artefacts (board packs, BA returns, AFS, customer statements) are generated by walking citations *down* through Policy → Standard → Process → Data. |
| **P7** — Implementation traceability | *Structural rule, upward.* System capabilities are justified by walking citations *up* through Procedure → Policy → Regulation. Plus: no orphans, every procedure mandate-owned. |

P7's own text already says "this principle is the operational extension of Principle 2 (every action traces to a source) into the implementation layers below policy and into the staffing layer above it." P6 is implicitly the same extension in the opposite direction.

Three principles, one substance: **the bank's artefacts sit in a single citable bidirectional graph; no orphan artefacts exist; presentations are generated, not authored.**

## 2. Proposed consolidation

Replace P2, P6, P7 with a single Principle 2 — **Traceability and the single-graph discipline** — with four named sub-rules.

> **Principle 2 — Traceability and the single-graph discipline.**
>
> Every artefact in the bank — events, controls, procedures, system capabilities, policies, standards, regulator instruments, presentations — sits in a single citable graph anchored in the obligations register, the policy register, the procedures index, the semantic layer, the persona / mandate library, and the legal-entity tree. No artefact exists outside the graph; the graph is testable from any node in either direction.
>
> **2.1 Atomicity (was P2).** No control, procedure, posting rule, screening rule, report line, contract clause, payroll deduction, tax computation, presentation paragraph, or system capability may exist without a structured citation to the regulation, standard, contract, internal policy, or upstream artefact that justifies it. The obligations register holds typed, versioned references. Code or process without a citation is by definition unjustified — it is either sourced or removed.
>
> **2.2 Upward chain — implementation traceability (was P7).** The chain `Regulation → Policy → Procedure → System Capability` is testable bidirectionally. Every regulation maps to the system capabilities that fulfil it; every system capability maps to the regulations it serves. **No orphan capabilities.** Every system capability has a procedure that names it. **No orphan procedures.** Every procedure has a mandate-owner whose persona file in `/Team/` (or whose governance seat) covers its substance — checked bidirectionally; orphans are reportable findings to Vera.
>
> **2.3 Downward chain — presentations derive from data (was P6).** External artefacts (regulator returns, board and committee packs, AFS, customer statements, marketing claims, public disclosures) are summarised derivations of the internal layers `Data → Process → Standard → Policy → Presentation`. Presentations are **generated, not authored**. Authoring at the presentation layer is reserved for narrative explanation — never for new substance. New substance enters at data (an event) or at policy (a governance-approved change) and propagates upward. Where the data is not yet in the system, the data is added first; the presentation derives from it.
>
> **2.4 Operational substrate.** The obligations register (Mira), the semantic layer (Anya), the policy register (Owen), the procedures index (Owen + domain leads), the persona / mandate library (Scrooge), the legal-entity tree (Imani), and the citation-graph tooling (platform) are the operational infrastructure that makes 2.1–2.3 enforceable. They are not optional; they are how this principle is enforced.

## 5. Tradeoffs

**For consolidation.**
- Conceptually honest. Three rules describing one graph become one rule with three sub-rules.
- Easier to teach to new hires ("the bank traces") and easier to defend to a regulator.
- Reduces the cognitive load of "which P am I citing here, P2 or P7?" — the answer becomes "Principle 2".
- The substrate (obligations register, semantic layer, policy register, procedures index, persona library) is *already* one operational system; the principle catches up to that.

**Against consolidation.**
- **Loud signalling loss.** Three named principles each catch a different operational habit:
  - P2 stops you writing untraceable code at the keyboard.
  - P6 stops you authoring substance at the presentation layer when in a board pack.
  - P7 stops you building orphan capabilities or carrying orphan procedures.
  Folding them into one risks one face being forgotten in practice — especially P6 (presentation discipline), which is the easiest to violate under deadline. The sub-rule structure mitigates but does not eliminate this.
- **Renumbering churn.** ~30 files touched mechanically; risk of stale references for a period. Vera's continuous-controls assurance is the right backstop, but there will be a clean-up window.
- **Backwards-compatibility cost in already-actioned decisions.** The CEO decision pack and policy-bundle approvals reference P-numbers (e.g. "P7 alignment" appears in role briefs and policy texts). These are historical records; we either leave them as-is (with a glossary note that "P7 = old name for P2.2") or rewrite — I would leave historical decision records as-is and add a glossary entry.

## 6. Alternative — "two principles" instead of one

If the loss of the P6 signal is the dominant concern, an alternative is **two consolidated principles**:

- **P2 — Traceability (atomic citation discipline).** What is now P2, plus the operational-substrate clause from 2.4 (the obligations register, semantic layer, etc.).
- **P6 — Single-graph discipline (downward and upward derivation).** What is now P6 and P7 merged: presentations generate downward from data; capabilities justify upward through procedure → policy → regulation; no orphans; mandate ownership.

Seven → six principles. P2 stays as the atomic-discipline anchor (loud at the keyboard). P6 carries both the presentation and implementation graph rules (loud at the publication and capability layer).

This option is conceptually a hair less honest (it splits a graph into two principles by direction), but operationally noisier where it matters — and the renumbering is smaller (only P7 disappears; P3, P4, P5 are unaffected by either option).

## 7. Recommendation

**Option A — full consolidation to five principles** is conceptually the cleanest, and the sub-rule structure (2.1 / 2.2 / 2.3 / 2.4) preserves the operational distinctions in a way that a careful reader will hold.

**Option B — six principles** is the pragmatic compromise if the loss of the standalone P6 signal worries you.

If forced to pick: **A**. The bank is small; teaching the principle to new hires from a clean five is more valuable than preserving today's seven-shaped citations. The cost is a one-time mechanical refactor. I would do it now while there are still <100 cross-references, not later.
