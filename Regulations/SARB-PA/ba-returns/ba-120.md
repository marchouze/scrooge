## Purpose of BA 120

Form BA 120 is the prudential return through which the bank reports its **Net Stable Funding Ratio (NSFR)** to the Prudential Authority. The NSFR is a **Pillar-1 structural-liquidity standard** that requires the bank to fund its activities with **sufficiently stable sources of funding** on an **ongoing, one-year structural horizon** — the structural counterpart to the LCR's 30-day acute-stress measure (BA 110 / Reg 26 LCR section).

The headline ratio (BCBS NSF `urn:reg:bcbs:nsf:20.2`; Reg 26(14)(d); D1/2023 §2.1.2):

```
        available stable funding (ASF)
NSFR  =  ----------------------------------  ≥ 100%
        required stable funding (RSF)
```

D1/2023 §2.1.2 states verbatim that *"The NSFR should be calculated in accordance with Regulation 26(14)(d) of the Regulations relating to Banks by dividing the available amount of stable funding by the required amount of stable funding and multiplying it by one hundred."* The NSFR limits over-reliance on short-term wholesale funding and incentivises a stable funding structure across both on- and off-balance-sheet items.

The bank must retain an **audit trail** reconciling the return to its general ledger: Reg 26(3) requires *"an audit trail in respect of the underlying data relating to the base models supporting the relevant form … [providing] a reconciliation between the total assets and the total liabilities reported on the form … and the … general ledger systems."* (Reg 26(3)(a), verbatim — the same liquidity-return audit-trail obligation that anchors the LCR return.)

## The NSFR numerator — Available Stable Funding (ASF)

ASF is the portion of capital and liabilities expected to be **reliable over the one-year horizon**. Each funding source is multiplied by an **ASF factor** reflecting its stability (the more stable the funding, the higher the factor). D1/2023 §2.2.1–§2.2.3 set the calibration principles verbatim: ASF *"is measured based on the broad characteristics of the relative stability of an institution's funding sources, including the contractual maturity of its liabilities and the differences in the propensity of different types of funding providers to withdraw their funding"* (§2.2.1); the calibration *"presupposes that long-term funding is more stable than short-term funding"* (§2.2.2); and *"regulatory capital and funding provided by retail and small business customers are the most stable sources of funding and have the highest ASF factors assigned to them"* (§2.2.3).

| Funding source / tenor | ASF factor | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Regulatory capital (Tier 1 + Tier 2 with residual maturity ≥ 1yr); other liabilities ≥ 1yr | **100%** | BCBS NSF `nsf:30.1`; D1/2023 §2.2.3 |
| Stable retail / small-business deposits (residual maturity < 1yr) | **95%** | BCBS NSF `nsf:30.1` |
| Less-stable retail / small-business deposits (residual maturity < 1yr) | **90%** | BCBS NSF `nsf:30.1` |
| Funding < 1yr from non-financial corporates, sovereigns, PSEs; financial-corporate funding with residual maturity 6m–1yr; operational deposits | **50%** | D1/2023 §2.2.4 (verbatim) |
| Funding < 6m from financial corporates (international standard) | **0%** | BCBS NSF `nsf:30.1`; D1/2023 §2.2.5 |
| **ZAR** secured/unsecured funding < 6m from financial corporates **excluding banks** (SA national-discretion phase-out) | **30% → 20% → 10% → 0%** (date-dependent) | D1/2023 §2.2.5 (verbatim) |

**SA national-discretion phase-out (D1/2023 §2.2.5, verbatim).** The PA had since 2017 granted a 35% ASF factor to ZAR funding < 6m from financial corporates (excluding banks) — a deviation from the international 0% standard, available only to banks conducting business in South Africa. D1/2023 phases this out:

| Period | ASF factor |
|---|---|
| 1 June 2023 – 31 December 2023 | **30%** |
| 1 January 2024 – 31 December 2024 | **20%** |
| 1 January 2025 – 31 December 2027 | **10%** |
| From 1 January 2028 | **0%** |

This date-dependent factor is the substrate-load-bearing SA delta on the NSFR (carried by `ORG-PR-NSFR-004`; implemented in the engine — see system-capability chain).

## The NSFR denominator — Required Stable Funding (RSF)

RSF is the amount of stable funding the bank's **assets and off-balance-sheet exposures require**, given their liquidity-risk profile. Each asset is multiplied by an **RSF factor** (the more liquid / shorter-dated / less encumbered the asset, the lower its RSF, since liquid assets can be monetised rather than needing stable funding). D1/2023 §2.3.1–§2.3.2 set the calibration verbatim: RSF *"is measured based on the broad characteristics of the liquidity risk profile of an institution's assets and off-balance sheet (OBS) exposures"* (§2.3.1), considering *"the need for resilient credit extension, bank behaviour, liquidity value, asset tenor and asset quality"* (§2.3.2).

| Asset / exposure (tenor / encumbrance) | RSF factor | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Coins & banknotes; central-bank reserves (excess) held at SARB | **0%** | D1/2023 §2.3.3.1 (excess cash reserves) |
| Minimum required cash reserves at SARB | **5%** | D1/2023 §2.3.3.1 (verbatim) |
| Unencumbered Level-1 HQLA (other than cash) | **5%** | BCBS NSF `nsf:30.1` |
| Unencumbered Level-2A HQLA | **15%** | BCBS NSF `nsf:30.1` |
| Unencumbered Level-2B HQLA; loans 6m–1yr; securities < 1yr | **50%** | BCBS NSF `nsf:30.1` |
| Performing loans (residual maturity > 1yr; non-financial; risk-weight ≤ 35%) | **65%** | BCBS NSF `nsf:30.1` |
| Other unencumbered performing loans / corporate loans (residual maturity > 1yr) | **85%** | BCBS NSF `nsf:30.1` |
| All other assets; net derivative receivables; encumbered ≥ 1yr | **100%** | BCBS NSF `nsf:30.1` |
| **Gross derivative liabilities** (before netting/collateral) — stable-funding add-on | **10%** (of gross) | D1/2023 §2.3.4.1 (verbatim) |

**SA RSF national-discretion items (D1/2023, verbatim):**
- **Cash reserves (§2.3.3.1):** minimum required cash reserves → **5%** RSF; any **excess** reserve balances held at the SARB → **0%** RSF.
- **Gross derivative-liability add-on (§2.3.4.1):** *"10 per cent of gross derivative liabilities (before any netting or collateral has been applied) are required to have stable funding raised against them."*
- **Interdependent assets/liabilities (§2.3.5.1):** beneficial treatment per BCBS NSFR framework §45 requires PA supervisory approval **plus** approval by at minimum the bank's executive committee responsible for liquidity-risk monitoring (`ORG-PR-NSFR-008`).
- **RCLF (§2.3.6.1):** the Restricted Committed Liquidity Facility from the SARB is reported as **Level-2B HQLA** per Reg 26(12)(b)(iii)(D) (`ORG-PR-NSFR-009`).

## The one-year structural horizon

The NSFR is calibrated to an **ongoing one-year horizon** — it tests whether the bank's funding structure is structurally stable, not whether it can survive an acute 30-day stress (that is the LCR). The framework assigns ASF factors by funding tenor and provider type, and RSF factors by asset tenor, liquidity, and encumbrance, such that long-dated illiquid assets must be matched by stable long-dated funding. The horizon is the NSFR's defining feature versus the LCR's 30-calendar-day acute-stress horizon (BA 110 / Reg 26 LCR section).

## Interaction with the LCR (BA 110)

The NSFR and LCR are **complementary** Pillar-1 liquidity standards sharing the same HQLA taxonomy:

- **LCR (BA 110, Reg 26 LCR section):** a **30-day acute-stress** ratio — HQLA stock ÷ net 30-day stressed outflows ≥ 100%. Short-term survival.
- **NSFR (BA 120, Reg 26(14)):** a **one-year structural** ratio — ASF ÷ RSF ≥ 100%. Structural funding stability.

The numerator/denominator share inputs: the **same HQLA classification** (Level-1/2A/2B per the HQLA classifier) drives the LCR numerator *and* the NSFR's low RSF factors (HQLA is cheap to fund — 5%/15%/50% RSF — because it can be monetised). A bank can pass one and fail the other: an LCR-rich bank holding ample HQLA against short-dated outflows may still fail the NSFR if it funds long-dated loans with < 6m wholesale money. Both are reported monthly within the Reg 26 liquidity-return family; both share the Reg 26(3) GL-reconciling audit-trail obligation. The build-phase substrate computes both off the shared ALM position snapshot (see system-capability chain).

## Binding obligations on the bank (summary)

1. **Maintain NSFR ≥ 100%** continuously over the one-year horizon — ASF ≥ RSF (BCBS NSF 20.2; Reg 26(14)(d); D1/2023 §2.1.2; `ORG-PR-07`).
2. **Compute the NSFR as ASF ÷ RSF × 100** per Reg 26(14)(d) (`ORG-PR-NSFR-001`).
3. **Measure ASF** by funding stability — highest factors to capital + retail/small-business deposits; long-term > short-term (D1/2023 §2.2.1–§2.2.3; `ORG-PR-NSFR-002`).
4. **Apply the 50% ASF factor** to < 1yr non-financial-corporate/sovereign/PSE funding and 6m–1yr financial-corporate funding (D1/2023 §2.2.4; `ORG-PR-NSFR-003`).
5. **Apply the date-dependent ZAR financial-corporate < 6m ASF phase-out** (30%→20%→10%→0%) (D1/2023 §2.2.5; `ORG-PR-NSFR-004`).
6. **Measure RSF** by asset liquidity-risk profile; implement the BCBS RSF factor table as PA-calibrated (D1/2023 §2.3.1–§2.3.2; `ORG-PR-NSFR-005`).
7. **Apply 5% RSF to minimum required cash reserves; 0% to excess SARB reserves** (D1/2023 §2.3.3.1; `ORG-PR-NSFR-006`).
8. **Raise stable funding equal to ≥ 10% of gross derivative liabilities** (before netting/collateral) (D1/2023 §2.3.4.1; `ORG-PR-NSFR-007`).
9. **Obtain supervisory + ExCo approval** before applying the interdependent-asset beneficial treatment (D1/2023 §2.3.5.1; `ORG-PR-NSFR-008`).
10. **Report the RCLF as Level-2B HQLA** per Reg 26(12)(b)(iii)(D) (D1/2023 §2.3.6.1; `ORG-PR-NSFR-009`).
11. **Submit form BA 120** to the PA in accordance with Annexures 4A/4B of D5/2025 and the Regulations, on the prescribed (monthly) cadence (D5/2025 §2.1.5; `ORG-PR-RETURNS-004`; exact cadence counsel-gated).
12. **Retain a GL-reconciling audit trail** for the base models supporting the return, available to the PA on request (Reg 26(3)).
13. **Operate the RAS structural-funding buffer** above the PA floor (internal gold-plate; RAS B2 calibration — the RAS targets a stricter operating buffer above 100%).
