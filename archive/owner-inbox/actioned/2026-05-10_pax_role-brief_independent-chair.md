## 1. Role purpose and regulatory anchor

### 1.1 Purpose

The Independent Chair is the most load-bearing single human appointment the bank makes at licence-day. One human carries **three governance roles** that SA banking law, the Companies Act, and King IV refuse to collapse into the executive seat:

1. **Board Chair** — presides over the Board, sets the agenda, owns the Board's relationship with the CEO (Marc), and is the named external interface for SARB Prudential Authority Board-composition review.
2. **Audit Committee Chair** — under Companies Act s.94(7), reports separately to shareholders; oversees the external auditor relationship (Banks Act s.61); is the senior NED that SARB and the eventual external audit firm engage on contentious financial-reporting and going-concern matters.
3. **Social & Ethics Committee independent NED** — under Companies Regulation 43, the S&E Committee must include at least one independent NED; under the bank's "minimum possible" composition (Owen + Imani §2.2), this is the same individual.

The CEO's "Minimum possible" modification of S3 (D-THIN-HUMAN-LAYER-MINIMUM) is what collapses these three governance bindings into one human. This is **not** a cost-cut; it is the smallest human roster the law permits a SARB-licensed bank with a public-company corporate form to operate. The Chair seat is the **binding constraint** that forces total NED count to three (the Companies Act s.94 floor for an Audit Committee), even though Companies Act s.66 alone would permit two NEDs.

## 5. Independence test (load-bearing)

This is the **single most important section of the brief**. The Chair seat lives or dies on the substantive-independence test. The Companies Act s.94 formal test is the floor; King IV's substantive test is the gate; SARB's reading is the binding constraint at the licence-application file.

### 5.1 Companies Act s.94(4)–(6) — formal substantive-independence test

Under **Companies Act s.94(4)(a)** read with **s.94(4)–(6)**, an Audit Committee member must:

- **Not be involved in the day-to-day management of the company's business** during the financial year, or have been so involved at any time during the previous financial year.
- **Not be a prescribed officer, full-time employee, or other executive officer** of the company or of another related or inter-related company in the previous three financial years (the **three-year look-back**).
- **Not be a material supplier or customer** of the company, such that a reasonable and informed third party would conclude that the integrity, impartiality, or objectivity of the director would be compromised by that relationship. **De minimis** is read substantively — there is no statutory threshold; SARB and SAICA practice treats anything above ≈ 5% of either party's revenue as presumptively material, with smaller relationships challenged on context.
- **Not be related to any person** who falls within the exclusions above (related = parent, child, sibling, spouse, life partner, and (for SA family-business contexts) extended-family relationships material enough to compromise objectivity).

Under **s.94(5)**, "an audit committee may not consist of any person who, in the previous financial year, has been a regular advisor to the company". The "regular adviser" exclusion is broader than the formal-employment exclusion — it includes external counsel, external auditors (which is independently barred), consulting engagements above de minimis, and similar professional-service relationships with the company.

Under **s.94(6)**, the disqualifications above apply to the AC member "and to a related or inter-related person". The look-through goes through controlling family relationships and related-entity structures.

### 5.2 King IV's substantive-independence test (harder than s.94)

King IV Principle 7 reads independence **substantively, not formally**. A director is independent if they:

- Are **not** a representative of, or have material business / professional / family relationships with, a major shareholder of the company.
- Are **not** representing or **financially material to** any other party with a material relationship to the company (lender, supplier, customer, joint-venture partner, professional adviser).
- Are **not** an employee, prescribed officer, or executive of the company or its group **in the most recent three years** (look-back aligns with Companies Act s.94 but is read without de-minimis softening — King IV treats *any* recent executive role as presumptively eliminating independence).
- Are **not** a participant in any share incentive scheme.
- Receive **no remuneration from the company** other than the directors' fees, and no consideration that could materially compromise their independence (no consultancy, no advisory fees, no management contract).
- Have **served fewer than nine years** on the Board (the King IV nine-year rule — beyond nine years, independence is presumed lost unless rebutted; SA-Tier-2-bank practice typically ages out NEDs at nine years).
- Maintain **independent judgement** on Board matters, demonstrated through actions on the Board.

The King IV test is **harder than the Companies Act s.94 test** in three ways: (i) no de-minimis softening on material relationships; (ii) the share-incentive bar; (iii) the nine-year tenure cap.

### 5.3 SARB Prudential Authority reading

SARB's reading under Banks Act s.60 and PA fit-and-proper sub-instruments is **substantive**, not formal. SARB is unconcerned with whether a candidate technically clears Companies Act s.94 if a reasonable observer would conclude the candidate is financially or relationally tied to the bank's group. SARB has historically rejected "independent" designations where:

- The candidate's NED-fee income from the bank's group represented > 25% of the candidate's total annual income.
- The candidate sat on the Board of a counterparty whose business was material (above de minimis) to the bank.
- The candidate's family member (spouse, sibling, adult child) had a material commercial relationship with the bank or its group.
- The candidate had performed external advisory work for the bank's group within the look-back, even where formally not "regular advisor".

SARB's posture at the licence-application gate is to challenge each "independent" designation in writing, with specific cited facts; the Chair's designation receives the heaviest challenge.

### 5.4 BCBS Corporate Governance Principles reading

BCBS (2015) Principle 3 reinforces King IV: independence is **substantive**, the test extends to financial dependence and reputational alignment, and the Chair seat carries a "strongly recommended" independent-Chair convention for systemically important banks. While the bank is not yet D-SIFI-classified, BCBS provides the international-supervisor reading that SARB cross-references.

### 5.5 Worked example of disqualifying relationships

The following candidate profiles **fail** the substantive-independence test for this seat:

1. **Former CEO / CFO / executive director** of the bank or any bank-group entity within the past three years (Companies Act s.94 + King IV).
2. **Current or recent (≤ 3 years) external auditor or audit-firm partner** assigned to the bank's audit (Companies Act s.94 + Banks Act s.61 + Auditing Profession Act 26 of 2005). Stronger: the candidate's current audit firm cannot be the bank's PA-approved external auditor, even if the candidate is not the audit partner.
3. **Current or recent (≤ 3 years) external legal counsel** to the bank or its group (Companies Act s.94(5) "regular advisor").
4. **Current member of a banking-counterparty Board** where the counterparty is material to the bank (correspondent-bank relationship; SAMOS sponsor relationship; CLS sponsor relationship — see `project_indirect_participant_posture.md`).
5. **Family member relationship with Marc (CEO) or any other licence-day human seat** (spouse, life partner, parent, child, sibling, in-law).
6. **Material business relationship through a controlled / inter-related entity** — e.g. the candidate is the Chair of a JSE-listed financial-services entity which holds a material block of the bank's debt or equity.
7. **Income concentration** — the candidate's NED-fee income from the bank exceeds 25% of total annual income (SARB substantive read).
8. **Recent participation in a share-incentive scheme** of the bank or its group (King IV).
9. **Tenure > 9 years** on the Board of the bank or any related entity (King IV; this is hypothetical at licence-day but binds across the candidate's career-tenure).
10. **Concurrent NED at a competitor SA bank or material counterparty** — SARB / FSCA conflicts policy bars this.

The Chair candidate must clear **all ten**. Per Owen + Imani §2.2, the substantive-independence test is the **binding constraint** that drives the candidate-pool size — the SA NED population that is genuinely independent of the bank's nascent group, has AC-chair tenure at a regulated financial institution, and carries appropriate financial-statements literacy is small.

### 5.6 Independence-test discharge in the recruitment process

Nolan (Recruiter) discharges the substantive-independence test in three steps:

1. **Screen at long-list** — formal Companies Act s.94 disqualifiers (no recent executive role; no audit-firm partnership; no regular-advisor history; no family relationship with any licence-day human seat).
2. **Investigate at short-list** — King IV substantive read (income concentration; share-incentive history; counterparty Board overlaps; family-business relationships; counsel-type relationships); references-based check (≥ 1 prior Board-relationship reference).
3. **Attest at offer** — written substantive-independence declaration in the appointment letter (Imani drafts per Owen + Imani §6.4); annual re-attestation thereafter.

- Mutual banks (e.g. GBS Mutual, Finbond pre-bank-licence comparators).
- Insurer Boards of comparable balance-sheet size (R5–R30bn assets equivalent at licence-day projections — though the bank's R300m capital target sits below this scale at Day-1).
- Banks-in-formation that have lodged licence applications in the past five years.

The bank's R300m capital-target and institutional-only wholesale-markets posture place it **below** the typical listed-Tier-2-bank Chair comp band, and **above** the typical S/M-cap insurer Chair band. The licence-day Chair package should sit in the **lower quartile** of that combined comparator class.

### 6.2 Structure (King IV NED appointment shape)

NED compensation is structured to **preserve independence** under King IV Principle 7. The Chair package therefore:

- **Excludes equity** — no shares, no options, no LTI participation. Independence is incompatible with equity exposure to the bank's performance.
- **Excludes variable** — no STI / bonus, no performance-linked payments. The fee is fixed.
- **Comprises three components**:
  1. **Annual retainer** — fixed annual fee for Board Chair role; covers standard meeting attendance and CEO 1:1 cadence.
  2. **AC Chair fee** — fixed annual fee for the AC Chair role, typically 50–80% of the Board Chair retainer.
  3. **S&E Committee membership fee** — fixed annual fee for S&E Committee membership; typically 15–25% of Board Chair retainer.
  4. **Per-meeting fees (optional)** — some SA banks pay per-meeting attendance fees on top of retainers; convention varies.
- **Chair premium** — the Board Chair retainer carries a premium of ≈ 2.0–2.5x a base NED retainer, reflecting the Chair's load.
- **Benefits-in-kind** — minimal; covered travel for in-person Board attendance; D&O insurance coverage.

### 6.3 Payment cadence

Quarterly in arrears is convention. Director fees are subject to PAYE under the Tax Administration Act and feed the human-cost line in Camille's licence-day human-cost budget.

### 6.4 Board-package sizing relative to the licence-day human-cost envelope

Per `Owner Inbox/actioned/2026-05-09_owen-imani_thin-human-layer-minimum-possible.md` §3 and the post-modification 6-human roster, the licence-day NED-fee aggregate (3 NEDs incl. Chair premium + S&E and AC committee fees) is the second-largest component of the licence-day human-cost line after the human-CRO + human-CFO + human-MLRO+FIC CO+IO triple-hatted compliance lead salaries. The Chair package alone is ≈ 35–45% of the NED-fee aggregate (Chair premium + AC Chair premium + S&E member fee).

### 6.5 What goes to the CEO for approval

PAX recommends the CEO approve **the framing in §6.1–§6.4**, with **specific numbers** generated by Camille for the CEO + Remuneration Committee at the offer-stage of recruitment (post-shortlist). The CEO retains control of the absolute number; Camille generates the framework in which the number lands.

## 7. Search strategy

### 7.1 Channels

| Channel | Strength | Weakness |
|---|---|---|
| **SA Board-search executive-search firms** (typical: Spencer Stuart SA, Heidrick & Struggles SA, Egon Zehnder SA, Korn Ferry SA, Russell Reynolds SA, smaller SA-only firms — Jack Hammer, GR8 Leaders, Search Partners International) | Established Board-search practices; deep SA NED-pool databases; substantive-independence diligence is part of the standard product. | Fees (typically 30–35% of first-year fees, paid by the bank); two-firm parallel approach is common. |
| **Institute of Directors South Africa (IODSA)** | Maintains the SA director registry; CD(SA) and Fellow rosters are searchable; IODSA member network is the SA NED community. | Lower volume than search firms; better for diligence and reference cross-check than initial sourcing. |
| **Regulator-alumni networks** (former PA / FSCA / FIC officers; former SARB department heads; National Treasury alumni) | High banking-regulatory fluency; substantive credibility with SARB at licence-application gate. | Limited pool; fit-and-proper sometimes contested if recently-regulator (PA cooling-off period applies). |
| **CA(SA) / banking-regulatory practitioner networks** (SAICA Financial Services Practice Forum; BASA Risk and Compliance Forums; Banking Sector Education and Training Authority alumni) | High financial-statements literacy; AC-chair pool is concentrated here. | Often combined with current-employment relationships requiring de-conflicting. |
| **Marc (CEO) personal / professional network** | Speed; reference quality; cultural fit. | Independence-test exposure — Marc-network candidates require enhanced independence diligence and (where any prior commercial relationship exists) automatic disqualification. The CEO-network channel is **last resort**, not first. |

**Recommended dispatch: two SA Board-search firms in parallel** plus the IODSA channel as a diligence cross-check.

### 7.2 Long-list / short-list concept

| Stage | Pool size | Filter |
|---|---|---|
| **Long-list** | 15–25 candidates | Surface SA-listed-bank Board / regulated-FSI Board experience + AC chairmanship track record. Basic Companies Act s.69 disqualification screen. |
| **Initial diligence** | Reduce to 8–12 | Apply formal Companies Act s.94 substantive-independence test + Banks Act s.60 fit-and-proper screen. Reference temperatures (informal calls). |
| **Short-list** | 3–5 candidates | King IV substantive-independence diligence; income-concentration check; counterparty-Board overlap check; financial-statements literacy interview; CEO-Chair fit conversation. |
| **Final candidate** | 1 | CEO + Nolan-led offer; written substantive-independence attestation; PA fit-and-proper file; appointment letter (Imani-drafted). |

### 7.3 Skills-matrix complementarity

The Chair seat is **one of three NED hires**. The Chair candidate is selected first; the remaining two NEDs are recruited against the **gap** the Chair leaves in the NED-skills matrix per `Owner Inbox/2026-05-06_governance-framework.md` §1 (and per Owen + Imani §3 item 2). The skills-matrix dimensions:

- Banking-regulatory fluency (SARB / FSCA / FIC).
- Financial-statements literacy at IFRS 9 + Banks-Act-Reg-Returns level.
- Risk / Treasury / ALM expertise.
- Technology / cyber awareness (JS 1/2024).
- Conduct / consumer / TCF expertise.
- Transformation / B-BBEE contribution.
- International / cross-border experience.
- Markets / trading-bank exposure.

The Chair is sequenced first because the Chair's profile (necessarily strong on Board-leadership, AC-chairmanship, financial-expertise) typically covers 3–4 of the 8 dimensions; NEDs #2 and #3 fill the remaining 4–5 between them. Inverting the order (recruit NEDs #2 and #3 first) creates a skills-matrix overhang that the Chair recruitment must absorb — generally a worse outcome.

### 7.4 Diversity and B-BBEE contribution

PAX recommends the search target a Chair whose appointment **materially advances** the bank's B-BBEE Codes contribution (gender, race, disability dimensions) **provided** substantive-independence and the AC-chairmanship-tenure must-have are not relaxed. The minimum-possible composition (3 NEDs) makes it harder to achieve the diversity-mix objectives the framework's §1 skills matrix sets; the Chair seat is the highest-impact contributor.

## 8. Timeline to licence-application lodgment

### 8.1 Sequencing under D-THIN-HUMAN-LAYER-MINIMUM

The CEO decision record `Owner Inbox/2026-05-08_scrooge_ceo-decision-record_d-thin-human-layer-minimum.md` operationalises a **6-human + Marc + audit-firm** composition where the human CRO is appointed **before** licence-application lodgment (Mira + Zara concentration-risk challenge). Under this sequencing:

- **Priority-1**: Human CRO (separate brief; not this).
- **Priority-2 (this brief)**: Independent Chair / AC Chair / S&E NED.
- **Priority-3**: Independent NED #2 (Audit Committee member; recruited against Chair-skills-matrix gap).
- **Priority-4**: Third NED (Audit Committee third member; recruited against remaining skills-matrix gap).
- **Priority-5**: MLRO + FIC CO + IO triple-hatted compliance lead.
- **Priority-6**: Human Company Secretary.

NEDs #2 and #3 cannot start until the Chair appointment is signed, because their skills-matrix shape depends on what the Chair brings.

### 8.2 Indicative timeline (agent-cadence equivalents; calendarised here only because SARB pre-application gate sets external dates)

- **T0 — CEO approves this brief.**
- **T0 → T+30 days** — Nolan engages two Board-search firms; brief and signed engagement letters; long-list construction begins.
- **T+30 → T+90 days** — long-list to initial-diligence shortlist (8–12); reference temperatures; substantive-independence pre-screen.
- **T+90 → T+150 days** — short-list (3–5); CEO meets each shortlist candidate; King IV substantive-independence diligence; financial-expertise interview; CEO-Chair fit conversation.
- **T+150 → T+180 days** — final candidate; offer letter; substantive-independence attestation; PA fit-and-proper file assembly; CIPC director-appointment filing readied.
- **T+180** — appointment effective; Chair signs in.

**Total: 4–6 months end-to-end.** SA Board-search firms quote 3–6 months for senior NED Chair searches; 4–6 months is the realistic range for a first-time-bank Chair where the substantive-independence test eliminates a substantial fraction of the long-list.

### 8.3 The Chair sets the tone for NED #2 and #3 sourcing

Once the Chair is appointed, the Chair:
- Reviews the NED-skills-matrix gap.
- Participates in the NED #2 and #3 search firm briefings.
- Conducts CEO-Chair-NED-final-round interviews on shortlist candidates.
- Signs each NED appointment letter alongside the CEO.

This is why the Chair is **load-bearing** in the licence-day Board-formation timeline. NEDs #2 and #3 are 6–9 months from Chair-appointment-date; the licence-application file's Board-composition section is finalised at NED-#3-appointment-date.

### 8.4 Cumulative pre-lodgment timeline

Chair (T0–T+180) ⇒ NEDs #2 and #3 (T+180–T+450) ⇒ licence-application Board-composition section ready at **T+450**.

If the licence-application target is set, the Chair search must open immediately on PR merge to land the appointment by the lodgment date with NED #2 and #3 sequencing room. **A delay in the Chair search compounds three-to-one in Board-formation timeline.**

## 9. Open questions for the CEO

Each question is one the CEO is best-placed to resolve before the search opens. Recommended answer is provided where PAX has a view; the CEO retains full discretion.

### 9.1 International Chairs with SA experience — in scope?

**Question.** Should the search include **internationally-resident candidates with SA banking experience** (e.g. SA-born former-bank-Chairs now resident in the UK, Mauritius, Australia)?

**PAX recommendation.** **No.** SA residence is a must-have. SARB Prudential Authority's read on Director residence under Banks Act s.60 is substantive — a Chair who is not SA-resident faces credibility challenge on whether they can credibly discharge fiduciary duties at a SARB-licensed bank. The international-Chair option is available **post-licence**, not at licence-day.

### 9.2 Compensation ceiling at SA-comparator levels — bind or guide?

**Question.** Is the §6.1 comparator-class framing a **ceiling** (the bank refuses to pay above the lower-quartile of comparator class) or a **guide** (the bank may flex if the candidate justifies it)?

**PAX recommendation.** **Guide, with CEO + Camille flex authority** (the CEO sets the absolute number). A ceiling is the right starting posture; the bank should not flex absent a candidate-shape that justifies it. But PAX has seen at least two SA Tier-2-bank Chair searches stall on rigid comp ceilings; flexibility is the better operating posture.

### 9.3 Sequencing relative to NED #2 and NED #3 sourcing — strict or overlap?

**Question.** Should NED #2 and NED #3 sourcing begin **only after** the Chair appointment, or should it begin **in parallel** to compress the timeline?

**PAX recommendation.** **Strict sequencing — Chair first.** The skills-matrix complementarity argument in §7.3 is binding. Parallel sourcing creates a worse outcome on the post-modification Board's skills mix and risks a sub-optimal Chair-NED match.

### 9.4 Audit-Committee-Forum interim role — Chair-elect bridge or post-appointment activation?

**Question.** Owen (Company Secretary, engineering / governance, agent) currently chairs the **Interim Audit Forum** until a Board AC is constituted (per `Team/Owen.md` §3, §15). When the Chair (this seat) is appointed, does the Chair **immediately take over** as Interim Audit Forum chair (closing Owen's interim conflict early), or does Owen continue chairing until full Board AC constitution at NED-#2-and-NED-#3-appointment-date?

**PAX recommendation.** **The Chair takes over the Interim Audit Forum on appointment**, with Owen reverting to the Forum-secretariat role. This closes Owen's registered interim conflict 6–9 months earlier than full-AC-constitution would, and gives the Chair operational engagement with the bank's audit-equivalent function before the full AC stands up. Owen + Imani's framing per `Owner Inbox/actioned/2026-05-09_owen-imani_thin-human-layer-minimum-possible.md` is consistent with this.

### 9.5 S&E Committee Chair — same person as Board Chair, or separated?

**Question.** Companies Reg. 43 names an S&E Committee with at least one independent NED but does not require the *Chair* of the S&E Committee to be that independent NED. The minimum-possible composition could give the S&E Committee Chair role to **NED #2 or NED #3** rather than this seat — distributing committee-chair load across the Board.

**PAX recommendation.** **Distribute — give the S&E Committee Chair to NED #2 or NED #3.** The Chair seat already carries Board Chair + AC Chair; layering S&E Chair on top concentrates committee-chairmanship load too heavily. The Chair can remain a member of S&E (as required by Companies Reg. 43) without chairing it. NED #2 or #3 picks up S&E Chair; the choice is made at the NED-#2-and-#3 recruitment stage.

### 9.6 King IV nine-year-tenure clock — does Chair-elect's prior NED tenure on adjacent boards count?

**Question.** A candidate with 7–8 years' prior NED tenure at *another* SA bank approaches the King IV nine-year-clock at this bank's Board within 1–2 years of joining. Is this a disqualifier or accepted?

**PAX recommendation.** **Accepted, but flagged.** King IV's nine-year clock runs **per Board** (each entity's clock is independent). However, the candidate's overall career-tenure as a bank Director is a substantive-independence factor under the SA-bank-NED ageing-out convention. PAX recommends the search not exclude on this basis but disclose at the licence-application file.

### 9.7 Treatment of the Audit-Committee-Forum-chair conflict — close on Chair appointment, or close on full AC constitution?

**Question.** Linked to §9.4 — Owen's interim conflict on chairing his own AC-equivalent (per `Team/Owen.md` §15). If the Chair takes over the Interim Audit Forum on appointment, Owen's conflict closes immediately. If not, Owen continues to manage the conflict via Thandiwe's (Chief Audit Executive — engineering / governance, agent) functional independence into the Forum.

**PAX recommendation.** **Close on Chair appointment** (consistent with §9.4). Conflict-closure events are register-tracked under Owen's own conflicts register.
