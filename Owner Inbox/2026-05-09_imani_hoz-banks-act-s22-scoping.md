## 1. The two legs of the s.22 test

Banks Act § 22 imposes a name-discipline obligation on entities that conduct (or intend to conduct) the business of a bank. Counsel must opine on **both** legs:

### Leg A — deceptive similarity to existing banks

- The mark "Hoz" must not be deceptively similar to the registered name of any other bank, mutual bank, cooperative bank, foreign-bank branch, or DFI authorised under the Banks Act 94 of 1990 or under cognate legislation. The test is broader than the TM Office's similarity test: it includes the regulator's view of likely customer / counterparty confusion, taking into account branding, holding-out, and the overall market-presence picture.
- The authoritative source for the comparison set is the SARB Prudential Authority's published list of authorised banks, mutual banks, cooperative banks, foreign-bank branches, and Schedule of registered DFIs. Counsel performs the authoritative scan.

### Leg B — implication of unauthorised activity

- The mark "Hoz" must not, by its form or its accompanying styling, imply that the entity conducts banking business if the entity is not authorised to do so. For a bank that *will* be authorised, this leg is generally satisfied at and after licence-day; the relevant test is the build-phase posture — whether using "Hoz Bank" or any "Hoz" + bank-implying suffix in public-facing materials before licence-day creates an implication of unauthorised banking activity.
- **Build-phase mitigation:** the bank's external use of the name during the build phase is governed by the build-phase no-real-customer / no-real-trading rule (`project_ai_driven_bank.md`). Counsel's opinion must specify whether the build-phase use Imani + Linnea + Owen anticipate (e.g. domain registration in Devon-Tomas's parallel PR; CIPC reservation in Gate 3; brand collateral in the substrate-application carry-through) is permissible without licence, and what disclosure / disclaimer posture if any is required.

## 2. SARB Prudential Authority pre-application engagement plan

Counsel must produce a written **pre-application engagement plan** specifying when and how the bank's name choice is notified to SARB Prudential Authority before the formal Section 12 / Section 13 licence application is lodged.

The plan must address:

- **Timing.** When in the pre-application sequence does the name choice surface to PA? Best-practice is name-pre-clearance at the earliest meaningful PA touchpoint, well before lodgment, so that an objection from PA does not invalidate the application document.
- **Channel.** Which PA touchpoint hosts the name conversation — the PA Banking Supervision team, a designated licensing contact, or a dedicated pre-application engagement?
- **Form of submission.** What does PA expect to receive — a written name-justification memorandum, the counsel's s.22 opinion, the TM-clearance opinion (Gate 1), or some combination?
- **Contingent-pathway plan.** If PA raises a s.22 concern that counsel's opinion did not anticipate, what is the fallback plan? The D-BANK-NAME-FALLBACK candidates (Lucet · Noeta · Synaps) are pre-cleared in parallel for exactly this reason; counsel's plan should name the fallback path.

This plan is also adjacent to (but not the same as) the deferred CRO-seat-criteria question on PA pre-application engagement timing more broadly. Helena (Chief Risk Officer, governance) owns the broader CRO-seat criteria; counsel's name-engagement plan is a component of the broader pre-application plan, not the whole of it.

## 4. Three-letter form — implications for the s.22 test

The Hoz mark is three letters. Implications for the s.22 test (counsel to confirm):

- **Leg A (deceptive similarity)** — short distinctive marks have *less* inherent room for confusion with another bank's brand than longer marks (because there are fewer overlapping characters / phonemes). The s.22 deceptive-similarity surface is therefore *plausibly lower* on Hoz than on a longer mark. Counsel confirms.
- **Leg B (implication of unauthorised activity)** — independent of length. The s.22 implication leg applies to the *name + styling combination*, not the bare three-letter form, and is therefore unaffected by the brevity of the mark.
- **Counsel's net view** — to be expressed in the s.22 opinion, with a risk rating that distinguishes the two legs.
