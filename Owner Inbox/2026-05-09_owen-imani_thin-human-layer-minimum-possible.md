## 2. Statutory minimum, seat by seat

For each statutory seat under SA banking law, the partition below records: legal source · separability rule · SARB precedent commentary · mergeable-with-CEO verdict. ### 2.1 Companies Act 71 of 2008 — directors

- **Source.** Companies Act s.66(2): a public company must have **at least three directors**. The bank is a public company suitable to hold a banking licence (per `2026-05-08_imani_legal-readiness.md` — corporate form is "single public company").
- **Separability.** Three directors is a hard floor. There is no provision under the Companies Act that allows a public company to operate with fewer.
- **SARB precedent.** SARB Prudential Authority's fit-and-proper expectation under the Banks Act 94 of 1990 reads the Companies Act floor as the *floor*, not the licence-day target. A licence-day Board for a SARB-licensed bank with three directors is at the bottom edge of credibility; SARB has historically asked applicants to demonstrate the Board can discharge reserved matters (committee chairs; AC composition under s.94 of the Companies Act read with the Banks Act). The Banks Act itself does not name a specific Board size, but **s.60** of the Banks Act and the Regulations Relating to Banks expect a Board "of sufficient size and composition" to discharge fiduciary duties.
- **Mergeable with CEO?** Marc serves as one director (executive). The remaining two slots are **mandatory separate humans**. Of those two, at least one must satisfy independence under King IV / Companies Act regulation 43 (Audit Committee independence — see §2.2) and chair the Board.
- **Register row.** [register: ORG-GV-01 covers CoSec; **ORG-GV-DIRECTORS-MINIMUM is a gap** — route to Mira to add a row citing Companies Act s.66(2) + Banks Act s.60.]

### 2.2 Companies Act 71 of 2008 — Audit Committee composition (s.94)

- **Source.** Companies Act s.94(2): a public company must have an Audit Committee with **at least three members**, all of whom must be **non-executive directors**, and at least one of whom must have appropriate financial / accounting expertise. Companies Regulation 43 (Social & Ethics Committee) layers a further Committee with at least three members, of whom at least one must be an independent NED — though the S&E Committee may share members with the AC.
- **Separability.** Three non-executive directors are a hard floor for the AC. Marc, as executive director, **cannot sit on the AC**. The bank therefore needs **three NEDs at minimum** to constitute a compliant AC.
- **SARB precedent.** A SARB-licensed bank's AC is also reviewed against BCBS Corporate Governance Principles 2015 — independence of audit-committee members is treated as substantive, not formal. NEDs who are independent in name but financially dependent on the bank's group are challenged.
- **Mergeable with CEO?** **No.** This is the binding constraint that pushes the human count above the Companies-Act-s.66 floor. The bank needs three NEDs to constitute an AC, even if Companies Act s.66 alone would allow a Board of two NEDs + Marc.
- **Caveat — read S&E and AC together.** S&E membership can overlap with AC membership; the same three NEDs can constitute both, provided one is independent. The single S&E-required-independent-NED is the same independent Chair under the framework's §1.
- **Register row.** [register: route to Mira — Companies Act s.94 + Companies Regulation 43 row not currently present in `_obligations-register.md`; add as ORG-GV-AC-MINIMUM.]

### 2.3 Banks Act 94 of 1990 — CEO; CRO; CFO; auditor (s.61)

- **Source — CEO.** Banks Act + Regulations Relating to Banks contemplate a CEO who is fit-and-proper and accountable to the Board. The CEO is an executive director.
- **Source — CRO and CFO.** Joint Standard 1 of 2024 + BCBS expectations + Banks Act / PA fit-and-proper standards (per `_obligations-register.md` ORG-GV-11) name CRO and CFO among the designated officers fit-and-proper standards apply to. **Whether they must be separate humans from the CEO** is not a hard statutory bar but is a SARB precedent matter.
- **SARB precedent — CEO/CRO/CFO merger.** SARB Prudential Authority's fit-and-proper review treats the CEO + CRO + CFO triumvirate as *separate roles*. A small bank may credibly run with the CEO covering one of the other two for an interim period, but a licence-day applicant naming Marc as CEO + CRO + CFO would be challenged on the **three-lines-of-defence** principle (CRO is second line; combining first-line CEO accountability with second-line CRO challenge is an architectural conflict). The Joint Standard 1 of 2024 expectation that the CRO function is operationally independent of the CEO is the binding constraint here, not statute.
- **Mergeable with CEO?**
  - **CRO:** **conditionally mergeable** — but only with explicit SARB acceptance, which licence-day applicants do not customarily seek because the resulting fit-and-proper risk is high. **Recommended: separate human, but the role can be carried by Helena (engineering CRO) under interim governance until licence-day, at which point a named human CRO is appointed.** Helena is an *agent*; the licence-day named CRO is the human Helena's outputs flow through. **Note:** the Helena-as-engineering-CRO / human-CRO seat distinction is the same engineering-vs-governance split CLAUDE.md already names; the human seat is the one SARB sees.
  - **CFO:** **conditionally mergeable** — same shape as CRO. Camille (engineering CFO) carries the engineering load; a human CFO is named at licence-day. Where the CFO function fully merges with the CEO under "minimum possible", the licence-application narrative must explicitly defend the merger; SARB's likely position is that for a R300m-target capital-base institution-only wholesale dealer, an external auditor + AC scrutiny *might* substitute for CFO independence at licence-day specifically, but this is an applicant's argument to make, not a statutory permission.
- **Source — auditor (Banks Act s.61).** Banks Act s.61 + Companies Act s.90 require the appointment of an external auditor approved by the Prudential Authority. **The auditor is a firm, not a single named human in the bank's roster** — but the firm's audit partner is a named individual whose fit-and-proper SARB will review. The audit partner is an employee of the audit firm, **not a bank employee**, and does not count toward the bank's own thin-human-layer headcount.
- **Mergeable with CEO?** **No** — auditor independence is non-negotiable under Companies Act s.94 and the Auditing Profession Act 26 of 2005.
- **Register row.** [register: ORG-GV-11 covers fit-and-proper for designated officers; **CEO/CRO/CFO separability under Banks Act s.60 + Joint Standard 1 of 2024 is a gap** — route to Mira to add ORG-GV-CRO-INDEPENDENCE and ORG-GV-CFO-INDEPENDENCE rows with the Joint-Standard citation.]

### 2.4 FIC Act 38 of 2001 — MLRO and FIC Compliance Officer (s.43A and s.43B)

- **Source.** FIC Act s.43A: an accountable institution must designate a **Money Laundering Reporting Officer (MLRO)** to whom STRs are reported internally. FIC Act s.43B: an accountable institution must appoint a **Compliance Officer** responsible for the institution's FIC obligations. These two functions are statutorily named.
- **Separability.** **One person can hold both designations** — this is established FIC practice and reflected in `_obligations-register.md` ORG-FC-11 ("Designate an internal AML compliance officer and a Money Laundering Reporting Officer (MLRO)"; current holder Zara, who is named in CLAUDE.md as **both** MLRO and FIC Compliance Officer).
- **SARB / FIC precedent on CEO-merger.** **The MLRO / FIC CO function cannot credibly be held by the CEO** under FIC's published precedent on accountable-institution governance. The MLRO is the named recipient of internal STR escalations; if the CEO is the MLRO, the STR-escalation pathway has no second-line check before it leaves the institution. FIC's published guidance on RMCP construction treats CEO + MLRO merger as a structural defect.
- **Mergeable with CEO?** **No** — **mandatory separate human.** Mergeable with the FIC Compliance Officer (statutorily contemplated). Mergeable with the POPIA Information Officer (see §2.5) under SA practice, where the same individual carries the conduct-and-privacy compliance lead role.
- **Register row.** [register: ORG-FC-11 in force; CEO-merger-prohibited gloss is implicit in published FIC guidance — route to Mira to harden the citation chain on the register row.]

### 2.5 POPIA Act 4 of 2013 — Information Officer (s.56)

- **Source.** POPIA s.56(a): every responsible party must designate an Information Officer. POPIA s.56(b): in the absence of designation, the head of the body / CEO is the IO **by default**.
- **Separability.** POPIA explicitly contemplates that the head of the body (i.e. CEO) **is** the IO unless someone else is designated. So in formal terms, the IO can be the CEO. **However**, the Information Regulator's published guidance (October 2021 onwards) recommends designation of a separate IO with appropriate authority and resources, and POPIA Regulation 4 (made under s.112) sets out duties — managing PAIA requests, breach-notification handling, privacy-impact assessments — that fit awkwardly on a CEO's day.
- **SARB / Information-Regulator precedent.** For a regulated financial institution, the Information Regulator's expectation is that the IO is a designated officer, not the CEO by default. Iris is named in CLAUDE.md as the IO; the build-phase posture per `project_ai_driven_bank.md` § WS-E1-IO-OPTIONS is that **Marc serves as interim IO** until designation is lodged with the Information Regulator (the lodgment is itself a CEO act, out-of-system).
- **Mergeable with CEO?** **Conditionally mergeable** — in formal POPIA terms, yes; in licence-application credibility terms, no for steady state. The "minimum possible" reading for a build-phase / licence-day bank is: **Marc holds IO interim until the same statutory human appointed as MLRO is designated as IO**. Combining IO with MLRO + FIC CO in one person is established SA practice (the conduct-and-privacy compliance lead) and is the lowest-headcount credible posture.
- **Register row.** [register: ORG-PR(IV)-13 in force as PARTIAL — IO designation deferred per Round 1 E1; the merger-with-MLRO posture is the route to closing the partial.]

### 2.6 FAIS Act 37 of 2002 — key individuals (where FSP licence is sought)

- **Source.** FAIS Act s.8 read with Determination of Fit and Proper Requirements 2017: every authorised FSP must have at least one **Key Individual** approved by the FSCA. The KI is responsible for managing or overseeing the rendering of financial services.
- **Separability.** Whether the bank needs an FSP licence at all under its institutional-only / wholesale posture is itself a counsel question (per `2026-05-09_imani_external-counsel-licence-application.md` § 2.4). **The author's read** — supplied here as engineer-not-counsel input, to be confirmed by external counsel at the licence-application gate — is that an institutional-only wholesale markets dealer transacting with **licensed-FSP / institutional / professional counterparties** under FAIS Subordinate Legislation typically does not require FSP licensing for those counterparty interactions, *but* the question is unsettled for novel-posture institutions and the conservative posture is to pursue an FSP licence in the categories the bank's product set requires.
- **SARB / FSCA precedent on KI-merger.** The KI may be the same person as the CEO, the CRO, or the CCO under FAIS practice (the FSCA's General Code of Conduct does not bar CEO-KI merger). However, the KI fit-and-proper test is a separate process from the Banks Act fit-and-proper, and the KI must be operationally responsible for the FSP activities — a CEO who is also KI for an institutional-only dealer is a credible posture under FAIS, less so under combined SARB + FSCA review.
- **Mergeable with CEO?** **Conditionally mergeable.** If FSP licence is pursued (the conservative posture pending counsel confirmation), the KI seat **can** be Marc, **or** Saskia (Head of Global Markets — already named in CLAUDE.md as a candidate FAIS Key Individual), **or** the same MLRO+FIC CO+IO triple-hatted human. The minimum-possible reading: **KI = Saskia** (when the human Saskia seat is hired alongside Imani's licence-day human-CRO / human-CFO appointments) **or KI = Marc-as-CEO** if the regulator accepts that posture.
- **Register row.** [register: route to Mira — FAIS Act s.8 row is not currently present in `_obligations-register.md` (the register's current scope is FIC-leaning); add ORG-FAIS-KI rows once Mira's regulatory-instrument expansion includes FSCA conduct standards.]

### 2.7 Companies Act 71 of 2008 — Public Officer (Tax Administration Act + Companies Act)

- **Source.** Companies Act + Tax Administration Act: the company must designate a Public Officer responsible for SARS-facing matters (cited as ORG-TX-08 in `_obligations-register.md`, current holder Camille).
- **Separability.** The Public Officer **may be the CEO** — there is no statutory bar to CEO + Public Officer merger.
- **Mergeable with CEO?** **Yes — mergeable.** Marc as Public Officer interim is straightforward.
- **Register row.** ORG-TX-08 (in force).

### 2.8 The CoSec — Companies Act ss.86–89

- **Source.** Companies Act ss.86–89: a public company must maintain a Company Secretary. The CoSec must satisfy specified competence, experience, and independence requirements.
- **Separability.** **The CoSec may be the company's CFO or financial director, but may not be the CEO** under Companies Act s.86(2)(b) read with the requirement that the CoSec is "duly empowered" to perform the CoSec function and reports to the Board on certain matters. SARB precedent on Banks Act fit-and-proper reads the CoSec's independence from the CEO as substantive.
- **Mergeable with CEO?** **No — mandatory separate human.** This is one of the two clear "no merger with CEO" categories under SA company law (the other is the auditor under §2.3).
- **Note on the engineering-Owen.** Owen is named in CLAUDE.md as the engineering CoSec (an *agent*); the human CoSec is the seat the licence-day human is appointed to. Owen-the-agent's outputs flow through the human CoSec, exactly as Helena/Camille do for CRO/CFO.
- **Register row.** ORG-GV-01 (in force).

### 2.9 Other roles flagged in `_obligations-register.md` ORG-GV-11

ORG-GV-11 names CEO, CRO, CFO, COO, Treasurer, Head of Markets, CCO, CISO, CAE, GC, CHRO, CoSec, IO as designated fit-and-proper officers "where seats exist". Of these, the **statutorily-required-at-licence-day** subset under SA banking law is: **CEO, CoSec, MLRO+FIC CO, IO, external auditor (firm)**. The remainder (COO, Treasurer, Head of Markets, CISO, CAE, GC, CHRO) are **substantively required for the bank to operate**, and the *engineering substrate* covers them today (Devon, Eitan, Saskia, Rashida, Thandiwe, future GC, future CHRO are agents in CLAUDE.md). They are **not** statutorily required as separate humans at licence-day; SARB precedent expects them to be named at licence-day for fit-and-proper, but the *holder* may be agent-engineered seats with a single human signing for them, **provided** the human-CEO + human-CoSec + human-CRO + human-CFO + human-MLRO+FIC CO+IO chain is in place. SARB's likely posture is to challenge any merger that pushes second-line accountability onto the CEO; the CRO and CCO seats are the load-bearing constraints.

## 4. Concentration-risk caveats

The "minimum possible" creates three SARB Prudential Authority fit-and-proper exposures that the licence-application file must explicitly defend. These are not statutory bars; they are precedent-based challenges the applicant must answer.

### 4.1 CEO + interim-CRO + interim-CFO concentration

Marc carrying CRO and CFO interim accountability runs against the three-lines-of-defence principle. SARB's expected challenge: **how does the Board challenge the CEO's risk and finance numbers when the CEO is also the CRO and CFO?** The applicant's answer must be: (i) Helena and Camille are operationally accountable engineering seats running independently; (ii) the AC and (proxy) BRC chaired by independent NEDs perform challenge; (iii) named human CRO and CFO are appointed as the **first two hires post-licence** with a defined timeline. **This is the load-bearing concentration-risk**; the recommendation is to mitigate by accelerating the human-CRO hire to *before* licence-day if SARB Prudential Authority pre-application engagement signals concern.

### 4.2 Triple-hatted MLRO + FIC CO + IO

The compliance lead carrying MLRO + FIC CO + IO is statutorily compliant (each merger is contemplated) but is a heavy single-person load. SARB / FIC's expected challenge: **what is the deputy / succession arrangement?** The applicant's answer: (i) Zara and Iris are operationally accountable engineering seats; (ii) the human compliance lead has a deputy-IO arrangement under POPIA Reg.4; (iii) the MLRO function has an alternate (as FIC-published-guidance recommends). The deputy-IO and MLRO-alternate need to be named — under the minimum-possible posture, the alternate is **the CoSec** (seat #5), provided fit-and-proper is satisfied for that overlap.

### 4.3 NED-independence depth

Three NEDs is the floor. Two of three must be independent in the strict s.94 sense (one, the AC Chair, is the bound). SARB's expected challenge under King IV and BCBS Corporate Governance Principles 2015: **is the Board of sufficient depth on banking-regulatory experience, technology / cyber, financial expertise, and transformation / B-BBEE?** The applicant's answer: (i) the skills-and-diversity matrix at three NEDs is necessarily concentrated; (ii) the bank commits to expanding the Board to five NEDs within a defined post-licence window; (iii) the framework's §1 skills matrix is the document the Board recruits against. SARB has historically accepted floor compositions for small banks at licence-day on the strength of an explicit expansion commitment.
