## Purpose of BA 210

Form BA 210 is the prudential return through which the bank reports its **counterparty credit risk (CCR)** to the Prudential Authority. CCR is the risk that the counterparty to a **derivative or securities-financing transaction (SFT)** defaults **before** the final settlement of the transaction's cash flows — a bilateral, time-varying exposure (it can swing in either party's favour as market rates move), distinct from the funded credit risk of a loan. Because the exposure is *potential* and *future*, it cannot be measured by carrying amount alone; it requires a forward-looking exposure-at-default (EAD) model. BA 210 carries the bank's CCR EAD and the resulting CCR-RWA contribution.

The headline CCR identity (D7/2020 §2.1; BCBS CRE52 §52.5 / d317 §10; computed by the in-repo `platform/risk/sa-ccr/` engine):

```
EAD  =  α × ( RC + PFE )                          with  α = 1.4        [D7/2020 §2.1; CRE52 §52.5; d317 §10]
RC   =  max( V − C , 0 )            (unmargined)                       [CRE52 §52.10–52.18; d317 §136]
RC   =  max( V − C , TH + MTA , 0 ) (margined / CSA)
PFE  =  multiplier × Σ AddOn                                          [CRE52 §52.5; d317 §149]
AddOn (per hedging set)  =  | Σ_i ( δ_i × d_i × MF_i ) | × SF                  [d317 §156–§166]
multiplier  =  min( 1 , 0.05 + 0.95 × exp( (V − C) / (2 × 0.95 × Σ AddOn) ) ) [5% floor; d317 §149]
CCR-RWA  =  EAD × counterparty risk-weight                            [Reg 38; CRE20 / cre:22.67]
```

where **V** is the net mark-to-market of the netting set, **C** is net collateral held, **δ** is the supervisory delta (±1 for the bank's linear products), **d** is the trade-level adjusted notional, **MF** is the maturity factor, and **SF** is the per-asset-class supervisory factor. The α = 1.4 multiplier is the regulatory conservatism factor BCBS calibrated to bridge the modelled EAD to a stressed EAD. The CCR-RWA (EAD × counterparty risk-weight) is a component of the **credit-RWA** that flows into total RWA → BA 100 (Reg 38).

**The bank's actual counterparty credit risk today.** In the build phase the bank's CCR is generated by the **global-markets desks' derivative book** — FX forwards / swaps and OTC interest-rate derivatives (the bank's `ir` and `fx` SA-CCR asset classes) — plus any repo / reverse-repo SFT exposure. FX **spot** is settlement risk (T+2), not CCR in the SA-CCR sense, and is treated under settlement-risk / Herstatt feeders (RAS §B2), not BA 210. The SA-CCR engine is **fully built** (`platform/risk/sa-ccr/`) and is the live measurement path: it computes RC + PFE + EAD per netting set, emits `CcrReplacementCostComputed` + `CcrEadComputed` events, and feeds the credit-limit engine (LEX utilisation) + the BA 200 credit-RWA computation + the BA 400 leverage exposure. The bank has elected SA-CCR (the only methodology directed by D7/2020); the **Internal Model Method (IMM)** is `NOT_APPLICABLE` (no Registrar approval sought).

## The Standardised Approach for Counterparty Credit Risk (SA-CCR)

SA-CCR is the bank's regulatory CCR-EAD methodology (D7/2020; BCBS CRE52). It replaced the **Current Exposure Method (CEM)** — D7/2020 directs SA-CCR for *"all derivative transactions"*. The EAD is `α × (RC + PFE)` with α = 1.4, computed **per netting set** (the set of trades under a single legally-enforceable ISDA Master + CSA, or GMRA for repo).

### 1. Replacement cost (RC) — the current exposure (CRE52 §52.10–52.18; d317 §136)

RC is the larger of the current net cost to replace the netting set's trades and (for margined sets) the collateral thresholds, floored at zero:

- **Unmargined** netting set: `RC = max(V, 0)` — V is the net MTM.
- **Margined** netting set (live CSA / GMRA): `RC = max(V − C, TH + MTA, 0)` — V less net collateral C, floored at the CSA threshold (TH) + minimum-transfer-amount (MTA), floored at zero.

The bank's `replacement-cost.ts` implements both branches; the `csaPresent` flag on the netting-set config drives the branch (Credit Risk Policy §3 line 129). RC is single-currency per netting set (cross-currency RC is rejected at runtime — Principle 5).

### 2. PFE add-on — the potential future exposure (CRE52 §52.5 / §52.45–§52.52; d317 §149–§191)

The PFE captures the *potential* increase in exposure over the trade horizon. It is `multiplier × Σ AddOn`, where the add-on is built bottom-up:

- **Adjusted notional** (per trade) = `δ × d × MF` — supervisory delta δ (±1 for the bank's linear IRS / FRA / FX-forward products; the option supervisory delta is a follow-on slice), trade-level notional d, and the **maturity factor** MF (unmargined: `√(min(M,1y)/1y)` capped at 1.0; margined: `√(MPOR/1y)`, MPOR = 10 business days).
- **Hedging sets** — trades are grouped into hedging sets (IR: `<currency>:<maturity-bucket>` lt-1y / 1y-5y / gt-5y; FX: `<currency-pair>`; commodity: `<commodity-type>`; credit / equity: single bucket per netting set at v1). Within a hedging set, long offsets short (`Σ(δ × d × MF)` netted, then absolute-valued); between hedging sets, no offsetting.
- **Supervisory factors (SF)** — per asset class (in `pfe-addon.ts` `SUPERVISORY_FACTOR`, BCBS d317 Table 2 / CRE52 §52.45–§52.52): **ir 0.5% / fx 4.0% / credit 1.30% (non-IG) / equity 32% (single-name) / commodity 18% (other)**. The v1 engine wires one representative SF per asset class; the granular IG/sub-IG and index/single-name sub-class differentiation is a documented follow-on.
- **Multiplier** — `min(1, 0.05 + 0.95 × exp((V−C)/(2 × 0.95 × ΣAddOn)))`, floored at 5% — when the netting set is over-collateralised (V − C ≪ 0) the multiplier shrinks PFE towards its 5% floor, recognising the collateral cushion (`pfe-addon.ts` `pfeMultiplier`).

### 3. EAD composition and the α = 1.4 factor (D7/2020 §2.1; CRE52 §52.5; d317 §10)

`EAD = α × (RC + PFE)` with **α = 1.4** (`ead.ts` `ALPHA_SA_CCR = 1.4`). The EAD is the credit-equivalent amount that enters the CCR-RWA computation (`EAD × counterparty risk-weight`, CRE20 / `cre:22.67`) and the LEX utilisation denominator.

### 4. Netting and collateral recognition (D7/2020 §2.2–§2.6; Reg 23(18)(b))

- **Bilateral netting** — recognised only where the netting set is covered by an **eligible bilateral netting contract complying with Reg 23(18)(b)** (D7/2020 §2.2.1 / `ORG-PR-SACCR-002`); the bank's ISDA-Master / GMRA legal-enforceability work is **Imani's live load-bearing slice** (`@platform/markets/netting-sets`).
- **Collateral** — for the **risk-based CCR** charge, eligible collateral reduces RC (margined branch) and shrinks the PFE multiplier. (Note the contrast with the **leverage-ratio** exposure measure, where D7/2020 §2.3.1 / `ORG-PR-SACCR-003` **prohibits** netting collateral received against the exposure — a leverage-specific rule that does **not** apply to the BA 210 risk-based CCR-RWA charge.)
- **Written / purchased credit derivatives, TRS, clearing-member exposures** — D7/2020 §2.5–§2.6 (`ORG-PR-SACCR-006..009`) set the treatments; these bind once the bank originates those products (currently outside the v0 universe).

## The CVA cross-reference (a separate but adjacent charge)

The **credit-valuation-adjustment (CVA) capital charge** — capital against the risk of mark-to-market losses from a *deterioration in the counterparty's credit quality* (as distinct from the CCR default risk SA-CCR measures) — is a **separate** Basel charge (BCBS MAR50 / the basic-CVA / standardised-CVA framework). The bank computes a CVA via the `model:cva-exposure-epe-v1` / `model:cva-engine-v1` calc-model suite (an FX-spot book carries no CVA — CVA excludes spot; the CVA tile activates on a booked OTC derivative, e.g. an IRS, per `project_continuation_2026_06_02_cva_rwa_cancel_irs_reval`). The CVA charge is reported on the capital-adequacy return (BA 100 / the Reg 38 integration); whether the **SARB BA 210 form carries a dedicated CVA section** (some jurisdictions report CVA on the CCR return) is **[citation: TBC]** — the Annexure 8B line schema is not in the in-corpus source. ## CCR-RWA computation and the BA 100 capital integration

The SA-CCR EAD feeds the **CCR-RWA** = `EAD × counterparty risk-weight` (the counterparty weight from the CRE20 standardised table — e.g. a bank counterparty under ECRA / SCRA, see [`ba-200.md`](ba-200.md) §1). The CCR-RWA is a component of the **credit-RWA** that sums with market RWA (Reg 28/29) and operational RWA (Reg 33) into **total RWA** → BA 100 (Reg 38). The output floor is **no-op for this bank** (standardised approaches throughout). The in-repo `rwa-engine.ts` consumes the SA-CCR EAD as the exposure input for derivative counterparties; the BA 210 *form-line compiler* (the netting-set / asset-class / RC / PFE / EAD / CCR-RWA section → Annexure 8B cells) is PLANNED (see system-capability chain).

## Material foreign-currency reporting

The CCR book is intrinsically multi-currency (FX forwards / swaps; multi-currency IRS). SA-CCR is computed **per netting set per currency** (cross-currency within a netting set is rejected — a netting set is single-currency by Credit Risk Policy §3 line 132); the per-currency EADs are revalued into ZAR for the consolidated BA 210 figure (Principle 5 — currency at the type level; ZAR is the presentation currency, the multi-currency exposure is the data). The engine's `resolveMtm` folds FX P&L only into ZAR netting sets (FX MTM is ZAR-denominated by schema); the cross-currency EAD consolidation convention for the BA 210 figure is a generator concern.
