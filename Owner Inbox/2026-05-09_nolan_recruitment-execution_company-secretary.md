## 1. Engagement summary

This run opens active search for the **separate-human Company Secretary** seat. Three load-bearing constraints govern the engagement:

1. **Separate-human, not bundled.** The CoSec is *not* merged with the Compliance Lead seat (Priority-5). Companies Act 71 of 2008 s.86(2)(b) bars the CEO from holding the CoSec; Mira (Compliance / RegTech engineer) + Zara (CCO) §4.2 splits the deputy-IO load to the CoSec while leaving substantive AML / FAIS / FIC mandate with the Compliance Lead. The two seats are independent humans.
2. **Deputy-IO under POPIA Reg. 4.** The CoSec carries the deputy POPIA Information Officer designation under Regulation 4, formally registered with the Information Regulator at lodgment. Iris (Information Officer, governance) is the principal IO; the CoSec's deputy load is the cover-arrangement.
3. **Fractional pre-licence-day → full-time at lodgment.** PAX's role-brief §6 recommends opening the search as full-time-from-day-one with fractional as a fall-back. Per Marc (CEO) standing preference for build-phase frugality and the agent-cadence operating model (CLAUDE.md "build phase vs licence-day"), this execution paper opens the search **fractional-pre-licence-day with a full-time conversion clause at SARB licence-application lodgment**. Rationale: the Board and committees are not constituted before the Independent Chair (Priority-1) is in seat; the agenda is thin; full-time engineering substrate (Owen (Company Secretary, governance) — engineering CoSec agent) carries the daily cadence. The fractional-first stance is signalled in the search brief and in offer terms; candidates whose only viable shape is full-time-from-day-one are not screened out, but the offer letter's default is fractional-with-conversion.

The seat is named in the SARB licence-application file. Hard gate: **in seat (signed engagement letter, fit-and-proper file complete) before licence-application lodgment**.

## 5. Screening rubric

### 5.1 Stage 1 — paper screen against §3 must-have grid

Each candidate is scored against the seven hard requirements from §3 on a 0 / 1 / 2 scale (0 = absent, 1 = present, 2 = strong-fit). Pass threshold for Stage 1 → Stage 2 advancement: **score ≥ 10 of 14**, with **no zeros** on the four load-bearing dimensions (CGISA credential; bank/material-listed CoSec experience; POPIA / IO depth; substantive independence).

### 5.2 Stage 2 — interview-stage scoring matrix

Eight dimensions, each scored 0–5:

| Dimension | Weight | Probe |
|---|---|---|
| Companies Act ss.86–89 substantive command | 1.0 | Walk through the s.86(2)(a) competence test as the candidate would document it for a regulator. |
| Banks Act + PA fit-and-proper substantive command | 1.0 | Describe a "Dear CEO" cycle the candidate has been in the room for; how the CoSec function discharged. |
| POPIA Reg. 4 deputy-IO readiness | 1.0 | Walk through a POPIA breach scenario where the IO is unavailable; what does the deputy-IO do in the first 72 hours. |
| King IV applied-and-explained fluency | 0.8 | Describe a King IV Practice Note the candidate has authored or materially shaped. |
| Substantive independence from CEO | 1.2 | Describe a moment on a prior role where the candidate had to push back on a CEO on a procedural / reserved-matters question. |
| Engineering-substrate fluency | 0.6 | Reaction to operating model where minutes are drafted continuously by an agent and signed by the human CoSec — comfort, concerns, conditions. |
| Multi-entity CoSec capacity | 0.5 | Describe prior multi-entity CoSec workload management; capacity for Hoz Group + Hoz Bank + Hoz Securities. |
| Build-phase fractional-engagement fit | 0.5 | Comfort with fractional pre-licence-day → full-time at lodgment shape. |

**Stage 2 threshold for shortlist (5–7 candidates):** weighted total ≥ 22 of 33 (≈ 67%).

### 5.3 Hard fails (automatic disqualifiers)

- **Fit-and-proper breach** — any prior PA / FSCA / FIC / Information Regulator finding adverse to the candidate; any Companies Act s.69 director-disqualification-equivalent flag.
- **Lack of regulated-bank or material-listed CoSec experience** — fewer than 3 years CoSec at a SARB-regulated financial institution or JSE-listed entity at material scale.
- **Cannot demonstrate Companies Act s.86(2)(a) competence** — neither CGISA credential nor equivalent track-record evidence.
- **Substantive non-independence from CEO** — references reveal a pattern of CEO-deference on procedural / reserved-matters questions; or candidate is in substance acting as a CEO under another title at a current employer.
- **Concurrent CoSec at a competitor SA bank.**
- **Information-Regulator finding** affecting the candidate's POPIA Reg. 4 fitness.

### 6.2 Owen's role — technical-fit assessor

Owen (Company Secretary, governance) — engineering-CoSec agent — interviews the candidate on the interlock between Owen's continuously-produced substrate (drafted minutes, action-tracker state, reserved-matters routing, register updates, charter refresh proposals) and the human CoSec's statutory-signature load. The interlock is load-bearing: the human CoSec is the *named accountable signer*, not the author from scratch. Owen's interview probes:

- **Comfort with the substrate's authoring layer.** Will the candidate critique drafted minutes substantively, or rubber-stamp? Will the candidate demand re-authoring from scratch (a structural mis-fit with Principle 7)?
- **Comfort with continuous cadence.** Owen runs continuously; the human CoSec runs per Board / committee cycle. Is the candidate comfortable that the substrate doesn't pause when they're on leave?
- **Conflict-management posture.** Owen is the engineering CoSec; the human CoSec is the statutory officer and Owen reports through the seat for engineering-CoSec outputs that need human signature. The conflict is managed analogously to the Helena (Chief Risk Officer, governance) ↔ Human-CRO seat: the human is the named statutory officer, the agent is the substrate. Owen probes whether the candidate accepts this shape.

### 6.3 Iris's role — deputy-IO Reg. 4 fit assessor

Iris (Information Officer, governance) — principal POPIA Information Officer — interviews the candidate on the deputy-IO designation:

- **POPIA Reg. 4 lodgment fitness.** The deputy-IO is registered with the Information Regulator at lodgment. The candidate must be willing to sign the Reg. 4 designation form and accept the fit-and-proper checkpoint that the Information Regulator's published guidance imposes.
- **Cover-arrangement scope.** When Iris is on leave / unavailable / on resignation notice, the candidate-as-deputy carries the IO function. The candidate must be able to walk through the s.22 breach-notification workflow cold.
- **PAIA Manual co-ownership.** The candidate co-owns the PAIA Manual with Iris (`_obligations-register.md` ORG-PR(IV)-16). Iris probes the candidate's PAIA-Manual maintenance posture.

### 9.2 Cover-arrangement scope

The deputy-IO cover-arrangement runs against the Compliance Lead's IO hat — wait, this is wrong on first reading: **Iris (Information Officer, governance) is the principal IO, not the Compliance Lead**. PAX role-brief §1 names Iris as the principal IO; the deputy-IO sits with the CoSec; the Compliance Lead's hat is MLRO + FIC Compliance Officer + FAIS conduct, not POPIA IO. The Mira + Zara §4.2 split places deputy-IO with the CoSec specifically to *avoid* triple-hatting the Compliance Lead. The cover-arrangement is therefore:

| State | Principal IO | Deputy-IO authority active |
|---|---|---|
| Iris in seat, available | Iris (Information Officer, governance) discharges IO function | Deputy on standby; no acts |
| Iris on leave / brief absence | Iris remains the IO of record; deputy may act on time-critical s.22 breach-notification matters under standing instruction | Deputy active for time-critical only |
| Iris on extended absence (sick leave, sabbatical, resignation notice) | Deputy-IO carries full IO function; CIPC and Information Regulator notified of acting designation | Deputy fully active |
| Iris vacancy (between IO appointments) | Deputy-IO carries full IO function until new IO appointed | Deputy fully active; full-time CoSec load required (forces full-time conversion regardless of build-phase fractional shape) |

### 9.3 Scope of deputy authority

The deputy-IO authority is scoped to:

- POPIA s.22 breach-notification (Information Regulator + data subjects).
- POPIA s.55 IO duties — encouraging compliance, dealing with requests, working with the Information Regulator.
- PAIA s.51(1) Manual maintenance and PAIA request handling.
- Lawful-processing register cycle (interim maintenance during IO absence).

The deputy-IO is *not* authorised to:

- Approve cross-border transfers under POPIA s.72 (reserved to principal IO; escalates to CEO via Scrooge if both unavailable).
- Sign new lawful-processing register entries that materially expand scope (reserved to principal IO).
- Author the annual IO report to the Information Regulator (reserved to principal IO).
