## 1. Mandate boundary

**In scope (governance seat):**

- **Banks Act s.60 designated officer** — CRO is a designated fit-and-proper executive officer under the Act's "persons in control / executive officers" framing [citation: Banks Act 94 of 1990 s.60; exact subsection mapping TBC pending Mira register row `ORG-GV-CRO-INDEPENDENCE`].
- **Joint Standard 1 of 2024 second-line independence accountability** — the human CRO holds the operationally-independent second-line risk function; combining first-line CEO accountability with second-line CRO challenge is the structural finding Mira+Zara surfaced [citation: Joint Standard 1 of 2024 §6–§7; exact paragraph TBC pending Mira register row].
- **ICAAP / ILAAP signing authority** — named accountable signatory for the bank's internal capital and liquidity adequacy assessment processes, with CFO co-sign on the financial-quantification slice [citation: Banks Act + Regulations Relating to Banks Reg 39; PA Fit-and-Proper Standards `ORG-HR-11`].
- **Risk Appetite Statement & Framework custodianship** — approves RAS for tabling at BRC; signs to BRC for governance approval; owns the framework's discipline against breach (per RAS § 6 tier framework).
- **Risk-taxonomy oversight across the bank's product perimeter** — institutional global-markets trading bank: market risk, credit risk, counterparty-credit, liquidity (LCR/NSFR/IRRBB), operational and operational-resilience, model risk, climate-related risk.
- **Direct access to the Board / BRC** — non-negotiable; in writing in the appointment letter [citation: BCBS Corporate Governance Principles for Banks Principle 6; `ORG-GV-10`].
- **PA supervisory engagement on risk** — owns the dialogue with the SARB Prudential Authority on prudential-risk matters; reviews and signs supervisory letters.

**Out of scope (governance seat does not do):**

- **Engineering the risk substrate** — Rohan (risk engineer) and Nadia (independent-validation engineer, peer-in-second-line, functionally independent of Rohan) build the measurements, validations, and projections. The human CRO governs, not engineers.
- **Model building** — Rohan owns; the CRO does not author models. The CRO disposes red findings (Helena §3 / `Procedures/by-policy/model-validation.md` step 6).
- **AML/CFT second-line accountability** — Zara (Chief Compliance Officer, governance — agent) carries this; the licence-day human seat is the Compliance Lead (seat #5, triple-hatted MLRO + FIC CO + IO).
- **Cyber-resilience programme accountability** — Rashida (Chief Information Security Officer, governance — agent) carries the JS 1 of 2024 §6 responsible-person designation through the agent fleet [citation: Joint Standard 1 of 2024 §6]. The CRO carries the *risk-appetite line* on cyber per `ORG-CY-01`, not the programme itself.
- **First-line revenue / customer-facing accountability** — disqualifying under three-lines-of-defence discipline (Helena §2.2 #2).
- **External-audit relationship** — Camille (Chief Financial Officer, governance — agent) and Marc (CEO) own; CRO contributes risk substrate as input.

**Strongly preferred (PAX talent-market additions to Helena §2.3):**

- **Prior named-CRO accountability** at a SARB-licensed or comparable-jurisdiction's licensed bank (i.e. has carried the seat once already, not stepping up for the first time). Licence-application credibility hinges on this; SARB pre-application engagement reads strongly on prior-named-CRO experience.
- **AI-substrate-aware** — willing to govern (rather than rebuild) the engineering line under Principle 7. The interview must surface this explicitly: a candidate who wants to "build out the risk function" rather than govern the existing engineering substrate is mis-fit.
- **Event-sourced architecture fluency** — Principle 1 means every risk number is a query over the event log, not a stored balance. The CRO does not need to engineer this; the CRO needs to *trust and govern* it.
- **Climate-risk fluency at PA Guidance Note 1 of 2024 depth** [citation: PA Guidance Note 1 of 2024]. Marked preferred not must-have per Helena §2.3 — see open question §8 #1.

**Disqualifying:**

- Active first-line role within prior 24 months.
- Any unresolved supervisory finding personally addressed at the candidate (PA, FSCA, foreign equivalent).
- Conflict per item 7 unresolved by appointment.
- Inability or unwillingness to sign ICAAP / ILAAP as named accountable officer.

## 3. Talent-landscape map

The SA CRO talent pool is **structurally thin** — the country has roughly 18 PA-licensed banks plus the four big banks' subsidiary CROs, plus the DFI CROs (DBSA, IDC, Land Bank, IDT, NHFC) and the major insurers' CROs (Old Mutual, Sanlam, Discovery, MMI, Liberty). The named-CRO population at any given time is order-of-magnitude 50–80 individuals nationally; the subset who have carried a SARB-licensed-bank CRO seat is order-of-magnitude 25–35.

### 3.1 SA primary market — currently-licensed-bank CROs

| Talent pool | Public-record observable size | Suitability signals | Constraints |
|---|---|---|---|
| Tier-1 SA bank CROs (Standard Bank, FirstRand, Absa, Nedbank) and their direct deputies | ~20 individuals across CRO + deputy-CRO level | Highest credibility for SARB pre-application engagement; deepest ICAAP / ILAAP cycle experience; strongest BRC-engagement track record | Restraint clauses; high TC packages [commercial: TBC]; cultural fit with AI-substrate-first bank uncertain |
| Tier-2 / mid-tier SA bank CROs (Investec, Capitec, African Bank, Discovery Bank, TymeBank, Bidvest Bank, Ubank, Grindrod, Sasfin, Mercantile, HBZ, Habib OBSL, Olympus, Bank Zero, Access Bank SA) | ~15–20 individuals | Closer-scale fit for an applicant-bank with R300m target capital; more likely to engage with a build-phase posture | Tier-2 CROs may not have full IRB / IMM experience; product-perimeter fit varies |
| Foreign-bank-branch-in-SA CROs (HSBC SA branch, Standard Chartered SA, Bank of China JHB, BNP Paribas SA, Société Générale SA, China Construction Bank SA) | ~10 individuals | Strong cross-jurisdictional fluency; institutional-wholesale-dealer mindset native | SA-branch CROs are typically delegates of head-office group CROs; PA-licence-application credibility weaker than SA-incorporated-bank CRO |

### 3.5 Headline talent-pool size

Conservatively, the SA-resident pool of viable candidates (ignoring restraint and conflict constraints) is **~25–40 individuals**. The subset who satisfy must-haves §2 #1 + #2 simultaneously (10y at deputy-CRO+, no first-line in prior 24 months) is **~10–20 individuals**. Adding the strongly-preferred prior-named-CRO requirement narrows to **~5–10 individuals**.

This is a thin market. Head-hunter engagement should be **two-firm parallel** (one SA-rooted firm, one global firm with SA presence) to maximise reach. Foreign-market fallback under critical-skills permit is a credible plan-B; see §7.

## 6. Conflict-of-interest map

**Audit-firm overlap.** The bank's external audit firm is appointed under Banks Act s.61. If the chosen audit firm is also the candidate's most-recent (≤3 years) employer, an independence concern arises. PA fit-and-proper would not necessarily disqualify, but the conflicts register entry must be explicit and the audit-firm engagement letter must address it. PAX recommendation: at offer-stage, surface the candidate's last-3-years employment to Camille for audit-firm-selection coherence (the bank has not yet selected its audit firm — the conflict is solvable by audit-firm choice).

**Counterparty conflict.** The bank's institutional-only wholesale dealer franchise contemplates onboarding tier-1 SA banks, asset managers, and offshore counterparties as ISDA / GMRA counterparties. Any candidate with a current directorship, advisory position, or material shareholding in such a counterparty must resign or divest before appointment. Conflicts register entry per `Procedures/by-policy/conflicts-declaration.md`.

**Prior-employer restraint clauses.** Tier-1 SA bank CRO contracts typically include 12-month restraints. Head-hunter due diligence at long-list stage should map restraint horizons; offer-stage consideration includes restraint-buy-out cost (which Camille covers under the package envelope).

**Directorship-overlap with the bank's NEDs.** If the candidate has a current or recent (≤3 years) board overlap with any of the three NEDs being recruited in parallel (seats #1–#3), that surfaces in the conflicts register at appointment. The CoSec (seat #4) maintains the conflicts register; first-pass at long-list.

**Three-lines-of-defence breach if prior role was first-line.** Item §2 #2 is the binding disqualifier; the conflicts register at appointment confirms.

## 7. Alternates / fallbacks

If the SA primary market does not yield a viable shortlist within the licence-application timeline:

1. **Foreign-market hire under critical-skills permit** — UK / AU / Singapore / EU candidate. Strongest fit signal: prior PRA-SMF4, APRA CPS 220 CRO, MAS-licensed-bank CRO, EBA-licensed-bank CRO. SA work-permit feasibility under critical-skills list [citation: TBC — Department of Home Affairs critical-skills list current year]. Lead time: typically 8–12 weeks for the critical-skills permit + 2–4 weeks for relocation, against the licence-application gate timeline.
2. **Big-4 audit-firm risk-advisory partner with prior bank-CRO experience** — bridges the SA audit / regulatory network with operational fluency. Independence concern addressed by ensuring the candidate's firm is not the bank's audit firm (see §6).
3. **Former SARB / PA supervisor** — strongest possible SARB-relationship signal; cooling-off period verify-per-candidate. PA conduct rules typically require 1–2 years from PA exit; the candidate's exit date is the binding constraint. PAX recommendation: at long-list stage, verify cooling-off-period status with external counsel.
4. **Insurer-CRO with prior bank-CRO experience** — Sanlam / Old Mutual / MMI / Discovery typical pool. Bridge candidate; product-perimeter fit per §2 #5 is the verify question.
5. **Helena interim governance shell as fallback** — if no human CRO is appointed before licence-application lodgment, Helena (the agent) continues as interim governance shell with Marc co-sign per `ORG-GV-17`. This is the path Helena's brief §4 contemplates as the fall-back. The licence-application file then carries an explicit JS 1 of 2024 derogation per Mira+Zara §3.1 — *not Marc's preferred posture* and not the recommendation; named here for completeness only.
