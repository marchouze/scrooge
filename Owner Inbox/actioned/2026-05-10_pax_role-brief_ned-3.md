## 1. Role purpose + regulatory anchor

### 1.1 Why this seat exists in the form it does

Companies Act s.94(2) requires that a public company (and, by Banks Act s.60 read with the Regulations Relating to Banks, every bank) appoints an Audit Committee of **at least three members, all of whom must be non-executive directors**. Section 94(4)(a)–(c) sets the substantive-independence test that AC members must satisfy. The standard SA reading (and the one the SARB Prudential Authority operates under in practice) is that **two of the three** AC members must satisfy the strict s.94 substantive-independence test for the AC's independence to hold — the third member must be non-executive but need not satisfy the strict-independence test [citation: Companies Act 71 of 2008 ss.94(2), 94(4)–(6); Owen `2026-05-09_owen_thin-human-layer-composition-final.md` §4.3 retained caveat; Owen+Imani `actioned/2026-05-09_owen-imani_thin-human-layer-minimum-possible.md` §3 item 4].

This **deliberate non-strict-independence** for NED #3 is the load-bearing feature of the seat. It widens the candidate pool to include banking-regulatory and financial-crime practitioners whose recent professional history would fail the strict-independence test (for example: ex-SARB / ex-Prudential-Authority officials still inside the cooling-off / look-back window; recent group-secondment from a sponsor / correspondent bank; an advisory relationship with the bank's group above the de-minimis threshold; recent senior bank executive who would otherwise be excluded by the King IV three-year look-back). The independence of the AC is preserved by the two-of-three independent floor (Chair + NED #2); this third seat carries depth.

## 2. Mandate scope

### 2.1 In scope (single-hat NED)

- **Audit Committee member** — third AC member; together with the Independent Chair (AC Chair, seat #1) and NED #2 (seat #2), constitutes the 3-NED AC required by Companies Act s.94(2). Reviews financial reporting, external-auditor performance, internal-audit programme, internal financial controls, and (per the framework) cybersecurity oversight under Joint Standard 1 of 2024 §8 [citation: Companies Act s.94(7); King IV Principle 8].
- **NED fiduciary duties** — Companies Act s.76 standards of conduct; s.77 personal liability; s.78 indemnification [citation: Companies Act ss.76–78].
- **Banks Act fit-and-proper director** — same standard as the other NEDs; non-strict-s.94-independence does *not* relax fit-and-proper [citation: Banks Act s.60; PA Fit-and-Proper Standards `ORG-HR-11`].
- **Standing attendee at the Risk Committee** (proxy BRC at licence-day; full BRC post-licence) — with the human CRO chairing.
- **Member of the Social & Ethics Committee** (Companies Reg. 43) — third member; transformation / S&E mandate.
- **MLRO-alternate fall-back** — if the AC Chair (primary alternate per Mira+Zara §4.2 / Owen §5.2) cannot serve the alternate role for any reason (counsel-confirmed alternate-must-be-non-Chair reading, or PA pre-application engagement signals), this seat inherits.

### 2.2 Out of scope

- **AC chairmanship** — held by the Independent Chair (seat #1). The convention under Companies Act s.94 and King IV reads the AC Chair as substantively independent; this seat cannot chair.
- **Designated financial-expertise AC member under s.94(7)(a)** — the seat is competent on financial reporting but not the designated financial-expert (NED #2 carries that designation per the NED #2 brief).
- **Executive accountability** — non-executive only.
- **Day-to-day risk function** — held by the human CRO (seat #6).
- **Chair of any Board committee** — independence convention under King IV recommends committee chairs satisfy substantive independence.

### 2.3 What this seat *adds* to the AC

The headline of the seat is that it brings **depth on the substantive matters the AC oversees** that the strict-independence test would exclude. Specifically — the seat is the placeholder for either an SA-Reserve-Bank-alumni / recently-retired-tier-1-bank-CRO/CFO/MLRO with banking-regulatory practitioner depth, or a deep CDD / financial-crime practitioner. Both candidate-classes typically fail the strict-independence test (the SARB-alumni route by virtue of recent regulator employment within cooling-off; the financial-crime-practitioner route by virtue of advisory relationships across the SA bank sector that breach the King IV three-year look-back).

The licence-application file's defence of the three-NED floor (Owen §4.3) leans heavily on the *quality* of the practitioner depth on the third seat. A non-strict-independent third NED who brings 20+ years of inside-the-bank-regulatory-machinery experience (or 20+ years of inside-AML/CFT-machinery experience) materially strengthens the AC's substantive challenge capacity.

## 5. Independence vs non-independence

### 5.1 The Companies Act s.94(4)–(6) test

Companies Act s.94(4) sets the substantive-independence test for AC members. A member is *not* independent if, at the time of appointment or within the **three years immediately preceding** appointment, the member:

- (a) was involved in the day-to-day management of the company or its related parties;
- (b) was a prescribed officer, full-time executive employee, or material shareholder; or
- (c) was a professional adviser whose fees from the company exceeded the de-minimis threshold.

Section 94(5) extends the test to immediate family relationships. Section 94(6) reads the test as substantive — the formal absence of a relationship does not cure substantive non-independence (a disguised consultancy still fails the test).

### 5.2 What disqualifies under strict independence

Common disqualifiers under strict s.94 / King IV that this seat permits:

- **Recent SARB / PA / FIC senior employment within the look-back window.** A retired Bank Supervision Department head or a retired Prudential Authority CRO who left within the last three years would fail King IV's three-year look-back test even though they are clearly non-executive.
- **Recent secondment from a sponsor / correspondent / counterparty bank.** A retired tier-1 bank CRO seconded to advise the bank during build-phase would fail strict independence.
- **Advisory relationship within the de-minimis-exceeded threshold.** A consulting principal whose firm advised the bank's group on the licence-application file would fail strict independence.
- **Material shareholder relationship within the look-back.** A founder-shareholder advisor with equity exposure inside the threshold would fail.

This seat **deliberately** permits one of these categories. The category to permit is selected at long-list / shortlist stage: PAX recommendation is the SARB-alumni route as the primary lever and the financial-crime-practitioner route as the secondary.

### 5.3 Why this seat is deliberately non-strict-independent

The two-of-three independent-floor reading of Companies Act s.94 (see §1.1) means the AC's independence is preserved by the Chair (#1) and NED #2 (#2). The third member's substantive contribution to the AC is therefore freed from the independence constraint and can be optimised on practitioner depth.

The licence-application narrative explicitly defends this composition under Owen §4.3: the three-NED AC is at the floor of credibility and SARB will probe the Board's banking-regulatory depth. Non-strict-independence on the third NED is *the* mechanism that allows the Board to bring SA-Reserve-Bank-alumni or retired-bank-CRO depth into the AC at licence-day, rather than waiting for post-licence expansion.

### 5.4 How AC independence is preserved

The AC's independence as a body is preserved by:

- **Two-of-three independent members** — Chair + NED #2 satisfy the strict s.94 / King IV substantive-independence test. Any AC vote that requires independent-majority approval (financial-statement sign-off, external-auditor recommendation) is carried by the two independent NEDs.
- **AC Chair is independent** — King IV Principle 8 reads the AC Chair as substantively independent; the Chair's chairmanship preserves the AC's independent face to the Board, the regulator, and the external auditor.
- **Disclosure** — every NED's independence-status is declared annually in the integrated report and the regulator-facing file. NED #3's non-strict-independent status is disclosed alongside the rationale (i.e., the practitioner depth) so the regulator and the shareholder both understand the trade-off.
- **Recusal regime** — NED #3 recuses from any AC matter where the candidate's prior employment or advisory history creates a substantive conflict (e.g., a SARB-alumnus recuses from any AC vote on supervisory correspondence relating to their prior work).
- **Sunset / expansion commitment** — Owen §4.3 commits to expanding the Board to five NEDs within a defined post-licence window. The expanded Board will return the AC to a three-of-three independent composition; this seat's non-strict-independence is a transitional feature.
