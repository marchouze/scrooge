## Purpose of BA 200

Form BA 200 is the prudential return through which the bank reports its **credit-risk exposure and capital requirement** to the Prudential Authority. Credit risk — the risk of loss from a counterparty failing to meet its obligations — is the **largest Pillar-1 risk** for a typical bank: the bank must hold capital against the risk-weighted value of its on- and off-balance-sheet credit exposures. Reg 23(2) sets the return's five purposes verbatim (see Citation): an executive summary of credit-risk exposure and capital requirement; a detailed analysis incl. **key credit-risk parameters, counterparty credit risk and credit impairments**; an IRB expected-loss / impairment analysis (only for IRB banks — **not this bank**); a specialised-lending analysis; and an other-assets capital analysis.

The headline capital identity (BCBS CRE20; Reg 23(5) read with Reg 38):

```
Credit RWA       =  Σ_exposures ( EAD × standardised risk-weight )      [CRE20]
Credit-risk capital requirement  =  8% × Credit RWA                     [Reg 38 minimum]
```

where **EAD** (exposure-at-default) is the on-balance-sheet carrying amount for funded exposures, or **notional × credit-conversion-factor (CCF)** for off-balance-sheet items; and the **standardised risk-weight** is read from the CRE20 asset-class / external-rating / LTV table. The credit RWA is the dominant component of total RWA, and total RWA is the denominator of every capital ratio (CET1, Tier 1, total), so a misstated credit RWA misstates the entire capital-adequacy return (BA 700, Reg 38).

**The bank's actual credit risk today.** In the build phase the bank's credit exposure is dominated by **interbank / counterparty exposures generated by the global-markets desks** — correspondent-bank nostro balances, FX-forward / -swap and OTC-derivative counterparty exposure (measured under SA-CCR), repo / reverse-repo, and settlement exposure — plus a build-phase placeholder loan book. The bank is on the **Standardised Approach** (`D-CREDIT-RISK-CAPITAL-APPROACH-V1`); the **IRB approach is not elected** (no Registrar approval sought; the IRB sub-frameworks of Reg 23(10)–(14) and the IRB-specific D8/2023 thresholds are `NOT_APPLICABLE`). Credit **impairment** is recognised under **IFRS 9** (12-month / lifetime expected credit loss, stages 1/2/3) and reported on the BA 200 credit-impairments lines.

## The Standardised Approach (SA) for credit risk

The SA is the bank's regulatory capital basis for credit risk (Reg 23(3)(a) + (5)–(9); BCBS CRE20). It computes credit RWA by assigning each exposure to a prescribed **exposure class**, looking up a **risk weight** for that class (refined by external rating, residual maturity, or LTV), netting eligible **credit-risk mitigation**, applying **CCFs** to off-balance-sheet items, and summing `EAD × RW` across all exposures.

### 1. Exposure classes and risk weights

The CRE20 standardised-approach taxonomy assigns each counterparty / exposure to a class with a prescribed risk weight (the in-repo `rwa-engine.ts` implements this switch clause-by-clause with inline CRE20 cites — see system-capability chain). The headline weights (BCBS CRE20 + Reg 38; verified against `prototype/platform/risk/rwa-engine.ts` `standardisedRiskWeight()`):

| Exposure class | Code | Risk weight | Basis |
|---|---|---|---|
| Sovereign — domestic currency, domestically funded | sovereign-domestic-currency | **0%** | CRE20.8 national discretion (PA 0%-weights ZAR RSA-sovereign exposures); `cre:20.8`. |
| Sovereign — foreign currency | sovereign-foreign-currency | **0 / 20 / 50 / 100 / 150%** | by external rating bucket. |
| Multilateral development bank (qualifying) | mdb-zero-weight | **0%** | CRE20 qualifying-MDB list. |
| Bank | bank | **20 / 30 / 50 / 100 / 150%** | ECRA (external rating, CRE20.18) with short-term (≤ 3-month original maturity) preferential weights; **SCRA** (Standardised Credit Risk Assessment, CRE20.21) Grade A/B/C fallback where no eligible external rating. |
| Public-sector entity (PSE) | pse | **50%** (build-phase default) | CRE20 — PSE treated as bank- or sovereign-equivalent per national discretion. |
| Corporate — investment grade | corporate-ig | **20 / 50 / 65 / 100%** | unrated investment-grade corporates at **65%** post-finalised reforms (CRE20.46); rated per external grade. |
| Corporate — non-IG / unrated | corporate-non-ig | **100 / 150%** | non-IG / below-BB. |
| Regulatory retail | retail | **75%** | CRE20 regulatory-retail. |
| Residential mortgage | residential-mortgage | **20 / 25 / 30 / 40 / 70%** (LTV-step) | CRE20.71 LTV-step table (post-finalised reforms). |
| Commercial real estate | commercial-real-estate | per CRE20.83 | material-dependence-on-cash-flows test. |
| Past-due (≥ 90 days) | past-due | **150%** | CRE20 past-due treatment. |
| Equity (non-trading-book) | equity | **250%** | CRE20 equity exposure. |
| Other / catch-all | other | **100%** | default. |

**External-rating use / SCRA.** Where an eligible External Credit Assessment Institution (ECAI) rating exists, the bank uses the **External Credit Risk Assessment approach (ECRA, CRE20.18)** for bank and corporate exposures. Where no eligible external rating is available (the common case in the SA market for bank exposures), the bank falls back to the **Standardised Credit Risk Assessment approach (SCRA, CRE20.21)** — Grade A / B / C buckets with prescribed weights (the engine defaults bank exposures to SCRA Grade B). The choice of ECRA vs SCRA, and the ECAI mapping table, are set in the Credit Risk Policy and operationalised in the RWA engine.

### 2. Credit-risk mitigation (CRM) — collateral, guarantees, netting

The SA recognises eligible **credit-risk mitigation** to reduce EAD or substitute a lower risk weight (BCBS CRE22; Reg 23(6)–(9)):

- **Financial collateral** — comprehensive approach (collateral haircuts) or simple approach (collateral-substitution risk weight); eligible collateral per the CRE22 list.
- **Guarantees and credit derivatives** — risk-weight substitution to the protection provider for the covered portion (eligible-guarantor list; the uncovered portion retains the obligor's weight).
- **On-balance-sheet netting** and **master-netting agreements** — enforceable ISDA / GMRA netting reduces counterparty exposure (carried on `ORG-PR-16`; the bank's clause-library / ISDA-GMRA legal-entity work is Imani's live load-bearing slice).
- **Large-exposures CRM interaction** — where CRM reduces exposure to the original counterparty, the bank must **recognise an exposure to the CRM provider** for large-exposures purposes (Reg 24(6)(d)(v); SA gross-value approach — see `large-exposures.md`).

### 3. Off-balance-sheet credit-conversion factors (CCFs)

Off-balance-sheet items (undrawn commitments, trade-finance, guarantees, note-issuance facilities) are converted to a credit-equivalent EAD by a prescribed **credit-conversion factor (CCF)** before risk-weighting (BCBS CRE20 CCF table; Reg 23). The `rwa-engine.ts` consumes EAD already net of CCF at v0.1 (*"the engine does not compute CCFs at v0.1 (that's a CRE52 / CCF-table follow-on)"*) — the CCF table is a credit-RWA-engine substrate gap.

Recorded as a substrate gap rather than a guessed table (Principle 2).

### 4. Counterparty credit risk (CCR) — SA-CCR

For derivative and securities-financing exposures, the EAD is measured under the **Standardised Approach for Counterparty Credit Risk (SA-CCR)** — BCBS CRE51–CRE52 (`urn:reg:bcbs:cre:22.67`), transposed via **Directive D7/2020** (carried on `ORG-PR-SACCR-001..010`):

```
EAD  =  α × ( RC + PFE )        with  α = 1.4                 [CRE52 §52.5; BCBS d317 §10]
PFE  =  multiplier × Σ AddOn    (over-collateralisation shrinks the multiplier to a 5% floor)
```

This is **built** (`prototype/platform/risk/sa-ccr/`) and is the live measurement path for the markets desks' derivative counterparty exposure (FX forwards/swaps, OTC IRD). CCR is also reported on the adjacent **BA 210** return (D5/2025 §2.1.9, `ORG-PR-RETURNS-008`); the SA-CCR EAD feeds both the BA 200 credit-RWA computation and the BA 210 CCR analysis.

## IFRS 9 credit impairments (the BA 200 impairment lines)

Reg 23(2)(b) requires the BA 200 to carry **credit impairments**. The bank recognises impairment under **IFRS 9 §5.5** — the expected-credit-loss (ECL) model with three stages:

| Stage | Trigger | ECL basis |
|---|---|---|
| **Stage 1** | Performing; no Significant Increase in Credit Risk (SICR) | **12-month ECL** |
| **Stage 2** | SICR but not credit-impaired | **lifetime ECL** |
| **Stage 3** | Credit-impaired (default) | **lifetime ECL** |

The hard ECL invariant (asserted by the BA 200 generator): `Σ(12-month ECL for stage-1) + Σ(lifetime ECL for stage-2/3) == Σ(allowance for credit loss)`. Derived ratios: **NPL ratio** = Stage-3 gross / total gross; **coverage ratio** = Stage-3 allowance / Stage-3 gross. Staging logic (quantitative SICR: days-past-due ≥ 30 / ≥ 90, PD migration; qualitative: restructured, default indicator) is set in [`Procedures/by-policy/ecl-staging-cycle.md`](../../../Procedures/by-policy/ecl-staging-cycle.md) (`PROC-RSK-ECL-02`) and the model-registry ECL suite (`model:ecl-staging-ifrs9-v1` … `model:ecl-engine-ifrs9-v1`). The restructured-exposure treatment is governed by SARB PA **Directive 7 of 2015** (`ORG-PR-61`).

## RWA computation and the BA 700 capital integration

The credit RWA computed under the SA (Σ EAD × RW) is summed with market RWA (Reg 28) and operational RWA (Reg 33 / OPE25) into **total RWA**, the denominator of the capital-adequacy ratios on the **BA 700** capital-adequacy and leverage return (Reg 38). The in-repo `rwa-engine.ts` computes `CreditRWA = Σ_exposures (EAD × RW) [CRE20]` and exposes it to the BA 100 / BA 700 capital-adequacy generators. The **output floor** (IRB capital ≥ 72.5% × SA-derived floor, phased 2025–2028 per `ORG-PR-31` / G3-2023) is a **no-op for this bank** — the bank is **on the SA**, so 72.5% × SA never binds against SA (`rwa-engine.ts`: *"the output-floor is no-op at v0.1 since engine IS the standardised approach"*).

## The bank's SA election (Reg 23(3); `D-CREDIT-RISK-CAPITAL-APPROACH-V1`)

Reg 23(3) gives the bank a **discretionary choice** of credit-risk measurement methodology: (a) the **standardised approach** (no Registrar pre-approval required); (b) the **IRB approach** (subject to prior written Registrar approval + conditions); or (c) a **combination** (also Registrar-approval-gated). The bank has elected the **standardised approach** per `D-CREDIT-RISK-CAPITAL-APPROACH-V1` (CEO-approved 2026-05-28). Consequences threaded through the substrate:

- Reg 23(3)(b)/(c) **IRB and combination** sub-frameworks → `NOT_APPLICABLE` (no Registrar approval sought).
- Reg 23(2)(c) **IRB expected-loss / impairment-vs-EL analysis** → `NOT_APPLICABLE` (this is the IRB-only BA 200 purpose; the bank reports IFRS 9 ECL, not IRB regulatory EL).
- **D8/2023 IRB-specific thresholds** (AIRB corporate cut-off > R15bn; AVC ≥ R1.2tn; FIRB effective maturity) → `NOT_APPLICABLE` (`ORG-PR-46` scope note); the **SA-relevant** thresholds (retail ≤ R12.5m; QRRE ≤ R1.5m; corporate SME) bind.
- **G3-2023 "Revised IRB approaches for credit risk = 1 Jul 2025"** implementation-date component → `NOT_APPLICABLE` (`ORG-PR-31` scope note); the **"Revised SA for credit risk = 1 Jul 2025"** component binds.
- **Output floor** → latent / no-op (binds only on an IRB election; SA never floors against SA).

This SA election is the build-phase posture; an IRB election would require Registrar pre-approval (Reg 23(3)(b)), would re-activate the IRB obligation rows and D8/2023 IRB thresholds, and would bring the output floor live.

## The large-exposures interaction (BA 200-series carries LEX)
