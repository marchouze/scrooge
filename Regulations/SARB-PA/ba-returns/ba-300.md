## Purpose of BA 300

Form BA 300 is the prudential return through which the bank reports its **operational-risk capital requirement** to the Prudential Authority. Operational risk — the risk of loss from inadequate or failed internal processes, people and systems, or from external events (including legal risk; excluding strategic and reputational risk) — is one of the **three Pillar-1 risks** the bank must hold capital against. Reg 33(2) sets the return's two purposes verbatim (see Citation): a **reconciliation** between gross operating income reported on form BA 120 and the gross income used to compute the operational-risk capital, and the **calculation** of the operational-risk capital requirement itself.

The headline capital identity (Reg 33(7) BIA; BCBS D196 §645–§654 → BCBS OPE25 SMA at the Basel level):

```
BIA  operational-risk capital  =  α × ( Σ_{y: GI_y > 0} GI_y ) / nPositiveYears   with α = 15%   [Reg 33(7); BCBS D196 §649]
Operational-risk RWA           =  12.5 × operational-risk capital                                  [Reg 38; 1 / 8% = 12.5]
```

where **GI** (gross income) is the three-year average of *positive-only* annual gross income (net interest income + net non-interest income, before deductions, per the BCBS BIA definition), and the **12.5 multiplier** converts the capital charge to a risk-weighted-exposure equivalent (the reciprocal of the 8% minimum so the charge re-enters the capital ratio cleanly). The operational RWA is summed with credit RWA (Reg 23) and market RWA (Reg 28/29) into **total RWA**, the denominator of every capital ratio on **BA 100** (Reg 38).

**The bank's actual operational risk today.** In the build phase the bank holds **no real capital** and has **no audited gross-income history** — the BIA average requires three audited fiscal years of positive gross income (Reg 33(7)), which the bank will not have until post-licence-day. Until then the BA 300 generator runs **rehearsal-grade** against synthetic gross-income fixtures, and the operational-RWA contribution to the BA 100 total-RWA denominator is **zeroed** (`rwa-from-positions.ts`: *"BusinessIndicatorInput is zeroed — no income events exist yet"*). The bank is a **small trading bank** whose operational-risk profile is dominated by the **global-markets desks' processing risk** (trade-booking / settlement / valuation errors), **technology / agent-runtime risk** (the autonomous-by-default operating model — model drift, prompt injection, autonomous decision errors, data-lineage failures, harness failures; Principle 6), and **third-party / cloud-concentration risk** — exactly the risk categories the RCSA cycle ([`rcsa-cycle.md`](../../../Procedures/by-policy/rcsa-cycle.md)) mandates as a named assessment group.

## The Basic Indicator Approach (BIA) — the bank's build-phase basis

The BIA is the bank's regulatory capital basis for operational risk (Reg 33(3)(a) + (7)). It is the simplest of the three Reg 33 methodologies and requires no Registrar pre-approval. It computes operational-risk capital as a fixed fraction (**α = 15%**) of the bank's three-year average annual gross income, counting **only positive years** in the average.

### 1. Gross income and the positive-year average (Reg 33(7); BCBS D196 §649–§650)

| Step | Rule | In-engine |
|---|---|---|
| Gather gross income per fiscal year | GI = net interest income + net non-interest income, before any provisions / operating-expense deductions and excluding realised gains/losses on banking-book securities and extraordinary / irregular items (per the BCBS BIA definition). | caller-supplied `OpRiskGrossIncomeRow[]` per year. |
| Exclude non-positive years | Years with GI ≤ 0 are excluded from both the numerator and the year-count denominator. | `buildBia()` filters `gi > 0`. |
| Average over positive years | `averagePositive = ΣpositiveGI / nPositiveYears`. | `averagePositiveMinor`. |
| Apply α = 15% | `capital = 0.15 × averagePositive`. | `BIA_ALPHA = 0.15` (inline-cited). |

The build-phase generator surfaces a **`[citation: TBC]` placeholder** when fewer than three positive years are supplied (*"BIA average computed over N positive years (need 3 for steady-state); build-phase rehearsal acceptable"*) — an honest signal that the steady-state BIA precondition is not yet met, rather than a silent low-year average (Principle 2 / no-silent-zeros).

### 2. The Standardised Approach (TSA) — built, but NOT_APPLICABLE without Registrar approval

The TSA (Reg 33(3)(b) + (8)) decomposes gross income across **eight Basel business lines** — each weighted by a per-line β factor — and averages the floored annual sum over three years. The β schedule (BCBS D196 §654 / Reg 33(8) Annex; in `ba-300-op-risk.ts` `BUSINESS_LINE_BETA`):

| Business line | β | Business line | β |
|---|---|---|---|
| Corporate finance | **18%** | Commercial banking | **15%** |
| Trading and sales | **18%** | Agency services | **15%** |
| Payment and settlement | **18%** | Retail banking | **12%** |
| | | Retail brokerage | **12%** |
| | | Asset management | **12%** |

```
TSA  operational-risk capital  =  (1/3) × Σ_y max( 0 ,  Σ_i β_i × GI_{y,i} )      [Reg 33(8); BCBS D196 §654]
```

The TSA branch is **built and tested** in the engine but is **`NOT_APPLICABLE`** to the bank's reporting until (a) the bank seeks and obtains **prior written Registrar approval** (Reg 33(3)(b)), (b) the bank meets the **Reg 33(8) minimum qualifying criteria**, and (c) a standing `Decision` event records the election. Once on the TSA, the **Reg 33(4) no-revert rule** binds — the bank cannot fall back to the BIA without Registrar approval.

### 3. The Advanced Measurement Approach (AMA) — NOT_APPLICABLE

The AMA (Reg 33(3)(c) + (9)) — internal loss data, external data, scenario analysis, and business-environment / internal-control factors — is **`NOT_APPLICABLE`** (no Registrar approval sought; the bank has no internal-loss-data history). Not built; not in scope at the build phase.

## The Basel direction of travel — the Standardised Measurement Approach (SMA, OPE25)

At the **Basel level**, the BIA, TSA/ASA **and** AMA were all **retired** and replaced by a single **Standardised Measurement Approach (SMA, OPE25)** from 1 January 2023 (BCBS *Basel III: Finalising post-crisis reforms*, December 2017 / **d424**). The SMA construction (BCBS OPE25; `urn:reg:bcbs:ope:25.7`):

```
Business Indicator (BI)           =  Interest, Leases & Dividend component (ILDC) + Services Component (SC) + Financial Component (FC)
Business Indicator Component (BIC) =  Σ marginal-coefficient buckets applied to BI    [marginal coefficients 12% / 15% / 18%]
Internal Loss Multiplier (ILM)    =  ln( e − 1 + (Loss Component / BIC)^0.8 )          [LC = 15 × average annual loss over 10 yrs]
Operational-risk capital (SMA)    =  BIC × ILM
Operational-risk RWA              =  12.5 × SMA capital
```

The **15% middle-bucket marginal coefficient** is the SMA analogue of the old BIA α (per the OPE spine note). The **SMA loss component (ILM)** activates only once a typed `OperationalLossEvent` stream accrues **5+ years** of internal loss history post-licence (until then ILM = 1 by national discretion for smaller banks, leaving SMA capital = BIC). The **SARB transposition timing of the SMA into Reg 33** (i.e. when Reg 33 is amended to replace the BIA/TSA/AMA construction with the OPE25 BI/BIC/ILM construction) is the substrate / regulatory gap below; the bank engine therefore implements the **Reg 33 BIA/TSA construction** as the build-phase basis (a deliberate simplification of the Basel SMA baseline catalogued in `ope-operational-risk.md`).

## The operational-risk management framework (Reg 39 / PSMOR) — the qualitative overlay

The BA 300 **capital** charge sits atop a **management** framework that is itself binding: the bank must operate a comprehensive operational-risk-management programme (Reg 39 + the BCBS Principles for the Sound Management of Operational Risk). The bank's primary bottom-up identification / assessment tool is the **Risk and Control Self-Assessment (RCSA) cycle** ([`Procedures/by-policy/rcsa-cycle.md`](../../../Procedures/by-policy/rcsa-cycle.md), `PROC-RISK-RCSA-01`) — an annual cycle (Q3 initiation → Q4 BRC approval) producing a rated residual-risk register that feeds the ICAAP operational-risk capital chapter. The RCSA explicitly mandates an **AI-agent operational-risk category** (model drift, prompt injection, autonomous decision errors, data-lineage failures, harness failures) as a named assessment group (Principle 6) — the operational-risk substrate that is most load-bearing for an autonomous AI-run bank. The management-framework obligations bind via `ORG-PR-17` (operational-risk framework), `ORG-PR-24` (documented framework per Reg 39 + PSMOR, annual review), and `ORG-PR-39` (the 12 PSMOR principles).

## RWA computation and the BA 100 capital integration

The operational-risk capital computed under the selected approach (BIA at the build phase) is converted to **operational RWA = 12.5 × capital** and summed with credit RWA (Reg 23 / BA 200) and market RWA (Reg 28/29 / BA 325) into **total RWA**, the denominator of the capital-adequacy ratios on **BA 100** (Reg 38). The in-repo `ba-300-op-risk.ts` computes `opRiskRwaMinor = 12.5 × selectedApproachCapital`; the `rwa-from-positions.ts` projection that aggregates total RWA for BA 100 currently **zeroes** the operational-RWA component (`BusinessIndicatorInput` zeroed — no income events) pending the GL trial-balance Business-Indicator / gross-income feed (Bea M2). The **output floor** (IRB ≥ 72.5% × SA) is **no-op for this bank** (the bank is on standardised approaches across credit / market / operational risk — consistent with [`ba-100.md`](ba-100.md) / [`ba-200.md`](ba-200.md) / [`ba-325.md`](ba-325.md)).
