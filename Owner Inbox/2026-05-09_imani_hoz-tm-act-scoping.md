## 1. Scope — TM classes counsel must search

The TM clearance must cover both the immediate trading use of the mark and the adjacent uses already foreseeable in the bank's operating model. Filing only Class 36 leaves the substrate, the training collateral, and any management-services exposure unprotected.

| Nice class | Heading | Why this class for Hoz |
|---|---|---|
| **36** (primary) | Insurance; financial affairs; monetary affairs; real estate affairs | Core class for the bank's trading services — banking, dealing, market-making, custody, payment services. Counsel's primary search and the registration-priority class. |
| **35** | Advertising; business management; business administration; office functions | Captures the bank's institutional-services / business-management surface (corporate-services arm, business-administration aspects of treasury client services). Defensive posture. |
| **9** | Computer software, electronic publications, downloadable applications | Captures the prototype substrate, any client-facing electronic platform, and downloadable application collateral. The bank operates digitally by default (Principle 3); the mark must be cleared on the substrate it runs on. |
| **41** | Education; providing of training; entertainment; sporting and cultural activities | Captures any future training collateral (institutional-counterparty training, market-making training programmes), conference / sponsorship use, and educational publications. Defensive posture; low marginal cost in the search pass. |

**Counsel may recommend extending to additional classes** (for example Class 16 for printed publications, Class 38 for telecommunications-as-channel) based on market practice. The list above is the minimum scope; extension is at counsel's recommendation.

## 2. Scope — foreign-jurisdiction set

The bank is SA single-branch (per `project_strategic_foundation.md`) and the trading services are SA-domiciled. The foreign-jurisdiction TM scope is therefore not a market-entry scope; it is a **defensive scope** to prevent foreign-domiciled counterparties or competitors from blocking the mark in markets where the bank either (a) trades cross-border with FX counterparties, (b) has a foreseeable institutional-counterparty pipeline, or (c) faces a generic defensive-registration risk in a major financial market.

| Jurisdiction | Why included |
|---|---|
| **South Africa** | Primary; bank is SA-domiciled and SA-trading. |
| **United Kingdom** | Major institutional-counterparty market for SA banks; defensive against UK-based fintech / banking counterparties. UKIPO. |
| **European Union (EUTM)** | Single registration covering all EU member states. Defensive against EU-based counterparties; most cost-efficient route to broad EU coverage. EUIPO. |
| **United States** | Largest financial-services TM register globally; defensive against US fintech / bank registrations. **Critical for the v2 fallback `Lucet` — see § 4.** USPTO; US Class 102 maps to International Class 36. |
| **Australia** | Major institutional-counterparty market in Asia-Pacific; AUD is a key FX cross. IP Australia. |
| **Nigeria** | Largest African economy; the bank's institutional-counterparty pipeline plausibly includes NG-domiciled banks and DFIs. NIPO (Nigerian Industrial Property Office) under the Trade Marks Act, Cap. T13. |
| **Kenya** | Largest East African economy; institutional-counterparty pipeline for cross-border FX. KIPI (Kenya Industrial Property Institute) under Trade Marks Act, Cap. 506. |
| **Egypt** | Largest North African economy; institutional-counterparty pipeline for FX with the broader MEA region. EGYPO (Egyptian Patent Office) under Law 82 of 2002. |

**Madrid Protocol option.** Counsel may recommend filing via the Madrid Protocol (WIPO) for the foreign jurisdictions in one designation rather than separate national filings. South Africa is **not** a member of the Madrid Protocol [citation: TBC — confirm with counsel] which constrains the design of the international filing route. Counsel decides the optimal filing-route mix; this brief specifies the *coverage*, not the *route*.

## 3. Scope — search pattern (counsel-executed)

Counsel performs both an exact-form search and a near-similar-mark search. Three-letter marks elevate the near-similar surface; counsel scopes:

- **Exact form.** "Hoz" as a word mark.
- **Phonetic neighbours.** "Hos" / "Hoss" / "Hoze" / "Hoes" / similar phonetic forms in the relevant alphabets.
- **Visual neighbours.** Same character-length forms with one-character substitution / insertion / deletion ("Boz" / "Hox" / "Hop" / "Hoy" / "Ho" / "Hozz" / etc.).
- **Compound forms.** "Hoz Bank" / "Hoz Capital" / "Hoz Markets" / "Hoz Treasury" / "Hoz Group" — counsel decides which compound forms to file in addition to the bare mark.
- **Defensive-portfolio scan.** Marks held by major SA banks, large foreign banks, fintech defensive portfolios — for "Hoz"-similar entries.

**Search depth:** registered marks + pending applications + opposed marks + recently-lapsed marks (where revival within the priority window is possible). [citation: TBC] for the SA-specific revival window under the Trade Marks Act 194 of 1993.

## 4. Self-administered preliminary check

**Public-domain awareness — flags for counsel's day-one scope:**

1. **Class 36 three-letter density.** The form `Hoz` is short enough to plausibly collide with abbreviations, ticker-style fintech marks, or defensive-portfolio registrations across Class 36 internationally. Counsel must search both the exact form and the near-similar set above.

2. **Lucet Health collision risk (v2 fallback) — explicitly scoped.** A US healthcare entity uses the mark `Lucet` (in healthcare-services classes — public-domain awareness only). Whether that mark blocks `Lucet` in Class 36 in the US is a concurrent-registrability question for counsel. Counsel **must check `Lucet` Class 36 in US + EU + SA before the v2 fallback can be relied upon**. This is the single most material flag on the fallback set, and the engagement letter should scope it explicitly so it is not raised mid-search.

3. **Noeta + Synaps — no public-domain collision flag identified by Imani**, but this is a dictionary-of-awareness check, not a TM-register search. Counsel must perform the authoritative search across the same class + jurisdiction set as for Hoz.

4. **`Hoz` near-mark of public-domain awareness.** "Hoz" is a Spanish word (meaning "sickle" / "scythe", or a place-name in Spain — public-domain awareness only). The TM relevance is whether any Spanish or EU-domiciled entity has registered "Hoz" or a `Hoz`-derivative as a TM in Class 36 or adjacent. Counsel scopes.
