# CISO Shortlist Brief — Hoz Bank

**Author:** Nolan (Recruiter, engineering team)
**Date:** 2026-05-12
**For:** Marc (CEO)
**Source brief:** `Team Inbox/actioned/2026-05-06_role-brief_chief-information-security-officer.md` (PAX, Role researcher)

> **Status note.** Under D-THIN-HUMAN-LAYER-MINIMUM (CEO approved 2026-05-08), the licence-day thin-human-layer roster covers six mandated seats — Independent Chair, Human CRO, NED #2, NED #3, Compliance Lead, and Company Secretary. The CISO seat is not in that six. The CISO function is currently fronted by the Rashida (Chief Information Security Officer) agent persona, with Senna (Security engineer) carrying the engineering seam. The original 2026-05-06 shortlist and hire-confirmation files were superseded by D-THIN-HUMAN-LAYER-MINIMUM on 2026-05-11 (Owen sweep). This brief is produced at CEO direction as a **pre-search preparation deliverable**: a ready-to-execute shortlist for the moment a decision to appoint a human CISO is made — whether that is before licence-day (if the bank judges the regulatory accountability risk warrants it), at licence-day, or post-licence. The search parameters and candidate profiles are constructed from the PAX role brief, the original shortlist research, and the candidate archetypes consistent with the SA market.

---

## 1. Role recap

The Chief Information Security Officer (CISO) at Hoz Bank is the named second-line cyber-and-information-security governance officer: the human whose name appears on Joint Standard 1 of 2024 (PA / FSCA Joint Standard on Cybersecurity and Cyber Resilience) programme filings, SARB / PA cyber-incident notification letters, POPIA s.19 operational-security governance, and Board / Risk Committee cyber-resilience reporting. What makes this search unusual is the intersection of three features that rarely co-exist in a single candidate pool. First, the bank is AI-driven: Rashida (the agent persona) already owns the InfoSec / Cyber Resilience / IR policies, runs the threat-model gate, governs the HSM key-rotation cycle, and produces the continuous-controls evidence Vera (Internal audit / continuous-assurance engineer) consumes — the human CISO arrives into a running, coded-substrate security function, not a blank page, and must be fluent enough in event-sourced, projection-based assurance to govern it rather than redirect it toward a conventional GRC-tooled model. Second, there is no traditional IT estate: no on-premises data centre, no legacy network perimeter, no managed-service SOC; the entire security surface is cloud-native (Azure-primary, per the M8 target), fully coded, FIPS 140-2/3 Level 3 HSM-managed, and governed via typed events — a profile that disqualifies the "IT-ops CISO with a Joint Standard 1 of 2024 attestation layer on top" archetype. Third, the SA regulatory context is binding from day one: PA fit-and-proper designation, Joint Standard 1 of 2024 named-responsibility under the bank's own programme, POPIA s.19–22 partnered accountability with Iris (Information Officer, governance), and the Banks Act 94 of 1990 operational-risk / cyber-risk framework — not aspirational compliance targets, but regulatory obligations the CISO owns personally at licence-day. The CISO reports to the CEO (Marc) administratively and functionally, with a direct report in Senna (Security engineer), co-ownership of the operational-resilience programme with Devon (Chief Operating Officer, governance), and a second-line peer relationship with Helena (Chief Risk Officer, governance).

## 2. Search parameters

### Seniority target

The appropriate seniority for this search is **CISO, Deputy CISO stepping up, or Head of Cyber Resilience** at a SA Tier-2 bank or regulated SA financial-services entity — not a group-level CISO at a Tier-1 bank (over-structured for the bank's current size and composition; Ceiling cost risk is real) and not a pure-GRC Head of Information Security without engineering depth (wrong archetype for a coded-substrate security function). The candidate must hold, or be able to hold on appointment, PA fit-and-proper designation and be prepared to be the named officer on Joint Standard 1 of 2024 filings from day one. A candidate who has been a deputy or Head of Cyber Resilience at a well-regulated institution and has personally engaged with SARB / PA on a reportable cyber-incident is in scope; a candidate who has only managed third-party GRC consultants is not.

### SA regulatory requirement

The CISO personally holds the following obligations from appointment:

- **Joint Standard 1 of 2024 (PA / FSCA Joint Standard on Cybersecurity and Cyber Resilience)** — named responsible person under the bank's programme. Reporting cadence to the Board / Risk Forum (Helena chair, interim); coverage governance and gap-tracking under the standard; PA / FSCA engagement on incident reporting and programme review.
- **POPIA ss.19–22** — operational security safeguards in partnership with Iris (Information Officer, governance). The CISO governs the security-measure implementation that ss.19–22 require; Iris holds the s.56 IO designation; the two seats co-own the POPIA breach-notification workflow (Procedures/).
- **Banks Act 94 of 1990 — operational and cyber risk implementation** — second-line security function head under the bank's operational-risk framework (Helena is the functional risk owner; the CISO supplies the cyber-risk seam).
- **BCBS Principles for the Sound Management of Operational Risk** — cyber risk is a named sub-category; the CISO's programme must be coherent with Helena's ICAAP/ILAAP governance.
- **King IV Code — IT governance principle** — the CISO is the officer the Board / Risk Forum looks to for IT governance assurance on cyber specifically; King IV §12 (technology and information governance) is the CISO's primary Board-reporting principle.
- **PA fit-and-proper** — the CISO is a designated person under regulator engagement; formal fit-and-proper assessment is required at appointment and on an ongoing basis.

### Remote vs on-site posture

**Build phase (current to licence-day):** Predominantly remote. The bank has no physical trading floor, no branch network, no data centre. The build-phase work — programme governance, cyber-resilience scenario rehearsal, threat-model gate, pre-licence security readiness gate — is conducted against a synthetic-flow, simulation-mode substrate. In-person interaction with Saskia (Head of Global Markets, governance), Helena, Owen (Company Secretary, governance), and Vera (Internal audit / continuous-assurance engineer) will occur at Board and committee-level meetings and at scenario-rehearsal events; these are expected to be quarterly or less frequent in the build phase.

**Licence-day and beyond:** On-site posture increases as the bank stands up live trading, live client-facing surfaces, and the full operational-resilience posture. By commencement-of-trading the CISO should be available for incident-command on short notice and for in-person regulator meetings with SARB / PA. A SA-resident candidate is required; offshore-based candidates are not viable.

### Compensation band

The CISO seat is **not** within the six seats covered by the D-COMP-FRAMEWORK-SIX-SEATS approved compensation framework (which covers: Independent Chair, Human CRO, Compliance Lead, Company Secretary, NED #2, NED #3). ## 3. Candidate profiles — three shortlist archetypes

The bank has not yet run a formal external search process for the human CISO seat (consistent with build-phase posture and D-THIN-HUMAN-LAYER-MINIMUM). The three profiles below are notional archetypes constructed from the SA candidate market for this role; they represent distinct archetypes the bank should consider when the search opens. These are not profiles of real named individuals.

---

### Profile A — The SA Bank Incumbent

**Background summary.** Currently Deputy CISO or acting CISO at a top-five SA bank (CIB or corporate-and-retail division). Five to eight years in a named cyber-governance role at a SA-regulated institution, including at least one PA / FSCA-engaged cyber-incident notification. CISSP, CISM, or CCSP. Has authored or led a Joint Standard 1 of 2024 implementation programme. Azure or AWS primary cloud security experience; FIPS 140-2/3 Level 3 HSM operations observed. Deep familiarity with the Banks Act 94 of 1990 operational-risk framework, King IV IT governance reporting, and the BCBS operational-risk standards; engaged with an Information Officer on POPIA ss.19–22.

**Key strengths for this role.** The SA regulatory fluency is the strongest of the three archetypes: has personally engaged with SARB / PA on incident reporting; knows Joint Standard 1 of 2024 from the implementation side, not just the compliance side; the PA fit-and-proper designation is low-friction; existing relationships with SARB / PA contacts reduce the regulator-engagement ramp on arrival. The POPIA partnered-officer relationship with an Information Officer is established practice, not a new obligation. Would arrive capable of signing the first Joint Standard 1 of 2024 reporting pack on day one.

**Key gaps.** Less experienced with AI-native infrastructure: a top-five SA bank's security function is built on conventional SIEM/EDR/XDR tooling, on-premises data centres (or hybrid), and managed-service partners — not a fully coded, event-sourced, projection-based substrate. There is a real ramp time required to govern Senna's build without redirecting it toward a conventional model. Cloud-native security at execution level (Azure IAM, network policy, KMS/HSM as code, confidential computing) may be observed rather than hands-on; the distinction matters when Senna's seam is the engineering layer the CISO oversees. May carry top-five-bank overhead expectations (team size, tooling budget, external vendor relationships) that don't fit the bank's lean build posture.

**Nolan's recommendation: Conditional.** Strong on the regulatory axis — the highest-confidence candidate for the PA fit-and-proper and Joint Standard 1 of 2024 accountability seam. Conditional on a clear-eyed conversation about the AI-native substrate and a demonstrated willingness to govern a coded-evidence pipeline rather than build a conventional one. Interview structure should probe this specifically; do not assume the regulatory fluency compensates for the substrate gap.

---

### Profile B — The Cloud-Native Security Architect

**Background summary.** Currently Head of Security Architecture or CISO at a SA or regional fintech, cloud-native financial-services firm, or cloud-native payment infrastructure. Strong on zero-trust design, cloud-native IAM, confidential computing, FIPS 140-2/3 Level 3 HSM operations, and secure SDLC discipline (SLSA, sigstore, SBOM governance). Has governed a detection-engineering capability built on coded pipelines rather than managed-service SOC. May have CISSP or CCSP; likely holds Azure, AWS, or GCP security certifications. Less experience with SA banking-specific regulation: has not personally been the named officer on a Joint Standard 1 of 2024 programme; POPIA obligations were enterprise-data-protection rather than named-IO-partner-level; may have limited PA / FSCA engagement history.

**Key strengths for this role.** The strongest fit for Senna's substrate: speaks the same language as the bank's coded security function; can govern an event-sourced, projection-based assurance pipeline without redirecting it; zero-trust and confidential-computing depth is directly applicable to the Azure M8 migration; SLSA-aligned supply-chain governance is leading-edge. The build-phase programme of work — threat-model gate, cyber-resilience scenario design, pre-licence security readiness gate — is natural territory. Would arrive accelerating the technical programme immediately.

**Key gaps.** The regulatory accountability seam is the binding constraint. Joint Standard 1 of 2024 named-officer responsibility, PA fit-and-proper designation, Banks Act operational-risk CISO framing, and the SARB / PA cyber-incident reporting relationship are new obligations, not refinements of existing practice. The bank cannot afford a CISO who is visibly in a learning curve with the regulator in the first 12 months of operation. A formal regulatory-onboarding plan is required: Mira (Compliance / RegTech engineer) and Zara (Chief Compliance Officer, governance) as regulatory-substrate coaches; Helena as risk-framework peer; a structured first Joint Standard 1 of 2024 programme-map review with the full governance team before any PA engagement. POPIA partnership with Iris requires a standing working arrangement, not a one-off orientation.

**Nolan's recommendation: Conditional.** Strong shortlist candidate if the bank is comfortable with a structured 90-day regulatory-onboarding plan and has the governance depth (Helena, Zara, Mira) to carry the candidate through it. Not viable if the PA / FSCA expects immediate regulator-level fluency on appointment — which is a question Mira should answer before the search opens.

---

### Profile C — The Regulatory Technologist

**Background summary.** Former FSCA or PA examiner or prudential-supervision analyst, subsequently moved into a security leadership role at a SA licensed financial institution. Three to five years at a regulator (with a cyber / IT supervision remit), followed by three to six years as CISO, Head of Cyber Risk, or Head of Information Security at a SA-licensed bank, insurer, or payments scheme. Deep understanding of how regulators read Joint Standard 1 of 2024 programmes, what PA / FSCA inspectors look for in a cyber-resilience programme, and how to write incident-notification letters that reduce rather than amplify regulator concern. CISSP or CISM likely; cloud-security certifications developing.

**Key strengths for this role.** Uniquely credible with PA in inspection and in incident-response: knows the standard from the enforcement perspective, not just the compliance perspective; can write PA-ready programme filings, incident reports, and risk-forum reports from first principles. The regulator-engagement seam — which is the highest-stakes CISO surface at licence-day — is this archetype's strongest suit. The SARB / PA relationship is warm by construction; the PA fit-and-proper designation is the smoothest of the three archetypes. May bring insight into FSCA / conduct-regulation overlay on the cyber resilience programme that the other two archetypes do not.

**Key gaps.** AI-security knowledge is developing rather than deep: the regulatory-technologist archetype typically has governance and inspection experience rather than hands-on technical security architecture. Would lean on Senna's technical judgment more heavily than Profile A or Profile B; the risk is a governance posture that defers to engineering rather than challenging it. Cloud-native security architecture, FIPS 140-2/3 Level 3 HSM governance, and coded-substrate assurance will require material ramp time. The trading-floor and market-infrastructure cyber surface (Saskia's seam — dealer-mandate authorisation, surveillance-pipeline integrity, JSE / SAMOS / SWIFT connectivity hardening) is likely outside prior experience.

**Nolan's recommendation: Stretch.** Appropriate only if the bank weights regulator-credibility above all else at licence-day and is willing to invest in a structured technical-onboarding programme (Senna as technical mentor; Atlas (Core banking platform architect) on the event-sourced security-substrate; Devon on operational-resilience programme co-ownership). Not the primary archetype to lead with, but a valuable second-shortlist or interview-leverage candidate — the PA-facing credibility of this archetype improves the bank's regulatory posture at exactly the moment it matters most.

## 4. Recommended next steps

### Interview process

**Stage 1 — Scrooge-coordinated panel (all archetypes):** Marc (CEO) + Helena (Chief Risk Officer, governance) + Rashida (Chief Information Security Officer, agent). Marc assesses CEO-level operating fit and governance philosophy; Helena assesses second-line peer relationship, risk-framework fluency, and ICAAP/ILAAP interface; Rashida assesses technical depth, substrate orientation, and whether the candidate can govern the coded security function without redirecting it. Stage 1 should include a structured scenario: describe a material cyber incident at the bank (simulated credential-compromise during a synthetic-flow scenario run); how does the candidate command the response and manage the PA notification path?

**Stage 2 — Regulatory and partnered-officer panel:** Iris (Information Officer, governance) + Zara (Chief Compliance Officer, governance) + Owen (Company Secretary, governance). Iris assesses POPIA ss.19–22 partnered-officer relationship readiness; Zara assesses RMCP / FIC / AML/CFT interface fit; Owen assesses Board / committee-governance credibility and fit-and-proper pre-check readiness.

**Stage 3 — Technical depth panel (Profile B candidates primarily; all others as required):** Senna (Security engineer) + Atlas (Core banking platform architect). Tests that the candidate can govern Senna's seam without redirecting it; assesses cloud-native security architecture depth; probes FIPS 140-2/3 Level 3 HSM key-governance understanding.

**Stage 4 — CEO closing conversation:** Marc (CEO) only. Compensation, start date, onboarding plan, and fit-and-proper pre-check process.

### Reference-check requirements

For a CISO at a SARB-licensed bank, the reference-check set is substantive and carries PA fit-and-proper implications. Required checks:

1. **Two professional references** from prior direct-line managers at regulated financial institutions (SA preferred). References must confirm: named-officer accountability, regulator engagement history, incident-command experience, and that no PA / FSCA / information-regulator fit-and-proper red flags exist.
2. **One character reference** from a peer in the SA cyber-governance community (e.g. ISACA SA, (ISC)² SA chapter contact, or equivalent) — not a subordinate.
3. **PA fit-and-proper pre-check:** Mira (Compliance / RegTech engineer) conducts a preliminary PA fit-and-proper screen (prior regulatory proceedings; prior disqualification events; directorship history; insolvency records) before an offer is tabled. The formal PA designation is a post-appointment process, but a clean pre-check is a precondition for offer.
4. **Criminal record and credit check** — standard regulated-institution onboarding requirement under Banks Act 94 of 1990 fit-and-proper guidance; Sade (HR systems engineer / AgentOps) carries the substrate for this once the AgentOps / HR systems lane is activated.

### Target timeline

In agent cadence: **next 2 scheduled recruitment runs** to finalise search parameters and open the external market (contingent on: CEO decision to open the search; Camille compensation-band confirmation; Mira regulatory-onboarding-plan assessment). First-round panels within the subsequent 1–2 runs; offer-stage within 2–3 runs of shortlist commitment.

### Onboarding milestones

The human CISO's first-90-days plan follows the PAX role brief §10 success criteria exactly, with the following sequencing:

1. **Days 1–14:** InfoSec / Cyber Resilience / IR policies re-baselined under human CISO sign-off (Rashida's agent-persona outputs are the inherited baseline; the human CISO re-asserts them). POPIA ss.19–22 partnered cadence with Iris established.
2. **Days 15–45:** Joint Standard 1 of 2024 programme map reviewed and presented to the Risk Forum (Helena chair). Threat-modelling gate re-baselined under human CISO sign-off; Senna's existing process re-asserted through the new governance seat.
3. **Days 45–90:** Cyber-resilience scenario test plan for the build phase completed; combined-assurance interface with Thandiwe (Chief Audit Executive, governance) and Vera established; customer-facing security standards document prepared for the post-licence surface; pre-licence security-readiness gate documented (co-owned with Saskia and Devon).
4. **At licence-day:** PA fit-and-proper designation confirmed; Joint Standard 1 of 2024 reporting cadence live; SARB / PA named-contact designation registered.

## 5. Nolan's recommendation

**Lead with Profile A (SA Bank Incumbent) as the primary shortlist archetype, with Profile B (Cloud-Native Security Architect) as a parallel interview-leverage candidate.**

The binding constraint at this bank is the regulatory accountability seam: the human CISO is the named officer on Joint Standard 1 of 2024 filings and PA / FSCA cyber-incident notifications from appointment. A candidate who is visibly learning that obligation in their first 12 months is a licence-application and inspection risk. Profile A's regulatory fluency is the right anchor. The conditional element — governance-over-a-coded-substrate — is a teachable orientation, not a structural disqualifier, provided the Stage 1 panel probes it directly and the candidate articulates a genuine interest in governing Senna's seam rather than replacing it.

Profile B is a legitimate shortlist candidate and should be interviewed in parallel to maintain market optionality and to test whether the regulatory-onboarding plan Mira would need to design is viable. If Mira's assessment is that the PA / FSCA will not accept a CISO who is new to SA banking regulation in the first year of licence operation, Profile B falls to post-licence consideration.

Profile C is a second-shortlist / interview-leverage candidate only — not a primary route. Use if neither Profile A nor Profile B yields a committable candidate.

**Open question requiring CEO input before search opens.** The CISO seat is not currently in the thin-human-layer six mandated by D-THIN-HUMAN-LAYER-MINIMUM. Before the search opens, the CEO should confirm: (a) is the CISO human-hire triggered before licence-day (if the bank judges the Joint Standard 1 of 2024 named-officer risk warrants a human in seat before the six-seat composition is fully assembled), at licence-day alongside the six, or post-licence as the first additional hire? and (b) should Camille produce a formal compensation-band confirmation for the CISO seat to sit alongside D-COMP-FRAMEWORK-SIX-SEATS, or does the CEO authorise the indicative Floor / Mid / Ceiling in §2 as the working band? Until these two questions are answered, the search is in preparation-mode, not active-mode.
