## 1. Treasury Investment Policy — Overarching

**Owner:** Eitan (Treasurer, governance) · **Approval:** Board (CEO interim per `D-THIN-HUMAN-LAYER-MINIMUM`) for policy; ALCO for rebalancing within approved parameters · **Cadence:** Annual policy review; monthly rebalancing review at ALCO; daily monitoring by Ravi · **Citation:** Basel III LCR (January 2013) + Regulations Relating to Banks reg.26 + SARB PGN001 + Exchange Control Regulations

### Purpose

This policy governs the management of Hoz Bank Limited's Treasury investment portfolio, which serves as the primary liquidity buffer (HQLA portfolio) required under the Basel III LCR framework and reg.26. Its purpose is to ensure that: (i) the liquidity buffer is composed exclusively of eligible HQLA instruments meeting the Basel III LCR and SARB PGN001 eligibility criteria; (ii) the investment universe is restricted to instruments appropriate for a regulatory liquidity buffer (not a return-maximising portfolio); (iii) credit quality, concentration, and duration constraints are maintained consistently; (iv) FX constraints under Exchange Control Regulations are observed; and (v) the portfolio can be monetised under stress conditions with minimal haircut.

The Treasury investment portfolio is not a trading book. It is a structural balance sheet position managed for liquidity purposes. Returns generated by the portfolio are incidental; the primary objective is regulatory eligibility, monetisability, and capital efficiency. The portfolio is managed by Eitan and Ravi, separately from the trading book managed by Saskia (Head of Global Markets, governance).

### Principles

- **Regulatory eligibility first.** No instrument enters the Treasury investment portfolio unless it meets the HQLA eligibility criteria under Basel III LCR and SARB PGN001. Return optimisation within the eligible universe is a secondary objective; it never overrides eligibility.
- **ZAR-denominated primary buffer.** The primary liquidity buffer is ZAR-denominated. Hard-currency instruments may be held in the investment portfolio only within the FX limits imposed by the Exchange Control Regulations (see §5). FX instruments in the portfolio are not held speculatively; any FX position from portfolio instruments is hedged to ZAR unless an FX open position is separately authorised by Eitan under the trading mandate.
- **No prohibited instruments.** Instruments on the prohibited list (§4) may not be held in the Treasury investment portfolio under any circumstances. A `TreasuryPortfolioProhibitedInstrumentFlag { instrumentId, reason }` event is emitted if a prohibited instrument is identified in the portfolio; Eitan reviews within 1 business day.
- **Securities lending restrictions.** Securities from the HQLA portfolio may be lent only with prior ALCO approval and subject to the restrictions in §7 of this policy. Uninstructed lending of HQLA assets is prohibited; it reduces the LCR liquidity buffer below the calculated level.
- **Events-first portfolio management.** Every portfolio transaction (purchase, sale, maturity, coupon receipt, repo of portfolio assets) is a typed event in the event log. Portfolio composition is a query over those events, not a separately maintained register.

### Roles

Eitan (Treasurer, governance) is the policy owner and the decision-maker for portfolio investment and rebalancing decisions. Ravi (Treasury/ALM engineer, engineering — reports to Eitan) builds and operates the portfolio management system, HQLA eligibility monitoring, and LCR contribution calculation. Helena (Chief Risk Officer, governance) provides independent review of the portfolio's credit quality and concentration exposures. Camille (Chief Financial Officer, governance) integrates the portfolio's IFRS classification (AC or FVOCI, per `Policies/financial-reporting-policy-v1.md`) and fair value measurements. Owen (Company Secretary, governance) manages the Exchange Control reporting for any hard-currency instruments. Vera (internal audit engineer) audits the portfolio's compliance with this policy annually.

## 2. HQLA Eligibility Framework

**Owner:** Eitan (Treasurer, governance) · **Approval:** Eitan (instrument-level eligibility); ALCO (category-level eligibility changes) · **Cadence:** Eligibility reviewed on instrument purchase and monthly for the portfolio · **Citation:** Basel III LCR (January 2013) — HQLA definition and criteria; SARB PGN001 (liquid assets for LCR purposes in South Africa)

### 2.1 Level 1 HQLA (No haircut in LCR)

Level 1 HQLA instruments are the highest-quality liquid assets under the Basel III LCR standard. They receive 0% haircut in the LCR calculation and have no upper cap on inclusion. The Bank's eligible Level 1 HQLA instruments are:

| Instrument | Eligibility condition |
|---|---|
| South African Government Bonds (SAGBs) and Treasury Bills | Zero risk-weight under Basel III (SA sovereign in ZAR; rated by ECAI); eligible per SARB PGN001 |
| SARB debentures and SARB notes | Issued by the South African Reserve Bank; zero credit risk |
| Coins and banknotes held at the Bank | Physical cash; 100% Level 1 |
| Reserves held at the SARB (cash reserve account) | Central bank reserves; 100% Level 1 |

The Bank's primary HQLA portfolio is expected to consist predominantly of SAGBs and SARB instruments. All Level 1 instruments must be unencumbered (not pledged as collateral, not subject to a lien or lock-up that prevents the Bank from monetising them within 30 days).

### 2.2 Level 2A HQLA (15% haircut in LCR; ≤ 40% of HQLA buffer)

Level 2A instruments may comprise up to 40% of the HQLA buffer (after haircuts). The Bank's eligible Level 2A HQLA instruments are:

| Instrument | Eligibility condition |
|---|---|
| SA Government-guaranteed bonds (DBSA, Eskom guaranteed bonds — if rated AA- or higher) | Issued by a public-sector entity; government-guaranteed; 20% risk weight under Basel III; must meet SARB PGN001 conditions |
| SARB-eligible covered bonds | Rated AA- or higher; not issued by the Bank itself; SARB PGN001 eligible |

### 2.3 Level 2B HQLA (25–50% haircut; ≤ 15% of HQLA buffer)

Level 2B instruments may comprise up to 15% of the HQLA buffer (after haircuts), subject to ALCO approval for any Level 2B holdings. The Bank does not intend to hold Level 2B HQLA at commencement of trading; any future Level 2B holdings require a formal ALCO decision. Level 2B instruments include certain qualifying RMBS, certain qualifying equities, and certain qualifying corporate bonds — none of which are in the Bank's initial investment universe.

## 3. Investment Universe

**Owner:** Eitan (Treasurer, governance) · **Approval:** ALCO for any expansion of the investment universe beyond the initial approved list · **Cadence:** Reviewed annually; expanded only on ALCO approval with Helena's credit risk sign-off · **Citation:** Basel III LCR (January 2013); SARB PGN001; Exchange Control Regulations

### Approved Instruments

The Treasury investment portfolio may hold only instruments from the following approved list:

1. **South African Government Bonds (SAGBs)** — fixed-rate and inflation-linked; all maturities on the SARB benchmark curve; JSE-listed; STRATE-settled.
2. **South African Treasury Bills (T-Bills)** — issued by National Treasury; tenors from 91 days to 364 days.
3. **SARB debentures and SARB bills** — issued by the SARB directly.
4. **SARB reserves account** — cash held in the cash reserve account at the SARB (counts as Level 1 HQLA).
5. **SA Government-guaranteed bonds** — DBSA, Eskom government-guaranteed series; rated AA- or higher by an approved ECAI; Level 2A treatment.
6. **Approved covered bonds** — SARB PGN001-eligible covered bonds meeting all Level 2A criteria; subject to issuer limits in §6.

Any instrument not on this list requires ALCO approval with Helena's credit quality assessment before inclusion. A `TreasuryInvestmentUniverseExpanded { instrumentType, alcoApprovalRef, helenaCreditAssessmentRef, effectiveDate }` event is required before the first purchase of a new instrument type.

## 4. Prohibited Instruments

**Owner:** Eitan (Treasurer, governance) · **Citation:** Basel III LCR (January 2013); Banks Act 94 of 1990

The following instruments are explicitly prohibited from the Treasury investment portfolio:

- **Corporate bonds (non-government-guaranteed):** eligible as Level 2B in the Basel III LCR but not permitted in the Bank's conservative investment policy; introduces issuer credit risk inconsistent with a liquidity buffer.
- **Equities (any class):** not eligible as Level 1 or Level 2A HQLA under the Basel III LCR for the Bank's mandate; prohibited from the investment portfolio (equities in the trading book are managed separately per `Policies/trading-mandate-v1.md`).
- **Collective investment schemes / ETFs:** indirect exposure to underlying instruments; eligibility depends on look-through; too complex for a conservative liquidity buffer.
- **Instruments issued by the Bank itself:** self-held instruments do not constitute eligible HQLA under the LCR; prohibited.
- **Instruments with remaining maturity < 30 days from eligible HQLA analysis** but with settlement conditions that prevent monetisation within 30 days.
- **Instruments subject to encumbrance** (pledged as collateral, subject to a repo agreement as the repo seller) unless the encumbrance is within the securities lending framework (§7).

## 5. FX Constraint

**Owner:** Eitan (Treasurer, governance) + Owen (Company Secretary, governance) for Exchange Control compliance · **Approval:** ALCO + Eitan for FX portfolio positions; Exchange Control (SARB Forex Department) for any hard-currency asset holdings · **Citation:** Exchange Control Regulations (Currency and Exchanges Act 9 of 1933) — foreign asset acquisition by residents; Exchange Control Circular D/SARB/13 (foreign assets)

The Bank's liquidity buffer must be ZAR-denominated as the primary composition. Hard-currency instruments may be held in the investment portfolio only if:

1. The holding is within the Bank's Excon-approved foreign asset allowance.
2. Eitan has determined that the hard-currency instrument provides genuine HQLA benefit (e.g., hard currency is required to meet hard-currency liquidity obligations under a stress scenario).
3. Owen has confirmed that the Exchange Control reporting obligations are met.

Any hard-currency portfolio holding creates an FX open position. The FX open position from the investment portfolio is managed within the Bank's overall FX open position limit (per `Policies/market-risk-policy-v1.md` §3 MR-3 FX sensitivity limit). FX hedging of the investment portfolio is permitted; the FX hedge instrument is reported to Treasury and does not appear in the trading book.

## 6. Issuer Concentration Limits

**Owner:** Eitan (Treasurer, governance) · **Approval:** ALCO · **Cadence:** Monitored daily by Ravi; reviewed monthly at ALCO · **Citation:** Regulations Relating to Banks reg.38 (large exposures); BCBS large exposures framework (April 2014)

The following concentration limits apply to the Treasury investment portfolio, supplementary to the Bank-wide concentration risk policy (`Policies/concentration-risk-policy-v1.md`):

| Issuer type | Maximum % of HQLA buffer |
|---|---|
| SA National Government (SAGBs + T-Bills) | 100% (no limit — sovereign primary HQLA) |
| SARB (debentures, bills, reserves) | 100% (no limit — SARB primary HQLA) |
| Single government-guaranteed issuer (e.g., DBSA) | 20% of HQLA buffer |
| Single covered bond issuer | 10% of HQLA buffer |
| All Level 2A instruments combined | 40% of HQLA buffer (LCR cap) |
| All Level 2B instruments combined | 15% of HQLA buffer (LCR cap; ALCO approval required) |

Concentration limit breaches trigger Ravi to notify Eitan within 1 business day. An `InvestmentPortfolioConcentrationBreached { issuerType, currentPct, limit }` event is the canonical record.

## 7. Duration Limits

**Owner:** Eitan (Treasurer, governance) · **Approval:** ALCO · **Cadence:** Monitored daily; reviewed monthly · **Citation:** BCBS IRRBB (April 2016) — banking book interest rate risk from investment portfolio; SARB Guidance Note on IRRBB

The duration of the investment portfolio contributes to the Bank's IRRBB EVE sensitivity (per `Policies/irrbb-policy-v1.md`). The following duration limits apply to the investment portfolio as part of the integrated ALM limit framework:

| Metric | Limit |
|---|---|
| Maximum portfolio-weighted average duration | 5 years |
| Maximum single-instrument modified duration | 10 years |
| Maximum duration contribution from Level 2A instruments | 3 years (weighted average for the Level 2A sub-portfolio) |

These limits ensure that the liquidity buffer does not itself introduce unmanageable interest rate risk. Duration limits are calibrated alongside the IRRBB EVE limits; Ravi monitors compliance daily. Duration limit breaches trigger the same ALCO escalation as ALM limit breaches.

## 8. Rebalancing Triggers

**Owner:** Eitan (Treasurer, governance) · **Approval:** ALCO ratifies rebalancing strategy; Eitan approves individual rebalancing transactions within the strategy · **Cadence:** Systematic review monthly at ALCO; event-triggered rebalancing as per triggers below · **Citation:** Basel III LCR (January 2013) — HQLA eligibility maintained on a going-concern basis

Rebalancing of the Treasury investment portfolio is triggered by:

1. **LCR breach approach:** LCR ratio falls below 110% (internal warning trigger, 10% above the regulatory 100% minimum) — Eitan initiates a rebalancing review to increase the HQLA buffer.
2. **Instrument downgrade:** A held instrument is downgraded below the minimum credit quality floor for its HQLA tier — the instrument is sold or reclassified to a non-HQLA banking book position within 5 business days.
3. **Concentration breach:** An issuer concentration limit (§6) is breached due to market movements or new purchases — rebalancing within 10 business days.
4. **Duration breach:** A duration limit (§7) is breached — rebalancing within 5 business days.
5. **Funding structure change:** A material change to the Bank's liability profile (e.g., large term deposit maturity, new institutional client onboarding) requires the HQLA buffer to be resized — Eitan proposes a rebalancing plan to ALCO within 5 business days.

All rebalancing transactions are reported to ALCO at the next monthly meeting. Material rebalancing (> 20% of the HQLA buffer value) requires prior ALCO approval.

## 9. Securities Lending from the Investment Portfolio

**Owner:** Eitan (Treasurer, governance) · **Approval:** ALCO for securities lending programme; Eitan for individual transactions within approved parameters · **Citation:** Banks Act 94 of 1990; Basel III LCR (January 2013) — encumbered HQLA treatment; `Policies/securities-financing-policy-v1.md` (SFT framework)

Securities lending from the HQLA portfolio is subject to the following restrictions:

- **HQLA encumbrance limit:** No more than 10% of the HQLA buffer may be on loan at any time. Instruments on loan are not counted in the HQLA buffer for LCR purposes per the Basel III LCR (encumbered assets rule).
- **Permitted purposes only:** Securities lending from the investment portfolio is permitted only for: (i) client facilitation by the trading desk (where the client requires specific JSE-listed instruments for settlement purposes); and (ii) Treasury liquidity optimisation (lending Level 2 HQLA to borrow Level 1, net HQLA quality improvement).
- **GMSLA requirement:** All securities lending transactions from the investment portfolio require an executed ISDA GMSLA with the counterparty per `Policies/securities-financing-policy-v1.md`.
- **Haircut floors:** Initial margin (haircut) floors for securities lending from the investment portfolio are the same as those in `Policies/securities-financing-policy-v1.md` §3.
- **ALCO notification:** All securities lending transactions from the investment portfolio are reported to ALCO at the monthly meeting. Transactions above the threshold in `Procedures/by-policy/securities-lending-monitoring.md` require prior Eitan approval.
