## 1. Pillar 3 Disclosure Policy — Overarching

**Owner:** Camille (Chief Financial Officer, governance) · **Approval:** Board (CEO interim per `D-THIN-HUMAN-LAYER-MINIMUM`) for the annual Pillar 3 report; Audit Committee reviews and approves the annual report annually · **Cadence:** Quarterly disclosure publication; annual report with financial statements; policy reviewed annually · **Citation:** Banks Act 94 of 1990 (general bank reporting and disclosure obligations) + Regulations Relating to Banks 2012 (as amended) Reg 39 (remuneration disclosure, cross-referenced by D7/2022 `[citation: TBC — exact Reg 39 sub-clause for Pillar 3 remuneration disclosures]`) + PA Directive 10 of 2025 (D10/2025 — the in-force Pillar 3 disclosure standard for South African banks; `ORG-PR-28`) + PA Directive 7 of 2022 (D7/2022 — Pillar 3 remuneration disclosures `[citation: TBC — precise D7/2022 remuneration-disclosure schedule references]`)

### Purpose

This policy governs how Hoz Bank Limited (the "Bank") prepares, reviews, approves, and publishes its Pillar 3 market-discipline disclosures under PA D10/2025. Pillar 3 is the third pillar of the Basel III capital framework: where Pillar 1 sets minimum capital standards and Pillar 2 governs the supervisory review process, Pillar 3 harnesses market discipline by requiring banks to publicly disclose the risk, capital, and liquidity information that enables counterparties, creditors, investors, and other market participants to form an informed assessment of a bank's risk profile. Market discipline operates when market participants have accurate, timely, and comparable information.

Pillar 3 disclosures are not a marketing exercise or an investor relations function. They are a regulatory obligation — a deliberate design feature of the Basel framework that makes a bank's risk-taking visible to the market. The PA has operationalised Pillar 3 through a succession of directives: D2/2014 established the initial framework; D1/2025 updated it; D10/2025 (the current in-force instrument) sets the applicable standard from its effective date. This policy governs under D10/2025; it acknowledges the supersession chain for historical-record purposes and sets out the Bank's approach to managing future supersessions.

This policy sits at the *policy* layer of the Reg → Policy → Procedure → System Capability chain (Principle 2). It cites the regulatory obligations above it; procedures under this policy (including `Procedures/by-policy/pillar-3-disclosure-preparation.md`, `Procedures/by-policy/pillar-3-disclosure-publication.md`, and `Procedures/by-policy/pillar-3-materiality-assessment.md`) operationalise it; the Pillar 3 disclosure generator — a component of the Bank's reporting substrate — is the system capability that produces the disclosure tables. The policy does not reproduce the content of the D10/2025 disclosure tables; those are defined by D10/2025 and rendered by the disclosure generator. This policy governs the governance standards, materiality framework, publication channels, and verification requirements that apply to those tables.

Pillar 3 disclosures are public-facing. Unlike BA returns (submitted to the PA in a structured format through a closed portal), Pillar 3 disclosures are published on the Bank's public website and filed with the PA. The public-facing nature of Pillar 3 disclosures creates a reputational dimension alongside the regulatory dimension: a Pillar 3 disclosure that is inaccurate, incomplete, or inconsistent with the BA-return suite is both a regulatory failure and a reputational risk. The Bank's Pillar 3 governance framework must therefore be rigorous enough to meet both standards simultaneously.

### Principles

- **D10/2025 is the governing instrument.** The Bank applies the disclosure standards set by PA D10/2025 in full. Where D10/2025 provides flexibility (permitted alternative approaches, locally-defined thresholds, materiality-based omissions), the Bank applies that flexibility only where explicitly permitted and documents its application. D10/2025 supersedes D1/2025 (which superseded D2/2014) for all disclosures from D10/2025's effective date. Disclosures made under prior instruments for periods to which those instruments applied remain valid for those periods.
- **Quarterly cadence — within the PA-prescribed timeline.** The Bank publishes Pillar 3 disclosures quarterly. The prescribed timeline under D10/2025 is `[citation: TBC — exact days-after-quarter-end timeline prescribed by D10/2025; Mira to confirm]`. The annual Pillar 3 report is published simultaneously with the annual financial statements. The quarterly and annual publication deadlines are tracked in the disclosure calendar maintained under `Procedures/by-policy/pillar-3-disclosure-preparation.md`.
- **Dual-channel publication.** Every Pillar 3 disclosure is published on the Bank's public website (primary channel) and filed with the PA (secondary channel). Both publications are required; neither substitutes for the other. The website publication must be accessible — not buried in a PDF archive — and the URL must remain stable between quarterly updates. The PA filing follows the PA's prescribed filing mechanism for Pillar 3 disclosures `[citation: TBC — D10/2025 PA filing mechanism; Mira to confirm whether the PA portal used for BA returns also accepts Pillar 3 filings]`.
- **CFO review on every disclosure.** Camille reviews every Pillar 3 disclosure (quarterly and annual) before publication. The review confirms: (i) all required D10/2025 tables are included; (ii) the data is consistent with the corresponding BA-suite returns for the same period; (iii) any materiality-based omissions are documented; and (iv) the disclosure is free of material errors. The review is recorded as a `Pillar3DisclosureReviewed { period, reviewedBy: "Camille", reviewTimestamp }` typed event before publication.
- **Annual Audit Committee approval.** The annual Pillar 3 report — the report that covers the full financial year and is synchronised with the annual financial statements — requires Audit Committee review and approval before publication. The Audit Committee review is supported by Vera's internal-audit opinion (§7 of this policy) and is recorded as a `Pillar3AnnualReportApproved { financialYear, approvedBy: "Audit Committee", approvalTimestamp }` typed event. Quarterly disclosures do not require Audit Committee approval; CFO review suffices for quarterly publications.
- **Board-level sign-off for the annual report.** The annual Pillar 3 report is a Board document. Under the interim governance structure (`D-THIN-HUMAN-LAYER-MINIMUM`), the CEO (Marc as CEO) provides the Board sign-off; at steady-state the Board itself signs off via the BRC or Audit Committee. Board sign-off is recorded as a `Pillar3AnnualReportBoardApproved { financialYear, approvedBy, approvalTimestamp }` typed event. The Board sign-off confirms that the annual Pillar 3 report accurately represents the Bank's risk profile and is consistent with the Board's understanding of the Bank's condition.
- **Consistency with BA-suite and ICAAP.** Pillar 3 disclosures are not independently authored; they are a public-facing presentation of data that also flows through the BA-return suite and the ICAAP. The capital ratios in the quarterly Pillar 3 disclosure must be identical to the BA 100 capital ratios for the same period. The liquidity ratios must be consistent with BA 900. The leverage ratio must be consistent with BA 700. Any inconsistency between the Pillar 3 disclosure and the corresponding BA return is a material error requiring correction before publication. The Pillar 3 disclosure generator produces its output from the same event substrate and the same BA-form computation logic; divergence is a substrate defect, not an authoring variation.
- **Materiality governs omissions, not convenience.** The Bank applies the materiality standard set by D10/2025 for omissions. A disclosure is immaterial if its omission or misstatement would not influence the decision of a reasonable user relying on that information. Immateriality is not self-certified by the preparer; it is assessed per the D10/2025 framework, documented, and retained for PA inspection. A claim of immateriality that would not withstand PA scrutiny is treated as a completeness failure, not an accepted omission.
- **Events-first data lineage.** Pillar 3 disclosure tables must be derived from the Bank's canonical event log (Principle 1). The Pillar 3 disclosure generator consumes the same event substrate as the BA-form generator; the tables are a different presentation layer over the same underlying data, not a separately maintained data set. A Pillar 3 table produced from data that does not trace to a typed event in the event log is a Principle 1 violation.
- **No forward-looking statements without basis.** Pillar 3 disclosures describe the Bank's risk profile as at the reporting date. Forward-looking statements (projections, targets, outlooks) are included only where D10/2025 specifically requires them (e.g., IRRBB interest-rate sensitivity scenarios) and are clearly labelled as forward-looking. No forward-looking statement is included without a basis in the stress-projection engine's output or an explicitly modelled sensitivity.
- **Supersession management as a standing obligation.** The Pillar 3 disclosure framework has a four-generation supersession chain: D2/2014 → D1/2025 → D10/2025 (and future successors). Mira monitors for PA signals of further supersession (consultation papers, draft directives, PA announcements); a draft supersession is flagged as a `PaDirectiveDraftSignalled { instrument, expectedEffectiveDate }` event, initiating the change-impact assessment process in sufficient time to update the policy and the disclosure generator before the new instrument's effective date.

### Breach

Breach taxonomy under this policy is three-severity:

- **Alert (Amber).** A quarterly disclosure is not ready within the PA-prescribed timeline but can be published within 5 business days of the deadline; or a minor inaccuracy (non-material by D10/2025 standards) is identified after publication. Camille notified immediately; disclosure corrected and republished with a correction notice on the website.
- **Hard Breach (Red).** A quarterly disclosure is not published by the PA-prescribed deadline; or a material inaccuracy is identified after publication (pre-correction). PA notification required; corrected disclosure published within 10 business days of error identification; Audit Committee informed. `Pillar3DisclosureBreachEscalated` event emitted.
- **Critical (Critical-Red).** A material misstatement in a published annual Pillar 3 report; or wilful omission of required tables; or failure to synchronise the annual disclosure with the annual financial statements. Immediate CEO and Board notification; immediate PA notification; external counsel advice sought. Vera engaged immediately.

## 2. Supersession Chain and Governing Instrument

**Owner:** Mira (Regulatory intelligence engineer, compliance) — supersession monitoring; Camille (Chief Financial Officer, governance) — policy update authority · **Approval:** Camille approves policy updates on supersession; Board notified of material framework changes · **Cadence:** Ongoing monitoring; policy updated within 30 business days of a supersession instrument's effective date · **Citation:** D10/2025 (`ORG-PR-28` — in-force) + D1/2025 (`ORG-PR-27` — supersession anchor) + D2/2014 (`ORG-PR-52` — historical anchor)

### Purpose

The Pillar 3 disclosure obligation in South Africa has evolved through four generations of regulatory instruments. Understanding the supersession chain matters for three reasons: (i) historical disclosures made under prior instruments must remain accessible and are not retroactively invalid; (ii) the Bank's obligations register must track supersession clearly to avoid double-counting or gap-leaving; and (iii) when the next supersession occurs, the Bank must be ready to implement the new instrument before its effective date.

### 2.1 Supersession Chain

**D2/2014 — Pillar 3 Market Discipline Disclosure (now superseded; `ORG-PR-52` retained as historical anchor).** D2/2014 was the first PA directive implementing the Basel III Pillar 3 framework for South African banks. It established the initial disclosure tables and the publication requirements. D2/2014 was the governing instrument from its effective date until D1/2025 superseded it. Any disclosures made by the Bank under D2/2014 for periods to which it applied (i.e., periods before D1/2025's effective date, noting the Bank was not yet licensed during D2/2014's tenure) are governed by D2/2014's standards. The Bank has no disclosure obligations under D2/2014 as it was not licensed during D2/2014's operative period; `ORG-PR-52` is retained in the obligations register as a supersession-chain anchor.

**D1/2025 — Pillar 3 Disclosure (superseded by D10/2025; `ORG-PR-27` retained as supersession anchor).** D1/2025 updated the Pillar 3 framework, reflecting BCBS Pillar 3 consolidation phases and any SA-specific adaptations. D1/2025 was superseded by D10/2025 before the Bank's licence date; the Bank has no disclosure obligations that fell under D1/2025 exclusively (D10/2025 governed from licence-day). `ORG-PR-27` is retained in the obligations register as the D1/2025 supersession anchor.

**D10/2025 — Pillar 3 Disclosure Standard (in-force; `ORG-PR-28`).** D10/2025 is the in-force instrument. It governs all Pillar 3 disclosures from its effective date. All Bank disclosures from licence-day forward are prepared under D10/2025. The disclosure tables, frequency, timing, publication channels, materiality standard, and verification requirements set by D10/2025 are the operative standards for this policy.

### 2.2 Future Supersession Management

Mira monitors the following signals for a potential D10/2025 supersession: PA consultation papers on Pillar 3 (published on the PA's official publications page); BCBS Pillar 3 revision consultative documents (which typically precede PA adoption); PA announcements at annual supervision conferences. When a credible supersession signal is identified, Mira files a `PaDirectiveDraftSignalled` event and initiates a change-impact assessment covering: which D10/2025 tables are affected; what new tables are proposed; what timeline is available for implementation; and what changes are required in the Pillar 3 disclosure generator. The change-impact assessment is delivered to Camille within 20 business days of the signal being identified.

On supersession — when a new directive replaces D10/2025 — this policy is updated to reflect the new governing instrument; the superseded instrument is demoted to a historical anchor; and the new obligations-register row becomes the active obligation. The policy version is incremented on each governing-instrument supersession.

## 3. Disclosure Tables

**Owner:** Camille (Chief Financial Officer, governance) for governance; Bea (Accounting & financial reporting engineer, engineering) for production; Devon (Chief Operating Officer) for market-risk tables · **Approval:** CFO review per quarterly cycle; Audit Committee approval per annual cycle · **Cadence:** Quarterly production; annual publication synchronised with financial statements · **Citation:** D10/2025 (`ORG-PR-28`) — the specific table references within D10/2025 are `[citation: TBC — exact D10/2025 table numbering; Mira to confirm against D10/2025 text]`

### Purpose

D10/2025 prescribes a suite of disclosure tables covering each major risk dimension of the Bank's profile. This section describes the content, data sources, and governance of each table category. The table-level detail — specific rows, columns, calculation methodologies, and permitted alternative formats — is maintained in `Procedures/by-policy/pillar-3-disclosure-preparation.md` and the Pillar 3 disclosure generator's documentation. This policy section names the categories, their regulatory basis, and any category-specific governance requirements.

### 3.1 Capital Adequacy Disclosures

Capital adequacy disclosures report the Bank's regulatory capital composition and adequacy metrics under the Basel III/IV framework.

**Capital ratios.** CET1, Tier 1, and Total capital ratios against RWA; the Pillar 1 minimum, Pillar 2A add-on, CCB, and CCyB components; the Bank's capital position relative to the PA-set requirements. These ratios must be identical to the corresponding BA 100 figures for the same period. The Pillar 3 disclosure adds narrative context — the bank's capital management approach, the composition of the capital ratios — that the BA 100 does not.

**RWA by risk type.** Total RWA broken down by credit risk, market risk (standardised approach and, once the Bank's FRTB Internal Models Approach (IMA) is approved by the PA, IMA), counterparty credit risk, and operational risk. The breakdown by risk type enables market participants to understand the Bank's risk-weighted asset composition. The data is sourced from BA 200, BA 300, BA 325/326, and the operational-risk RWA computation.

**Capital instruments.** Summary disclosure of the terms and conditions of any outstanding CET1 (share capital), AT1 instruments (if issued), and T2 instruments (if issued). At v1, the Bank's capital structure is CET1-dominant; the AT1 and T2 sub-tables are either empty (with a notation that the Bank has no outstanding AT1/T2 at the reporting date) or not applicable, depending on D10/2025's convention for zero-balance tables `[citation: TBC — D10/2025 convention for zero-balance tables]`.

**Capital transition (if applicable).** Any transitional provisions affecting capital deductions, grandfathering of legacy instruments, or IFRS 9 ECL transitional arrangements per PA D3/2023 are disclosed separately where D10/2025 requires. The Bank's application of the IFRS 9 transitional arrangements (per the Capital Management Policy's IFRS 9 provisions read-through principle) is disclosed here during the transitional period.

### 3.2 Liquidity Disclosures

Liquidity disclosures report the Bank's compliance with and management of the Basel III liquidity standards.

**Liquidity Coverage Ratio (LCR).** Quarterly average LCR (as computed across the quarter, not as a point-in-time snap shot per D10/2025's averaging methodology `[citation: TBC — D10/2025 LCR averaging convention]`); the components of High-Quality Liquid Assets (HQLA) by asset category; and net cash outflows under the 30-day stress scenario. The data is sourced from BA 900 and the Bank's liquidity-management event substrate under Eitan's (Treasurer, governance) oversight. The LCR in the Pillar 3 disclosure must be consistent with BA 900 for the same period.

**Net Stable Funding Ratio (NSFR).** NSFR computed per the Basel III NSFR framework as adopted by the PA; Available Stable Funding (ASF) by category and Required Stable Funding (RSF) by asset category; the NSFR ratio. Sourced from BA 900 and the Bank's structural funding event substrate. Consistent with BA 900.

**Encumbrance.** Asset-encumbrance disclosure per D10/2025 requirements `[citation: TBC — whether D10/2025 requires an asset-encumbrance table and its format]`: encumbered vs unencumbered assets by category, and sources of encumbrance (repo, collateral posted for derivatives, other). This disclosure provides market participants with visibility into the Bank's liquidity-constrained asset base.

### 3.3 Leverage Ratio Disclosures

The leverage ratio disclosure reports the Bank's non-risk-based capital backstop under the Basel III leverage ratio framework.

**Leverage ratio.** Tier 1 capital / total leverage exposure; the components of the leverage exposure measure (on-balance-sheet, off-balance-sheet, derivatives, SFTs); and the PA-set minimum. The data is sourced from BA 700 and must be consistent with BA 700 for the same period. The leverage ratio disclosure also includes a comparison with the prior quarter to show the trend.

### 3.4 Credit Risk Disclosures

Credit risk disclosures provide market participants with information on the Bank's credit-risk exposures, portfolio quality, and provisioning.

**Portfolio quality.** Credit exposures by asset class (sovereign, bank, corporate, retail) and by credit-quality step; gross and net of credit-risk mitigation; on-balance-sheet and off-balance-sheet. For an institutional trading bank, the sovereign and bank counterparty asset classes will dominate the credit portfolio, with corporate counterparty exposures arising through OTC derivative CCR.

**Impairment and ECL staging (IFRS 9).** Stage 1, Stage 2, and Stage 3 exposures and the corresponding Expected Credit Loss (ECL) provisions. Stage 1 (performing), Stage 2 (significant increase in credit risk), and Stage 3 (credit-impaired) classification follows IFRS 9 per the Bank's credit-risk management framework. For an institutional global-markets bank with a selective counterparty credit profile, Stage 3 exposures should be minimal at inception; the disclosure is substantive even at low volumes because the methodology is disclosed regardless of the balance.

**Credit-risk concentration.** Concentration disclosures per D10/2025: geographic concentration (SA vs non-SA); counterparty-type concentration (sovereign, bank, NBFI, corporate); single-counterparty concentration (large exposures consistent with BA 600). Concentration risk is a specific Pillar 3 disclosure requirement because it identifies risks not fully captured by standardised RWA.

**Credit-risk mitigation.** Eligible collateral, guarantees, and netting arrangements used to reduce credit exposure; the effect of credit-risk mitigation on BA 200 RWA. The Bank's OTC derivative collateral arrangements (ISDA CSAs `[citation: TBC — Imani (Legal-as-code engineer, engineering) to confirm CSA portfolio scope]`) are the primary credit-risk mitigation mechanism; their recognition and effect on CCR (SA-CCR in BA 325) are disclosed.

### 3.5 Market Risk Disclosures

Market risk disclosures are particularly material for Hoz Bank as an institutional global-markets trading bank. They provide counterparties, creditors, and market participants with visibility into the Bank's trading book risk profile.

**Standardised approach (SA-MR) metrics.** RWA by risk type under the standardised market-risk approach (interest-rate risk, equity risk, foreign-exchange risk, commodities risk, credit spread risk) per the FRTB revised standardised approach as adopted by D2/2024 and D10/2025. These metrics are sourced from BA 300 and must be consistent with BA 300 for the same period.

**Value at Risk (VaR).** Where the Bank operates a VaR model (in the Internal Models Approach (IMA) track under the FRTB framework, subject to PA approval), the VaR-based metrics are disclosed per D10/2025: historical simulation VaR at 99% confidence / 10-day horizon; stressed VaR; and backtesting results. At v1, the Bank operates under the standardised approach; VaR disclosures apply when IMA approval is sought. The Pillar 3 policy is authored to govern VaR disclosures pre-emptively, so the framework is in place when the IMA is adopted.

**FRTB Internal Models Approach metrics (when applicable).** Expected Shortfall (ES) at 97.5% confidence; per-risk-class ES; the Risk Factor Eligibility Test (RFET) results; the Profit & Loss Attribution (PLA) test results; and the number of trading desks approved under IMA vs those on the standardised approach fall-back. These disclosures are required under D10/2025 once IMA is operational; they are named here as planned disclosures.

**Sensitivity analysis.** Key market risk sensitivities — DV01 (interest rate), CS01 (credit spread), equity delta, FX delta — for the trading book as at the reporting date. These sensitivity disclosures enable market participants to assess the Bank's market risk profile at a finer granularity than RWA provides. Devon produces the sensitivity data from the position event substrate under Devon's ownership (Markets engineer, engineering).

**IRRBB (Interest Rate Risk in the Banking Book).** Per BCBS D335 *Standards: IRRBB* (April 2016) as adopted by the PA through D10/2025 `[citation: TBC — whether D10/2025 requires IRRBB disclosures and their format; Mira to confirm]`: the impact of interest-rate shocks on Net Interest Income (NII) and Economic Value of Equity (EVE) under the six prescribed BCBS scenarios; the Bank's IRRBB management approach; and the IRRBB exposure relative to the supervisory threshold. Eitan (Treasurer, governance) and Devon own the IRRBB sensitivity computation; Rohan (Risk engineer, engineering — reports to Helena (Chief Risk Officer, governance)) integrates it into the stress-projection engine.

### 3.6 Operational Risk Disclosures

Operational risk disclosures report the Bank's operational risk profile and the approach used to compute operational risk RWA.

**Approach disclosure.** The Bank's method for computing operational risk RWA: at v1, the Standardised Measurement Approach (SMA) per BCBS Basel III/IV as adopted by the PA `[citation: TBC — whether the PA has adopted SMA or uses the BIA/TSA/AMA spectrum; Mira to confirm against D10/2025 and D2/2024]`. The disclosure names the approach, the Business Indicator Component (BIC) and Internal Loss Multiplier (ILM) if applicable, and the resulting operational risk RWA.

**Loss data.** Historical operational risk loss event data: gross losses by Basel II loss event category, net of recoveries, for the most recent years per D10/2025 `[citation: TBC — D10/2025 historical loss data window requirement]`. For the Bank's first Pillar 3 report, the historical loss data window is limited to the period of licensed operation; the Bank discloses this limitation explicitly. The operational risk engineer (role on Helena's engineering bench — current name attribution drift surfaced 2026-05-21; substantive role assignment pending) curates the operational-loss event taxonomy; the data is sourced from the Risk Return (§3 of the Regulatory Reporting Policy) for consistency.

**Operational risk management approach.** Qualitative disclosure of the Bank's operational risk management framework — governance, risk appetite, key control processes — at the level of detail D10/2025 requires `[citation: TBC — D10/2025 qualitative disclosure requirements for operational risk]`.

### 3.7 Remuneration Disclosures

Remuneration disclosures under Pillar 3 are a distinct obligation from the general remuneration disclosures in the annual financial statements. They are required by Reg 39 and PA D7/2022 as part of the Pillar 3 framework, and are designed to provide transparency on how remuneration practices align with risk management.

**Governance and design.** Disclosure of the remuneration governance framework: the role of the remuneration governance body (Board Remuneration Committee, or CEO interim during build phase); the material risk-takers identified for Pillar 3 remuneration purposes; the principles governing variable remuneration design (risk-alignment, deferral, malus/clawback). At v1 build phase, there are no human employees subject to variable remuneration (per `project_ai_driven_bank.md` — no real employees until licence-day); the remuneration disclosures cover the statutory minimum human directors and any key individuals appointed at licence-day. The disclosure framework is authored to be operational from licence-day.

**Aggregate quantitative disclosures.** Total fixed remuneration, total variable remuneration, and deferred variable remuneration by category of material risk-taker, per D7/2022 `[citation: TBC — exact D7/2022 table format and aggregation categories]`. These are quantitative; they are populated from the payroll event substrate once the Bank has human employees at licence-day.

**Deferral and malus/clawback.** Outstanding deferred awards (by year of grant and vesting status); any malus or clawback applied in the period; and the rationale per D7/2022 requirements. The malus/clawback governance framework is part of the remuneration policy (a separate instrument to be authored when employment contracts are activated at licence-day per Imani's remuneration-contracts scope).

The remuneration disclosures are prepared by Sade (AgentOps engineer — reports to Nolan (Chief People Officer, governance)) when the human payroll substrate is live; at build phase, the disclosure is made with a notation that no human variable remuneration exists.

## 4. Materiality Assessment

**Owner:** Camille (Chief Financial Officer, governance) · **Approval:** Camille approves materiality assessments; Audit Committee reviews annually · **Cadence:** Applied on every disclosure cycle; reviewed annually as part of the Audit Committee review · **Citation:** D10/2025 (`ORG-PR-28`) — D10/2025 materiality standard `[citation: TBC — precise D10/2025 materiality definition and omission documentation requirements]`

### Purpose

D10/2025 permits the omission of immaterial disclosures. Materiality is defined per D10/2025 as `[citation: TBC — D10/2025 materiality definition]`. The Bank does not treat immateriality as a convenience; it is a governed determination made for each disclosure table on each reporting cycle, documented, and retained for PA inspection.

### 4.1 Materiality Determination Process

For each D10/2025 disclosure table, Bea (with Mira's regulatory-standards guidance) assesses materiality at the start of each quarterly disclosure cycle: (i) is the Bank active in the risk type covered by the table? (ii) does the Bank's exposure meet any quantitative threshold set by D10/2025? (iii) would the omission of the table influence a reasonable user's assessment of the Bank's risk profile?

If all three answers are "no," the table may be omitted under D10/2025's materiality carve-out. The omission is documented as a `Pillar3TableOmitted { period, tableId, materialityRationale, documentedBy: "Bea", approvedBy: "Camille" }` event. The PA may challenge any claimed omission during a supervisory examination; the `Pillar3TableOmitted` event and its documented rationale constitute the Bank's record.

Omissions are reviewed annually by the Audit Committee. If the Audit Committee is not satisfied that a claimed immateriality determination would withstand PA scrutiny, the table is included in future disclosures regardless of the initial assessment.

### 4.2 Partial Disclosures

Where a table is material but the Bank's substrate does not yet produce all required data (a known substrate gap during the build and early-operation phase), the table is published with the available data and an explicit notation of the data not yet available, with a remediation timeline. A partial disclosure is preferable to an omission; it demonstrates good faith and provides the PA with what is available. Partial disclosures are documented as `Pillar3PartialDisclosure { period, tableId, missingElements[], remediationTimeline }` events.

## 6. Verification Framework

**Owner:** Camille (Chief Financial Officer, governance) — CFO review; Thandiwe (Chief Audit Executive, governance) — internal audit commission · **Approval:** Audit Committee for the annual internal-audit opinion · **Cadence:** CFO review per quarterly cycle; Vera opinion annually · **Citation:** D10/2025 (`ORG-PR-28`) + Banks Act 94 of 1990

### Purpose

D10/2025's Pillar 3 tables are not audited by the external auditor in the same way as the annual financial statements. This creates a specific risk: Pillar 3 disclosures may contain errors that the external audit process would not catch. The Bank's response is a two-tier verification framework: CFO review on every cycle (first-line); Vera internal-audit opinion annually (third-line). Second-line review by Mira (regulatory accuracy of the D10/2025 table structure) is embedded in the disclosure preparation process.

### 6.1 CFO Review

Before every quarterly Pillar 3 disclosure is published, Camille conducts a structured review: (i) cross-referencing each capital, liquidity, and leverage table against the corresponding BA-return figure; (ii) reviewing period-on-period movements for unexplained material changes; (iii) confirming that all required tables are present or have a documented materiality omission; and (iv) confirming that the data lineage for each table traces to a typed event in the event log (spot-checked via the `sourceEventIds[]` audit trail). The review is documented as a `Pillar3DisclosureReviewed` event. A quarterly disclosure is not published without a corresponding `Pillar3DisclosureReviewed` event; publication before review is a Vera Critical finding.

### 6.2 Vera Internal-Audit Opinion

Vera (internal audit engineer, reports functionally to Thandiwe) provides an annual opinion on the completeness and accuracy of the Pillar 3 disclosure process. The opinion covers: whether the disclosure preparation process followed the procedure in `Procedures/by-policy/pillar-3-disclosure-preparation.md`; whether CFO review events were recorded for every quarterly disclosure in the year; whether the annual disclosure was approved by the Audit Committee and the Board; whether any materiality omissions were adequately documented; and whether the data in the Pillar 3 tables traced to the event log. The Vera opinion is presented to Thandiwe and the Audit Committee at the annual review.

### 6.3 External Auditor Process Review

The external auditor is not required to audit the Pillar 3 tables; the Basel framework does not mandate external audit of Pillar 3 disclosures. However, the external auditor reviews the Pillar 3 disclosure process as part of the annual audit engagement: does the process produce disclosures consistent with the audited financial statements? Are the capital ratios in the Pillar 3 disclosure consistent with the BA 100 returns that the external auditor has access to? The external auditor's findings on the Pillar 3 process are reported to the Audit Committee alongside the Vera opinion.

## 7. Exceptions and Escalation

**Owner:** Camille (Chief Financial Officer, governance) · **Approval:** Camille approves exceptions; escalates to CEO and Board per breach taxonomy · **Cadence:** Event-triggered · **Citation:** D10/2025 + Banks Act 94 of 1990

### Purpose

An exception under this policy is any deviation from the standards in §§1–6 that is pre-approved, documented, and time-bounded. Exceptions include: PA-approved extensions to publication deadlines; materiality-based omissions per §4; partial disclosures per §4.2; and build-phase limitations on remuneration disclosures per §3.7.

### 7.1 Exception Categories

**Deadline extension (PA-approved).** An approved PA extension to a Pillar 3 publication deadline. Must be requested before the scheduled deadline; the extension approval from the PA must be in writing. Documented as `Pillar3ExtensionRequested` event followed by `Pillar3ExtensionApproved { paReference, extendedDeadline }` event.

**Materiality omission (§4).** A Pillar 3 table omitted under D10/2025's materiality carve-out. Documented as `Pillar3TableOmitted` event; retained for PA inspection; reviewed annually by Audit Committee.

**Partial disclosure (§4.2).** A Pillar 3 table published with incomplete data due to a known substrate gap. Documented as `Pillar3PartialDisclosure` event; website disclosure includes explicit notation; remediation timeline committed.

**Build-phase remuneration limitation (§3.7).** During the build phase, remuneration disclosures reflect the absence of human variable remuneration. Disclosed explicitly in the remuneration table with a notation. Activated automatically at licence-day when human employment commences.

### 7.2 Escalation

Breaches (per §1.4) escalate as follows:
- Alert: Camille and Bea; disclosure corrected and republished within 5 business days with a correction notice. ALCO notified at next session.
- Hard Breach: Camille initiates; CEO notified within 24 hours; Audit Committee informed; PA notified within 24 hours if a published disclosure contained a material inaccuracy. Corrected disclosure published within 10 business days.
- Critical: Camille initiates; CEO and Board notified immediately; PA notified immediately; external counsel engaged; Vera engaged for an independent review. `Pillar3DisclosureBreachEscalated { severity: "Critical" }` event emitted.
