## 1. Role purpose + regulatory anchor

The Company Secretary is the bank's statutory Companies-Act officer and the human face of the bank's Board secretariat. The seat carries three load-bearing accountabilities:

1. **Statutory CoSec function** under Companies Act 71 of 2008 ss.86–89 — competence and experience requirements (s.86(2)(a)), CEO bar (s.86(2)(b) read with SA practice), and reporting line to the Board (s.88(2)(c)).
2. **Designated officer under SARB PA fit-and-proper substrate** — the CoSec is one of the designated officers within the PA fit-and-proper perimeter, with substantive (not merely formal) independence from the CEO. This is Owen + Imani §2.8 substrate authority and is consistent with SARB PA precedent on substantive CoSec independence.
3. **Deputy POPIA Information Officer under Regulation 4** — placed on this seat per Mira + Zara §4.2 (the conduct-side amplification splits the alternate load: deputy-IO sits with CoSec; MLRO-alternate sits with the AC Chair NED). The deputy-IO carries the IO function during the IO's absence; lodgment with the Information Regulator under Reg. 4 is itself a CEO act.

| Anchor | Section / clause | Bearing on this seat |
|---|---|---|
| Companies Act 71 of 2008 | ss.86–89; s.86(2)(b); s.88(2)(c) | Mandatory separate-human statutory function; CEO bar; Board reporting line. [register: ORG-GV-01 IN FORCE] |
| Banks Act 94 of 1990 | s.60 + PA fit-and-proper sub-instruments | CoSec is designated officer in fit-and-proper scope. [register: ORG-GV-11] |
| King IV Code on Corporate Governance for South Africa | Principle 7 (composition of governing body); CoSec independence convention | Substantive independence from CEO; standing attendee at Board and committees. |
| POPIA Act 4 of 2013 | s.56 (IO designation); POPIA Regulation 4 (deputy-IO contemplated) | Deputy-IO designation form per Mira + Zara §4.2. [register: ORG-PR(IV)-13 sub-gloss gap, Mira-owned closure] |
| BCBS Corporate Governance Principles for Banks (2015) | Principle 4; Principle 5 | Board composition and the supporting governance machinery — CoSec is the governance-machinery custodian. |

The bank already has Owen-the-agent as engineering CoSec (a Principle-7 standing autonomous agent). The licence-day human CoSec is the regulator-facing seat through which Owen-the-agent's outputs flow — the human is the named statutory officer; the agent does the substrate work continuously.

## 2. Mandate scope

### 2.1 In-scope mandate

**Board & committee secretariat** — agendas (Board, Audit Committee, Risk Committee, Social & Ethics Committee, Remuneration & Nominations Committee), packs, minutes, resolutions, action-tracking, reserved-matters routing. Standing attendee at all four committees in secretariat capacity.

**Statutory registers and filings** — registers of directors, prescribed officers, secretaries, auditors; CIPC filings (CoR forms; annual return; MOI amendments); statutory records retention.

**Director governance** — induction packs; ongoing director development administration; conflicts and related-party register custodianship (custodian on Companies Act ss.75–76 disclosure cycle).

**Whistleblowing** — channel administration under Companies Act s.159; intake triage; routing to CEO + CAE within 4h if material (per Owen-the-agent's engineering trigger spec).

**Regulatory-correspondence custody** — single custodial point for SARB PA / FSCA / FIC / Information Regulator / SARS / CIPC / JSE supervisory correspondence; routed to the responsible governance seat under documented routing rules (Camille for AC-pathway items; Helena for BRC-pathway items; Thandiwe for IAF / future-AC items; Iris for IO items; Zara for AML / FAIS items).

**Deputy POPIA Information Officer (Reg. 4)** — carries the IO function during the IO's absence; co-owns the PAIA Manual with the IO (already named in `_obligations-register.md` ORG-PR(IV)-16); supports the IO on lawful-processing-register cycle and breach-notification workflow (POPIA s.22 read with the conduct-side §4.2 split).

**Engineering interface** — the human CoSec receives Owen-the-agent's continuously-produced substrate (drafted minutes, action-tracker state, reserved-matters routing decisions, register updates) and exercises human review + signature where statute or judgement requires. The human is not authoring from scratch; the human is the named accountable signer.

**Multi-entity service** — same human serves as CoSec across Hoz Group + Hoz Bank + Hoz Securities (per D-LEGAL-ENTITY-TREE-V0; no statutory bar on a single CoSec serving multiple group entities).

### 2.2 Out-of-scope mandate

- **Day-to-day management** of any business line.
- **AML / CFT substantive ownership** — sits with the Compliance Lead (Priority-5 hire); CoSec does not double as MLRO or FIC Compliance Officer.
- **MLRO-alternate role** — placed on the AC Chair NED per Mira + Zara §4.2 (avoids single-point-of-failure at CoSec seat).
- **Internal audit** — Thandiwe (Chief Audit Executive, governance) owns; CoSec is secretariat to the IAF / AC, not the auditor.
- **Substantive risk measurement** — Helena (CRO, governance) and Rohan (Risk engineer) own.
- **Substantive compliance opinions** — Zara (CCO, governance) and Mira (Compliance / RegTech engineer) own.

## 4. Fit-and-proper considerations

**SARB PA fit-and-proper.** The CoSec is a designated officer within the PA's fit-and-proper perimeter. Companies Act s.69 (director-disqualification grounds) does not directly apply to CoSec, but SARB PA substantively expects equivalent fit-and-proper standing — clean disclosures, no s.69-equivalent disqualifications, no concurrent conflicts.

**Companies Act s.86(2)(a) competence test.** The CoSec must demonstrate "competence and experience" — discharged through CGISA membership in good standing or equivalent track-record evidence.

**POPIA Reg. 4 deputy-IO fit.** The deputy-IO designation is itself a separate fit-and-proper checkpoint per the Information Regulator's published guidance — appropriate authority, appropriate resources, appropriate access to the IO substrate. The conduct-side §4.2 caveat is satisfied by *not* doubling the CoSec as MLRO-alternate.

**Concurrent-engagement screen.** Concurrent CoSec at a competitor SA bank is an automatic disqualifier (per Nolan's recruitment scope §7). Concurrent CoSec at non-competing JSE-listed entities is not automatically disqualifying but must be disclosed; capacity to discharge a regulated-bank CoSec workload at the level expected is the test.

**Whistleblowing-channel integrity.** Companies Act s.159 whistleblowing-channel administration places the CoSec at the centre of a sensitive flow — fit-and-proper review must include reference checks for handling whistleblowing matters with discretion.

## 5. Independence test

**Statutory independence — Companies Act s.86(2)(b).** The CoSec cannot be the CEO. The CEO bar is read in SA practice as a substantive bar (cannot be a person who in substance acts as CEO under another title), not merely a formal one. Owen + Imani §2.8 codifies this for the bank's substrate.

**SARB PA precedent.** The PA reads CoSec independence as substantive — the candidate must be able to challenge the CEO on procedural and reserved-matters questions without conflict. References should specifically test this.

**Cannot also serve as a Director.** The CoSec serves the Board *as secretariat*, not *as member*. The seat is incompatible with concurrent directorship of any entity in the Hoz group.

**Reporting line.**

- **Functionally:** to the Board on Board-secretariat duties (Companies Act s.88(2)(c)), with direct line of access to the Chair.
- **Administratively:** through Devon (COO, governance) for day-to-day operational interface; engineering substrate (Owen-the-agent) feeds the seat continuously.
- **Engagement with CEO:** professional, but the CoSec is *not* the CEO's officer — the CoSec is the Board's officer.

**King IV Principle 7 alignment.** The CoSec's independence from the CEO is one of the King-IV-coded checks; the bank's licence-application file will assert this independence and the candidate must withstand interview-stage probing on it.

- 2–3 incumbents at SA Tier-2 bank CoSec roles (open to move).

- 0–1 rising-talent candidate (deputy CoSec at a Tier-1 bank with 5–8 years' experience) — see Open Question §9.

4. **Fractional / part-time pre-licence-day.** PAX recommends full-time-from-day-one with fractional as a fall-back negotiation lever. Does Marc agree, or does Marc prefer an explicit fractional-friendly stance to access semi-retired Tier-1 bank-CoSec talent?

6. **Market-data refresh.** PAX's compensation band is a directional read, not a fresh benchmark. Should the role-brief land with a commissioned fresh market-data scan (R30–60k spend, ~2 weeks) before the search opens, or proceed with the indicative band?
7. **Multi-entity CoSec sequencing.** Per D-LEGAL-ENTITY-TREE-V0 the same human serves all three entities. Does Marc want this stated in the offer letter as an explicit term, or treated as an operational matter the appointed CoSec ratifies on day one?
