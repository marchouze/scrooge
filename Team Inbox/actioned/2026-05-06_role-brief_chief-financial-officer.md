# Role brief — Chief Financial Officer (CFO)

**Author:** PAX
**Date:** 2026-05-06
**For:** Nolan

## 1. Role title and one-line purpose

**Chief Financial Officer (CFO)** — governance owner of the bank's finances; signs the financial statements and capital adequacy returns; runs treasury, accounting, and tax at executive level; reports directly to the CEO.

## 2. Why this role exists

The CEO has confirmed (2026-05-06) that the CFO holds finance and reports directly to the CEO. The Banks Act and Regulations Relating to Banks expect a named CFO who signs the BA returns and the audited financial statements. King IV identifies the CFO as a designated executive with named responsibilities. The Audit Committee charter cannot operate without a CFO counterpart. SARB Prudential Authority engages the CFO directly on capital, returns, and earnings matters. In a fully coded bank where balances and capital ratios are queries (P1), the CFO's discipline is to *trust the projections and sign* — not to maintain a parallel set of books.

This is a governance seat. Camille does not personally write postings, run treasury trades, or build a tax pipeline. Camille governs the people who do, and answers for the numbers.

## 3. Scope of work (priority order)

1. **Financial reporting governance.** IFRS-compliant financial statements; BA returns to the PA (BA 100 capital, BA 200 balance sheet, BA 300 off-balance-sheet, BA 325 LCR, BA 326 NSFR, BA 330 large exposures, etc.); SARS submissions; audited annual financial statements.
2. **Capital management.** Capital adequacy at PA-set minima plus buffer; capital planning aligned to ICAAP (which is Helena's deliverable, with CFO contribution); capital actions (instrument issuance, dividends, buy-backs) and AT1 / T2 management.
3. **Treasury and ALM (governance).** Operational management of the balance sheet — Ravi as treasury / ALM engineer reports to the CFO; the CFO runs ALCO with Helena providing risk oversight.
4. **Accounting governance.** Sub-ledger architecture, GL discipline, IFRS 9 ECL governance (with Helena), close cadence, reconciliation discipline. Bea reports to the CFO.
5. **Tax governance.** SARS submissions, FATCA / CRS, IAS 12 deferred tax, transfer pricing, VAT FS-apportionment. Yael reports to the CFO.
6. **FP&A and management information.** Budget, forecast, scenario planning; MI for the executive and the board; outputs are queries (P3) over the event log via Anya's projections.
7. **External audit relationship.** Coordinates with the AC and Vera (internal audit / future CAE) on the external auditor's plan, findings, and management response.
8. **Investor and stakeholder finance.** As the bank takes capital from shareholders or wholesale lenders, the CFO is the finance-facing face.
9. **Pricing governance.** Co-governs with Helena (risk-adjusted pricing) and Niko (commercial pricing); ensures FTP attribution, cost-of-funds, and capital cost flow into product pricing.

## 10. Principle alignment

**P1 — Events as source of truth.** Balances, P&L, capital, RWA, LCR, NSFR are queries. The CFO trusts the projection and signs; does not maintain a parallel finance ledger. Restatements and corrections are events with full lineage.

**P2 — Traceability.** Every accounting policy, every BA-return mapping, every tax position cites the standard or section that justifies it. The CFO's policy library is register-linked.

**P3 — Cloud-native, no manual.** Returns, statements, MI packs, ALCO packs, AC packs — all queries. Manual workings are tracked exceptions.

**P4 — Security by design.** Finance data — capital plans, earnings projections, tax positions — is highly sensitive; access is purpose-bound; read events are audited.

**P5 — Multi-everything.** Reporting currency is a presentation choice (IAS 21); functional currency is a property; consolidation across entities is a query. Multi-entity, multi-currency, multi-jurisdiction reporting is the default.
