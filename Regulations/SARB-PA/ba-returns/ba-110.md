## Purpose of BA 110

Form BA 110 is the prudential return through which the bank reports its **Liquidity Coverage Ratio (LCR)** to the Prudential Authority. The LCR is a **Pillar-1 liquidity standard** that requires the bank to hold a stock of **unencumbered high-quality liquid assets (HQLA)** sufficient to survive a **30-calendar-day** severe liquidity-stress scenario.

The headline ratio (BCBS LCR `urn:reg:bcbs:lcr:20.5`; Reg 26):

```
        stock of HQLA (post-haircut, post-cap)
LCR  =  --------------------------------------------  ≥ 100%
        total net cash outflows over 30-day stress
```

D1/2022 §1.2 (verbatim, per the register v1.36 extraction) states the LCR *"promotes short-term resilience of banks to liquidity risk by ensuring sufficient unencumbered HQLA to meet liquidity needs for a 30 calendar-day stress scenario."* Regulation 26(2) sets out, *"among other things, … (a) at the reporting date, in respect of specified time buckets — (i) the contractual mismatch between assets and liabilities; (ii) the 'business-as-usual' mismatch …; (iii) the bank-specific stress mismatch; (b) … the quantity and sources of funding available …; (c) … the reporting bank's potential concentration risk …; (d) … the reporting bank's exposure to foreign exchange [in significant currencies]; (e) the expected change in the bank's balance sheet."* (Reg 26(2)(a)–(e), verbatim from `rrb-structured.json`.)

The bank must retain an **audit trail** reconciling the return to its general ledger: Reg 26(3) requires *"an audit trail in respect of the underlying data relating to the base models supporting the relevant form … [providing] a reconciliation between the total assets and the total liabilities reported on the form … and the … general ledger systems."* (Reg 26(3)(a), verbatim.)

## The LCR numerator — HQLA composition, haircuts and caps

HQLA is the stock of unencumbered, high-quality liquid assets the bank can monetise in stress with little or no loss of value. The framework defines three tiers, each with a prescribed haircut and a portfolio cap (BCBS LCR `urn:reg:bcbs:lcr:30.43`; Reg 26(7)):

| Tier | Composition | Haircut | Cap |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Level 1** | Cash; central-bank (SARB) reserves; 0%-risk-weight sovereign (SA government) bonds. | **0%** | No cap. |
| **Level 2A** | 20%-risk-weight sovereign bonds; qualifying covered bonds; corporate bonds rated **AA−** or better. | **15%** | Level 2 (2A + 2B) ≤ **40%** of total HQLA. |
| **Level 2B** | Qualifying equities (JSE Top 40); corporate bonds rated **BBB− to A+**; qualifying RMBS. | **25–50%** | Level 2B ≤ **15%** of total HQLA (within the 40% Level-2 cap). |

The post-cap HQLA stock (the LCR numerator) is the closed-form solution of:

```
stockHQLA = Level1 + min(0.85 × Level2A_raw, 0.40 × stockHQLA)
                   + min(factor × Level2B_raw, 0.15 × stockHQLA)
```

The 40% Level-2 cap and the 15% Level-2B sub-cap are the binding constraints; for a build-phase posture dominated by Level-1 SARB operational cash, the no-cap regime applies (the cap-binding regimes are exercised by synthetic-fixture tests in the engine).

## The LCR denominator — net 30-day stressed cash outflows

The denominator is **total net cash outflows** over the 30-day stress horizon:

```
netCashOutflows = stressedOutflows − min(stressedInflows, 0.75 × stressedOutflows)
                = max(grossOutflows − recognisedInflows, 0)
```

- **Stressed outflows** apply prescribed **run-off rates** to each liability/commitment category (BCBS LCR `urn:reg:bcbs:lcr:40.1`; Reg 26 run-off schedule):

| Funding category | Run-off rate |
|---|---|
| Retail deposits — stable | **3%** |
| Retail deposits — less stable | **10%** |
| Unsecured wholesale funding — operational | **25%** |
| Unsecured wholesale funding — non-operational | **up to 100%** |
| Secured funding — Level-1 collateral | **0%** |
| Secured funding — Level-2 collateral | **15%** |

- **Inflow-recognition cap.** Recognised inflows are **capped at 75% of gross outflows** (BCBS LCR `urn:reg:bcbs:lcr:40.77`; Reg 26(11)) — so that even a bank with very large contractual inflows must hold HQLA covering at least **25% of gross outflows** (the net-cash-outflow floor). This prevents over-reliance on inflows that may not materialise in stress.

## The 30-day stress horizon

The LCR is calibrated to a **single, acute, 30-calendar-day** stress scenario combining idiosyncratic and market-wide shocks (a partial loss of retail deposits, a partial loss of unsecured wholesale funding, a loss of secured short-term financing, contractual outflows from rating downgrades, increased market volatility affecting collateral, and unscheduled draws on committed facilities). The bank must hold enough HQLA to cover **net** cash outflows across the full 30 days under that scenario. The horizon is the LCR's defining feature versus the NSFR's 1-year structural horizon (BA 120 / Reg 26 NSFR section).

## Reporting cadence

- The LCR **measurement** is continuous; D1/2022 imposes a **continuous-maintenance** obligation — the bank must maintain an LCR ≥ 100% **from the first day of operations** (D1/2022 bind-trigger `COMMENCEMENT-BIND`, per register `ORG-PR-LCR-001..010`).
- Regulation 26 frames the return as a **monthly liquidity-risk return** (the regulation's summary describes *"the monthly liquidity risk return"*). D5/2025 §2.1.4 places BA 110 within the standing monthly return set.

## Material foreign-currency reporting

Regulation 26(2)(d) requires the return to capture, *"in respect of significant currencies, the reporting bank's exposure to foreign exchange."* D1/2022 specifies the currency treatment for the LCR scope of application (consolidation + significant-currency reporting). This is the Principle-5 (multi-currency) hook on the LCR return — significant currencies are monitored on their own LCR basis, with ZAR the presentation currency for the consolidated view.

## Binding obligations on the bank (summary)

1. **Maintain LCR ≥ 100%** continuously from commencement of operations — HQLA stock (post-haircut, post-cap) ≥ net 30-day stressed cash outflows (BCBS LCR 20.5; Reg 26; D1/2022).
2. **Classify HQLA into Level 1 / 2A / 2B**, apply the prescribed haircuts (0% / 15% / 25–50%) and the 40% Level-2 / 15% Level-2B caps (BCBS LCR 30.43; Reg 26(7)).
3. **Apply the prescribed run-off rates** to liabilities/commitments to compute stressed outflows (BCBS LCR 40.1; Reg 26).
4. **Cap recognised inflows at 75% of gross outflows** (the 25% net-outflow floor) (BCBS LCR 40.77; Reg 26(11)).
5. **Report significant-currency FX liquidity exposure** (Reg 26(2)(d); D1/2022 currency treatment).
6. **Submit form BA 110** to the PA in accordance with Annexures 3A/3B of D5/2025 and the Regulations, on the prescribed (monthly) cadence (D5/2025 §2.1.4; exact cadence counsel-gated).
7. **Retain a GL-reconciling audit trail** for the base models supporting the return, available to the PA on request (Reg 26(3)).
8. **Operate the RAS liquidity buffer** above the PA floor (internal gold-plate; D-RAS — the RAS targets a stricter operating buffer above 100%).
