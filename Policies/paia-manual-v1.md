## Preamble

Section 51 of the Promotion of Access to Information Act 2 of 2000 (PAIA) requires every private body to compile a manual describing the categories of records it holds, the manner in which records may be requested, the grounds for refusal, and related access-to-information infrastructure. This Manual fulfils that obligation for Hoz Bank Limited, Hoz Securities Limited, and Hoz Group Limited (collectively "the Bank" or "Hoz Group").

PAIA gives effect to the constitutional right of access to information in section 32 of the Constitution of the Republic of South Africa, 1996. This Manual is a public document. It is available free of charge at the Bank's registered address and on the Bank's intranet once constituted.

## Part 6 — How to request access to records

### 6.1 Who may request

Any person may request access to a record held by the Bank under PAIA s.11, provided the person:
- (a) is the record's subject (requestor is the data subject), or
- (b) requires the record for the exercise or protection of any rights.

Private bodies are not required to grant access to all records; requests must demonstrate a right that requires access to the record (PAIA s.50). The Bank will assess each request on this basis.

### 6.2 Prescribed form — Form C

All requests must be made using **Form C** (as prescribed under PAIA). Form C is available:
- From the Bank's Information Officer (contact details in Part 1)
- From the SAHRC website (www.sahrc.org.za)
- From the Department of Justice and Constitutional Development (www.justice.gov.za)

The completed Form C must be submitted to the Information Officer. A requestor who is unable to complete the Form for reasons of disability or illiteracy may request assistance from the Information Officer.

### 6.3 Submission method

| Method | Details |
|---|---|
| **Email** | io@hozbank.co.za [placeholder] — attach completed Form C |
| **Post** | Information Officer, [postal address — placeholder], South Africa |
| **Hand delivery** | At the Bank's registered address — during business hours (post licence-day) |

### 6.4 Required information in the request

Form C requires the requestor to provide:
1. Full name and contact details (postal address or email address)
2. Description of the record(s) requested with sufficient particularity to enable the record to be identified
3. The right the requestor seeks to exercise or protect, and the reasons the record is required
4. Preferred form of access (inspection, copy, extract, braille, audio, etc.)
5. Where the requestor acts on behalf of another person, proof of that authority

### 6.5 Fees

Fees are prescribed by the Minister of Justice under the PAIA Regulations (Government Gazette No. 31910 of 9 February 2009, as amended). The current prescribed fee schedule is:

| Fee type | Amount (per regulations) |
|---|---|
| Request fee (payable before processing begins) | R50.00 |
| Access fee — reproduction of records (per A4 page) | R1.10 per page (copy); R0.75 per page (search and preparation) |
| Access fee — computer-printed copy | R0.75 per page |
| Access fee — inspection | R30.00 per hour (or part thereof) |
| Deposit (where fee is likely to exceed R600.00) | One third of estimated total fee |

**Fee exemptions:** No request fee is payable if the requestor is a personal requester seeking their own personal information. No access fee is payable where the total cost of access is less than R10.00.

The Bank reserves the right to require advance payment of any deposit before commencing the search.

### 6.6 Response timeframes

| Stage | Timeframe |
|---|---|
| Acknowledgement of receipt | As soon as practicable after receipt |
| Decision on request | Within 30 days of receipt of the request (PAIA s.25(1)) |
| Extension (complex request) | Additional 30 days where 30 days is insufficient — requestor must be notified before expiry of initial period (PAIA s.26) |
| Total maximum | 60 days from receipt |

The 30-day period does not run if the requestor's identity needs to be established, if a third party needs to be consulted (PAIA ss.47–49), or if a deposit is required and has not yet been paid.

### 6.7 Form of access

The requestor may specify the preferred form of access in Form C:
- Inspection of the record
- Paper copy or electronic copy of the record
- Extract, transcription, or summary
- Braille copy, audio recording, or other accessible format

The Bank will provide access in the form requested unless doing so would unreasonably interfere with the Bank's operations or damage the record.

## Part 7 — Grounds for refusal

The Bank may or must refuse access in the following circumstances. All refusals are documented and notified to the requestor with reasons within the applicable timeframe.

### 7.1 Mandatory refusal (PAIA ss.34–38)

The Bank **must** refuse access where disclosure would:

| Ground | PAIA provision |
|---|---|
| Unreasonably disclose personal information of a third party (where the third party has not consented and privacy interests outweigh the public benefit) | s.34 |
| Reveal information supplied in confidence by a third party that meets the test for breach of confidence | s.36 |
| Jeopardise the law-enforcement or security interests listed in s.37 (investigation of criminal activity, national security) | s.37 |
| Disclose trade secrets or confidential commercial information of a third party where disclosure would cause harm | s.36 |
| Disclose Cabinet and Cabinet committee records | s.33 (not applicable to private bodies, listed for completeness) |

### 7.2 Discretionary refusal (PAIA ss.39–45)

The Bank **may** refuse access, in its discretion, where disclosure would:

| Ground | PAIA provision |
|---|---|
| Unreasonably prejudice commercial interests or trade secrets of the Bank itself | s.42 |
| Endanger the life or physical safety of an individual | s.41 |
| Cause harm to the banking system's integrity or financial stability (systemic risk) | s.42 read with the Banks Act |
| Reveal research information where premature disclosure would prejudice the research | s.45 |
| Be manifestly frivolous, vexatious, or repetitive | s.45 |

### 7.3 Severable access

Where part of a record may be refused on a ground above but the remainder does not attract refusal, the Bank will sever the protected portion and grant access to the remainder (PAIA s.28).

### 7.4 Deemed refusal

If the Bank fails to respond within the applicable timeframe, the request is **deemed to have been refused** (PAIA s.27). The requestor may then pursue the remedies in Part 9.

### 7.5 Special categories under POPIA

Where the record requested contains **special personal information** within the meaning of POPIA s.26 (health, biometric data, criminal offences, etc.) of a third party, the Bank will apply the mandatory PAIA s.34 personal-information ground. Access to such records will not ordinarily be granted without the express consent of the data subject, except where a PAIA s.34(2) override applies.

For a full description of how the Bank handles personal information and data subject rights, see the POPIA Privacy Policy at `Policies/popia-privacy-policy-v1.md`.

### 8.1 PAIA vs POPIA access rights

PAIA and POPIA provide complementary but distinct access rights:

| Dimension | PAIA | POPIA |
|---|---|---|
| Who may request | Any person who needs the record to exercise or protect a right | Data subjects — natural persons only — seeking their own personal information |
| What may be requested | Any category of record held by the body | Personal information of the requestor |
| Applicable entity | Public bodies (Part 2) and private bodies (Part 3) | Responsible parties holding personal information |
| Form required | Form C | No prescribed form; request to the Information Officer |
| Response period | 30 days (extendable to 60 days) | As soon as reasonably possible |
| Grounds for refusal | ss.33–45 grounds | POPIA ss.23–24 grounds |

Where a requestor is a natural person seeking their own personal information, the Bank will process the request under both PAIA and POPIA concurrently and will respond under the instrument most favourable to the requestor.

## Part 9 — Remedies

### 9.1 Internal appeal

There is no formal internal-appeal mechanism mandated by PAIA for private bodies. A requestor who is dissatisfied with a decision may contact the Information Officer to seek reconsideration, providing additional context or documentation that may affect the decision.

The Information Officer will reconsider the decision and provide a response within 15 business days of the reconsideration request.

### 9.2 Complaint to the Information Regulator

A requestor who is dissatisfied with the Bank's decision (including a deemed refusal) may lodge a complaint with the **Information Regulator** under PAIA s.56 and POPIA s.73.

| Contact | Details |
|---|---|
| **Information Regulator (South Africa)** | |
| Website | www.inforegulator.org.za |
| Email | inforeg@justice.gov.za |
| Postal address | JD House, 27 Stiemens Street, Braamfontein, Johannesburg, 2001 |
| PO Box | PO Box 31533, Braamfontein, 2017 |
| Telephone | 010 023 5207 |

The Information Regulator may investigate the complaint, facilitate resolution, or make a finding. Regulated bodies are bound by such findings.

### 9.3 Court application

A requestor who remains dissatisfied after approaching the Information Regulator may approach the **High Court** (or Magistrate's Court, where the claim falls within its jurisdiction) for appropriate relief, including an order compelling access or granting declaratory relief (PAIA s.82).

Court proceedings are available after a complaint to the Information Regulator has been resolved or a period of 30 days has elapsed without resolution.

### 9.4 SAHRC complaint

Separately from PAIA access complaints, any person may lodge a complaint with the **South African Human Rights Commission** regarding a violation of the right of access to information enshrined in s.32 of the Constitution. The SAHRC and the Information Regulator have distinct but overlapping mandates in this regard.
