## 3. Search-firm engagement

### 3.1 Selection — primary, secondary, reserve

PAX surfaced six channels in role-brief §7 and recruitment-scope §7. Of those, three are conventional executive-search firms with a SA banking-CRO track record: Spencer Stuart, Heidrick & Struggles, and Russell Reynolds. The remaining three (Egon Zehnder, Amrop, Jack Hammer) are surfaced as alternates with the following reasoning:

- **Egon Zehnder SA** — has placed senior risk seats but the SA bank-CRO bench is thinner than the three primaries; held in reserve if any of the primary three has a conflict (e.g. recently mandated by a competitor SA bank for a related search).
- **Amrop SA** — boutique with strong SA financial-services-but-not-banking placement record; not the strongest fit for Priority-1 bank-CRO with PA fit-and-proper engagement.
- **Jack Hammer SA** — strong on culture-fit and executive-leadership work but does not specialise in regulator-engaged designated-officer placements; not selected for Priority-1.

**Selected primary:** **Spencer Stuart SA — financial-services practice.**

Justification:

1. Documented SA tier-2 / boutique-bank CRO placements within recent memory (PAX's role-brief §7.1 surfaces Spencer Stuart, Heidrick & Struggles, and Russell Reynolds as the three with that track record).
2. Strong alumni-network reach into the SARB Prudential Authority alumni pool, which is one of the four sourcing channels in §5 of this plan.
3. Reputational standing with SARB PA — vendor-of-record status with PA does not exist as a formal designation, but PA staff working through fit-and-proper engagement on shortlists do read vendor identity as a proxy for diligence rigour. This is read-across from Imani's external-counsel-engagement work (`Owner Inbox/2026-05-09_imani_external-counsel-licence-application.md`); the same reputational discipline applies on search.
4. Capacity and attention — Spencer Stuart's SA financial-services practice is sized to take on a Priority-1 mandate at this seat seniority without slotting it into a rotation.

**Selected secondary (competitive brief):** **Heidrick & Struggles SA — banking practice.**

Justification: PAX role-brief §7.1 recommends a two-vendor competitive brief (default-if-no-answer in §9 question 6). Heidrick & Struggles is the second-strongest candidate against the same criteria. The competitive-brief mechanic is described in §3.2.

**Reserve:** **Russell Reynolds SA — financial-services practice.** Held in reserve and not briefed at the outset to preserve vendor-relationship capital and to avoid over-mapping of the candidate market (PAX role-brief §7.1 explicit caution: vendor-fatigue reduces the candidate pool over time).

## 5. Sourcing channels

Four channels operate in parallel. The two retained vendors (Spencer Stuart, Heidrick & Struggles) work channels (a)–(c); Nolan and Owen operate channel (d) directly.

### 5.1 Channel (a) — SA bank-CRO alumni map

The conventional-bank-CRO alumni pool at tier-1 and strong tier-2 SA banks: Standard Bank, Absa, Nedbank, FirstRand, Investec, Capitec. The vendors map sitting and recently-departed CROs and Deputy-CROs at these banks. Coverage target: complete map of the sitting cohort plus the past-five-years departures.

**Concentration-risk filter.** Candidates currently at a SA bank that is a likely market counterparty for the bank's institutional global-markets posture (Standard Bank Markets, Absa CIB, RMB / FirstRand Markets, Investec Markets, Nedbank CIB) require explicit concentration-risk articulation under King IV Principle 11. The articulation goes to Imani (Legal-as-code engineer) for conflicts-and-related-party register entry before first interview. A candidate is not auto-disqualified by current employment at a counterparty bank, but the conflict must be governable through a clean break (notice + gardening leave + non-compete).

### 5.2 Channel (b) — IRM-SA Fellow pool

The Institute of Risk Management South Africa (IRM-SA) Fellow-grade members are a reasonable cross-section of the SA risk-management senior community. The vendors approach IRM-SA for an introduction-set (not a candidate list — IRM-SA does not operate as a search firm). PAX's role-brief §3 fluency requirements anchor the screen.

### 5.3 Channel (c) — SARB Prudential Authority alumni network

Ex-PA / ex-SARB risk-supervision seniors are a strong-fit pool — direct supervisory fluency and PA-engagement credibility. Two structural cautions:

1. **Cooling-off practice.** PAX role-brief §8.1 flags `[citation: TBC]` on the exact PA cooling-off practice for ex-PA-staff appointments to bank designated-officer seats. Imani confirms this with external counsel at fit-and-proper file assembly **before** any ex-PA-staff candidate is progressed to a Marc-final-interview. Until that confirmation is back, ex-PA-staff candidates are tracked in pipeline but not advanced past first interview.
2. **Independence considerations.** Ex-PA seniors are strong-fit on prudential-supervisor credibility but the substantive-independence read of Joint Standard 1 of 2024 §7 includes prior-relationship considerations — a candidate whose recent PA seat was the supervisory contact for the bank's licence-application engagement is structurally compromised even after cooling-off. Vendors filter on this; Owen (Company Secretary, governance) cross-checks against the bank's recorded PA-engagement contacts list.

The vendors approach this channel via Owen's professional network plus the ex-PA-staff register Imani maintains in the conflicts-and-related-party register substrate.

### 5.4 Channel (d) — Nolan + Owen direct-sourcing list

A 12–20 named-candidate direct-sourcing list, built by Nolan from publicly-available signals (LinkedIn, SARB BA-return signatories, ICAAP / ILAAP attribution where disclosed in regulator engagement, IRM-SA membership records) and Owen's professional network. Two purposes:

1. **Benchmark cross-check** against the vendor longlists — if the vendor maps differ materially from the direct-sourcing list, that is a signal of either vendor over-mapping (over-counted on a single network) or vendor under-coverage (missed the direct-sourcing names).
2. **Direct approach** to the small subset of candidates the vendors have over-mapped or under-approached; coordinated with the vendors to avoid double-approach.

## 6. Screening rubric

The screening rubric scores each candidate on a 1–5 scale across nine dimensions mapped to role-brief §3 must-haves and recruitment-scope §7. Hard fails on any single dimension are documented and the candidate is exited.

### 6.1 Scoring matrix

| # | Dimension | Source | Weight | Hard-fail trigger |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Prudential supervision exposure (10+ years SARB-regulated bank or comparable) | Role-brief §3 must-have | 15% | < 10 years senior risk-management exposure in a regulated bank — automatic exit |
| 2 | Prior CRO or Deputy-CRO seat | Role-brief §3 must-have | 15% | No prior CRO or Deputy-CRO seat in a regulated bank — exit (subject to role-brief §9 question 3 if CEO opens non-bank-CRO lane) |
| 3 | ICAAP / ILAAP signing experience | Role-brief §3 must-have | 12% | Has not personally signed ICAAP / ILAAP for a regulated bank — exit |
| 4 | RAS+RAF authorship (SAM-style) | Recruitment scope; role-brief §2.1, §2.3 | 10% | No RAS authorship track record — exit |
| 5 | BCBS-CG-Principles fluency | Role-brief §3 must-have | 10% | Cannot articulate substantive-independence test under BCBS 2015 §3 in interview — exit |
| 6 | Joint Standard 1 of 2024 fluency | Role-brief §3 must-have | 10% | Cannot articulate operational-independence test under JS 1/2024 §7 in interview — exit |
| 7 | Fit-and-proper hard floors (Banks Act + PA + JS 1/2024) | Role-brief §4; Banks Act s.60B | 10% | Any prior fit-and-proper finding at SARB PA, FSCA, or comparable-regime regulator — automatic exit |
| 8 | Conflicts / concentration-risk governability | Mira+Zara concentration-risk paper; King IV Principle 11 | 8% | Concurrent directorship at competitor bank or material counterparty that cannot be governed — exit |
| 9 | Cultural fit with autonomous-by-default operating model | Role-brief §3 nice-to-have; Principle 7 | 10% | Active hostility to AI-substrate operating model in interview — exit (subject to CEO override; rare) |

**Total weight 100%.** Composite score is the weighted sum on the 1–5 scale, producing a 0–5 number. Cut for shortlist progression: **composite ≥ 3.5 with no hard fail on any dimension.**

### 6.2 Hard fails — automatic exit

The hard-fail triggers in the matrix above are reinforced by these standalone exits documented separately on the candidate file:

- Fit-and-proper failure under Banks Act s.60B (or comparable-regime equivalent) — automatic exit.
- Concentration-risk in current employer that creates a structural conflict not closeable through clean break — automatic exit.
- Non-SA-resident with no credible relocation pathway closeable before licence-application lodgment — automatic exit (subject to role-brief §9 question 2 if CEO opens SA + region lane).
- Failed reference check on integrity / honesty — automatic exit.
- Insolvency, criminal record, or non-clean tax-compliance — automatic exit per Banks Act + PA fit-and-proper file structure.

### 6.3 Documentation

Each candidate's screening file is assembled in a per-candidate folder (post-substrate-build: a CandidateRecord projection over the recruitment-pipeline event log; today: a Scrooge-coordinated set of files in `Team Inbox/recruitment/human-cro/<candidate-anonymised-id>/`). The screening file contains:

- The vendor's longlist note and evidence package.
- The Nolan-completed scoring matrix with each dimension's score and supporting evidence-pointers.
- The conflicts-and-related-party check from Imani.
- The fit-and-proper preliminary screen against publicly-available signals (final fit-and-proper file is assembled by Imani + Owen at the licence-application gate).
- The interview notes (after each interview gate).
- The references file.

## 7. Interview / decision pathway

Four interviewers, four roles. Each gate is documented; no candidate progresses without all four sign-offs at the relevant gate.

### 7.1 Interviewers

| Interviewer | Role in the process | Position pairing |
|---|---|---|
| Marc | Final decision authority; cultural-fit and CEO-relationship interview | Marc (CEO) |
| Helena | Technical-fit interview — depth on engineering-CRO outputs the human CRO will oversee | Helena (Chief Risk Officer, engineering — incumbent) |

### 7.2 Helena's role specifically — conflict-managed technical-fit

Helena (Chief Risk Officer, engineering — incumbent) interviews each shortlisted candidate on **technical depth**, not on **ranking**. The conflict to manage: the human Chief Risk Officer this hire produces will oversee Helena's own engineering-CRO outputs (per `Team/Helena.md` §3, dotted line in current substrate, solid line at licence-day).

**Conflict mitigation:**

1. Helena's interview produces a **technical-depth assessment** on a 1–5 scale across the engineering-CRO interlock dimensions (model-risk governance fluency, ICAAP / ILAAP signing depth, IFRS 9 ECL governance, RAS appetite-line operationalisation, stress-testing scenario adequacy, supervisory-letter response fluency). It does **not** produce a ranking.
2. Devon (COO, governance) and Marc (CEO) own ranking decisions; Helena's input is one of three technical inputs (alongside the screening-rubric composite and the references file).
3. Helena's assessment is documented and lodged in the candidate file. If a candidate is rejected on Helena's technical-depth assessment, the rejection is reviewable by Devon — Helena cannot unilaterally exit a candidate.
4. If Helena identifies a candidate as a **Helena-incumbency-replacement** signal (i.e. a candidate who would, in Helena's read, prefer to operate without an engineering-CRO substrate), Helena flags the signal to Devon for governance discussion. The signal is **information**, not a veto — the substantive-independence test under Joint Standard 1 of 2024 §7 attaches to the human seat's relationship to the CEO, not to the engineering-CRO substrate.

This design follows the role-brief §5.4 substantive-independence safeguard: independence-affecting events are pre-decided with Thandiwe (Chief Audit Executive, governance) at licence-day; pre-licence-day, Devon (COO, governance) carries the analogue.

### 7.3 Gates

| # | Gate | Owner | Inputs | Output |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Vendor longlist review | Nolan + Devon | Vendor longlist (~12–20 candidates per vendor, deduplicated against the Nolan + Owen direct-sourcing list) | Filtered longlist (~8–12 candidates) progressed to vendor-side screening |
| 2 | Vendor-screened shortlist | Nolan | Vendor-screened candidates with screening-rubric scores | 5–7 candidates progressed to bank-side first interview |
| 3 | First interview (Devon + Helena) | Devon (chair) | Candidate, screening file | Each candidate either advanced or exited; advanced cohort ≤ 5 |
| 4 | Reference + due-diligence + preliminary-fit-and-proper screen | Nolan + Imani + Owen | Advanced cohort | Cleared cohort ≤ 5 |
| 5 | SARB PA fit-and-proper pre-vetting alignment (per §10) | Owen + Imani + external counsel | Cleared cohort | Pre-vetted cohort ≤ 3 |
| 6 | Marc-final interview | Marc (CEO) | Pre-vetted cohort | Selected candidate (1) |
| 7 | Offer + engagement-letter close | Devon + Owen + Imani + Camille | Selected candidate; Camille's compensation band; Imani's engagement-letter draft | Signed offer letter |
| 8 | Notice-period bridge + onboarding plan | Devon + Owen | Signed offer | Seat-filled date confirmed; lodging-readiness gate cleared |

**Independence safeguard.** No CEO involvement in gates 1–5. The CEO's first formal touch is at gate 6 (final interview). This is a Joint Standard 1 of 2024 §7 read-across: the human CRO's appointment process should not be CEO-dominated, and the CEO does not see candidates until the field is pre-vetted and reduced to a Board-defensible final cohort.

### 7.4 Decision rule

The selected candidate at gate 6 is the candidate with:

- Highest composite screening score (gate 2); **and**
- No hard fails (gates 1–5); **and**
- Marc's final-interview approval (gate 6); **and**
- Cleared SARB PA fit-and-proper pre-vetting (gate 5).

Where two candidates score within 0.3 on the composite and both clear all gates, Marc's final-interview judgement is decisive (this is the residual that an autonomous system cannot make per Principle 7; the CEO sits in the residual).

## 8. Compensation framing

> Specific numbers are deferred to Camille (Chief Financial Officer, governance) per role-brief §6.4 and recruitment-scope §6 hand-off. This section restates the indicative band shape from role-brief §6 so vendors and candidates engage with the comparator-class anchor without requiring CEO-disclosable numbers at the vendor-brief gate.

### 8.1 Comparator class

- **Anchor:** SA tier-2 / boutique trading-bank Chief Risk Officer comparator class (role-brief §6.1).
- **Below:** big-four-SA-bank CRO compensation (Standard Bank, Absa, Nedbank, FirstRand) — the seat does not sit in that band given the bank's R300m-target capital posture and pre-licence stage.
- **Above:** generalist-corporate-CRO compensation — the seat is named-regulatory-accountable under Banks Act, which lifts it materially above non-bank risk-officer roles.
- **Adjacent comparators (negotiation context only):** SA insurance-group CROs of comparable size; building-society CROs; foreign-bank-branch CROs operating in SA. Useful for negotiation framing; the band-anchor remains bank-CRO comparators.

### 8.2 Package shape

- **Cash component:** base salary at the comparator-class median; variable cash component limited and risk-adjusted per BCBS Principles for Sound Compensation Practices (variable cannot dominate; clawback applicable on subsequent risk-decision quality assessment).
- **Long-term incentive (LTI):** at the bank's small scale and pre-licence stage, LTI is structured as a multi-year deferred-cash arrangement with risk-adjusted vesting (no equity available pre-licence; equity-equivalent post-licence subject to BRC + Camille design). Vesting is subject to no fit-and-proper findings during the vesting window.
- **Sign-on / retention:** a sign-on retention split across one-, two-, and three-year anniversaries makes economic sense at this stage given the bank's pre-revenue posture.
- **Notice and gardening leave:** 6-month notice on either side, with a gardening-leave clause Imani drafts to protect non-compete on the institutional global-markets vertical.

### 8.3 Risk-adjusted deferral

Per BCBS Principles for Sound Compensation Practices and the role-brief §6.3 read, variable cash and LTI both vest subject to: (a) no material risk-event traceable to the human Chief Risk Officer's decision-making during the vesting window; (b) no PA fit-and-proper finding against the candidate during the vesting window; (c) no third-line (Vera / Thandiwe) finding of governance failure traceable to the seat. Camille sets the deferral percentages.

### 8.4 What the CEO sets (deferred to D-HUMAN-CRO-COMP)

Per role-brief §6.4, three CEO-level inputs feed into the band before Camille completes the framing:

1. The absolute compensation band (Camille's input, but the ceiling is a CEO-direction item — role-brief §9 question 1).
2. Whether there is geographic flexibility (role-brief §9 question 2).
3. Whether the package can be inflated to reach a candidate not currently sitting (role-brief §9 question 5 — big-four step-down candidates).

These three directional answers feed Camille's band; the band feeds the vendor brief. Until they are resolved, the vendor brief carries the indicative band shape only.

## 10. Regulator pre-vetting alignment

The SARB Prudential Authority does not formally pre-vet shortlisted candidates ahead of formal application; the fit-and-proper file is reviewed at licence-application lodgment. However, **structural pre-vetting** through informal channels and through Imani's external-counsel engagement is the pragmatic path:

### 10.1 Pre-vetting mechanism

- **Imani + external counsel** review each pre-vetted-cohort candidate's fit-and-proper file against the PA's known fit-and-proper expectations under Banks Act + PA fit-and-proper sub-instruments (register row ORG-GV-11). The PAX role-brief §11 flags the exact PA directive number as `[citation: TBC]` — counsel resolves at this gate.
- **Owen** runs the conflicts-and-related-party register entry against the candidate's declared conflicts and against the bank's recorded counterparty list.
- **Mira (Compliance / RegTech engineer) + Zara (Chief Compliance Officer, governance)** review the candidate against the Joint Standard 1 of 2024 §7 operational-independence test: does the candidate's prior employer, current relationships, and proposed engagement-letter terms constitute a structural-independence challenge?
- **External counsel** runs the substantive-independence read of BCBS Corporate Governance Principles 2015 §3.

### 10.2 Why pre-vetting before Marc-final interview

The Joint Standard 1 of 2024 §7 risk is that a candidate enters Marc's final-interview, is selected, and is then surfaced as fit-and-proper-defective at lodgment. This produces:

1. A delay in licence-application lodgment (the seat is unfilled at lodgment, returning the bank to the interim-CRO-as-CEO posture the priority-1 sequencing was designed to avoid).
2. A signal to SARB PA that the bank's fit-and-proper screening process is itself defective — read across from the Joint Standard 1 of 2024 §6 responsible-person designation.

Pre-vetting at gate 5 (before Marc-final-interview) means: the only candidates Marc sees at gate 6 are candidates who have cleared every fit-and-proper structural test in advance. The CEO does not select a candidate the regulator will subsequently reject.

### 10.3 Pre-vetting deliverable

The pre-vetting deliverable per candidate is a one-page memo from Imani, signed by Owen, that declares:

- Fit-and-proper structural test (Banks Act + PA + JS 1/2024) — pass / conditional / fail.
- Substantive-independence read (BCBS 2015 §3) — pass / conditional / fail.
- Conflicts-and-related-party register entry — clean / conditioned / blocking.
- External-counsel sign-off.

Only candidates with `pass / pass / clean` (or with conditions resolved before gate 6) progress to Marc-final interview.

## 13. Substrate gaps surfaced

Per Principle 7 (autonomous-by-default), this plan surfaces the substrate gaps that prevent a fully-autonomous run of the recruitment-execution agent. Each gap is a roadmap item, not a hidden constraint.

### 13.1 Automated fit-and-proper screening pipeline

**Gap:** No automated pipeline screens candidate names against the SARB Prudential Authority and FSCA disqualification registers, against the SA insolvency register, against the SA criminal-record register, against the SARS tax-compliance register, or against the international fit-and-proper findings databases (FCA, PRA, OCC, ECB, APRA, MAS).

**Today's substitute:** Imani (Legal-as-code engineer) runs a manual screen at gate 4 (preliminary fit-and-proper) and external counsel cross-validates at gate 5 (SARB PA pre-vetting alignment).

**Why a substrate gap:** A human-officer fit-and-proper attestation pipeline is referenced in recruitment-scope §9 (substrate gaps) as Sade + Imani + Owen co-owned; target pre-licence-application. The same pipeline serves recruitment screening and ongoing fit-and-proper attestation post-appointment.

**Owners:** Sade (HR engineer — AgentOps reshape; human-HR slice activates at licence-day per CLAUDE.md operating model), Imani (Legal-as-code engineer), Owen (Company Secretary, governance).

**Dependency:** This pipeline is also required for the other five thin-human-layer hires; building it once for this Priority-1 search amortises across the programme.

### 13.2 Recruiter-to-CEO typed escalation channel

**Gap:** Per Principle 7, every agent has a typed escalation channel to a named human overseer. Today, Nolan's escalation to Marc (CEO) runs through Scrooge as in-session orchestration — a Scrooge-coordinated tick rather than a typed channel.

**Today's substitute:** Scrooge-coordinated session escalation with explicit Owner Inbox brief.

**Why a substrate gap:** The agent runtime substrate (Principle 7 foundational substrate alongside event store, obligations register, IaC) includes the typed escalation channel. It is roadmap-level, not session-level.

**Owners:** Atlas (core banking platform architect — runtime substrate); Scrooge orchestrates the interim.

### 13.3 Candidate-pipeline projection over a recruitment-pipeline event log

**Gap:** No event-sourced recruitment-pipeline domain exists in the prototype. CandidateRecord, RecruitmentMandateOpened, CandidateScreened, CandidateInterviewed, CandidateOffered, CandidateDeclined / CandidateAccepted events are absent.

**Today's substitute:** Per-candidate file folders in `Team Inbox/recruitment/human-cro/<candidate-anonymised-id>/`; manual aggregation into a Nolan-curated pipeline view.

**Why a substrate gap:** Per Principle 1 (events as source of truth), the candidate pipeline should be a projection over an event log. Per Principle 6 (single-graph discipline), the recruitment-execution domain should sit in the prototype alongside the other agent-owned domains. Per Principle 7, the recruiter agent should run continuously over the pipeline event log, not session-per-search.

**Owners:** Atlas (core banking platform architect — domain scaffolding); Nolan (recruiter agent — domain-handler authorship); Anya (Data / analytics engineer — projection runtime).

**Target:** Post-runtime, but a minimal MVP (the four candidate-state-transition events) can land before the second of the six hires opens to amortise the substrate across the programme.

### 13.4 SARB / FSCA disqualification register integration

**Gap:** The SARB and FSCA disqualification registers are not integrated into the bank's substrate. Manual lookup is the substitute.

**Today's substitute:** Manual lookup at gate 4 and gate 5; external counsel cross-validation.

**Why a substrate gap:** This is a sub-component of §13.1 above but called out separately because the SARB / FSCA register integration is a known-vendor-data-feed work item that can be scheduled independently of the broader fit-and-proper pipeline.

**Owners:** Mira (Compliance / RegTech engineer — register integration); Imani.
