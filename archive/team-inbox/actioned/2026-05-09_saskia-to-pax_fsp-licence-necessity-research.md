## 1. The question

**Under what conditions does the bank's institutional-only / wholesale posture require an FSP licence under FAIS Act 37 of 2002 at all?**

The bank's strategic foundation (`project_strategic_foundation.md`) is an institutional global-markets dealer — JSE bonds and equities plus OTC interest-rate derivatives, transacting with institutional / professional counterparties only, single-branch in South Africa. The build-phase posture records *"no FSP licence pursued in interim"*. Whether an FSP licence is required at all at commencement-of-trading is the open question this dispatch routes.

The question has four sub-parts:

### 1.1 What does FAIS scope cover for an institutional-only / wholesale dealer?

- The FAIS Act binds providers of *advice* and *intermediary services* to *clients* in respect of *financial products* (FAIS Act s.1 definitions; s.7 FSP licence requirement) `[citation: TBC — exact section references and any subsequent amendments to be confirmed by PAX]`.
- Institutional / professional counterparties may be wholly or partly exempt under FAIS subordinate legislation `[citation: TBC — Mira + Zara reference s.45-related exemptions in their conduct paper row Q4; PAX confirms the exact Subordinate Legislation citation, the exemption mechanism, and the conditions]`.
- Clarify: does the institutional-only counterparty profile (banks, asset managers, hedge funds, corporates above an asset / turnover threshold, regulated insurers, government / SOE treasury) fall inside or outside FAIS scope?

### 1.2 Does the bank's product set drive a different reading?

- Product set: JSE-listed bonds (cash); JSE-listed equities (cash); OTC interest-rate derivatives (IRS, FRA, basis swaps, possibly OIS).
- Each is a *"financial product"* under FAIS Act s.1 read with the Categories of FSP determination `[citation: TBC]`. But the *intermediary-services* and *advice* definitions, as applied to institutional / professional counterparties, are the binding tests — not the product status alone.
- Does dealing-on-own-account vs agency vs principal-broker matter for the FSP-scope question? An institutional dealer that runs principal positions and quotes prices to counterparties is a different shape from an agency broker or an advisor; the FSP-licensing implications differ.

### 1.3 Is the FSCA's "wholesale FSP" / institutional-FSP framework relevant?

- The FSCA has published guidance and category-specific licensing approaches `[citation: TBC — PAX confirms the current FSCA category-of-FSP guidance, the wholesale / institutional-FSP framework if any, and whether Conduct Standards 1–3 of 2018 (already cited in obligations register Domain M) shape the licensing-scope question separately from the activity scope]`.
- Conduct Standards on OTC Derivative Providers (FSCA Conduct Standard 2 of 2020 and the 9 June 2023 amendments, already in the obligations register Domain M) impose ODP-specific obligations independent of FAIS — clarify the FAIS-vs-ODP-vs-banking-licence interaction.

### 1.4 Forward-compatibility with COFI

- The Conduct of Financial Institutions Bill (COFI) reshapes conduct licensing toward activity-based licensing rather than category-based licensing `[citation: TBC — PAX confirms COFI's current passage status as of 2026-05-09 and its FAIS-replacement timeline]`. Even if the answer today is "no FSP licence required", does the COFI activity-licensing model change the answer — and does the bank need to register against an activity that COFI will recognise but FAIS does not?

### 4.3 Comparable-jurisdiction precedent

The institutional-only / wholesale-only licensing perimeter is a recurring shape across Anglosphere financial-services regimes; precedent is informative but not binding. Examples:

- **United Kingdom — FCA Handbook, COBS 3 (Client categorisation).** Eligible counterparty / professional-client / retail-client tripartite distinction; the way the UK handles institutional-only dealers (no client-money rules; reduced suitability; reduced communications obligations). PAX captures the structural shape, not the binding force.
- **Australia — Corporations Act 2001 / ASIC AFS licensing, "wholesale client" definition (s.761G).** Threshold-based wholesale client exclusion from the retail conduct overlay.
- **European Union — MiFID II Annex II (per se / elective professional clients; eligible counterparties).** The conduct-rule reduction for institutional and ECP interactions.
- **United States — institutional-investor exemptions in advisers / broker-dealer registration.** Different regime (IAA / Exchange Act) but precedent on activity-based vs counterparty-based exemptions is informative.

For each comparable, PAX captures: (a) the regime's institutional / wholesale exemption mechanism; (b) examples of institutional-only banks operating without retail-conduct registration; (c) any structural lessons for the FAIS reading.
