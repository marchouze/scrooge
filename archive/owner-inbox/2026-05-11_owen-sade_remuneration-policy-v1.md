## 1. Overarching Remuneration Policy

### 1.1 Purpose

This policy establishes the bank's philosophy, governance framework, and structural requirements for remuneration of all natural persons covered by its scope. It is designed to:

- promote conduct that is consistent with the bank's long-term interests, risk appetite, and capital adequacy;
- align the interests of directors, executives, key individuals, and Material Risk Takers (MRTs) with those of the bank, its shareholders, and its counterparties;
- prevent incentive structures that encourage excessive risk-taking inconsistent with the bank's Risk Appetite Statement and Framework (RAS);
- meet disclosure standards required under the Banks Act 94 of 1990, King IV Report on Corporate Governance (King IV), and BCBS Sound Compensation Practices and related FSB Implementation Standards.

### 1.2 Regulatory and governance foundations

This policy is grounded in the following sources, in descending order of authority:

| Source | Specific provision |
|---|---|
| Banks Act 94 of 1990 | s.60B (Remuneration disclosure); Regulation 39 (Director fees) |
| Regulations Relating to Banks | Part VII — Remuneration and Governance |
| King IV Report on Corporate Governance | Principle 14 (Remuneration) |
| BCBS Sound Compensation Practices (2010) | Compensation Principles 1–8 + Standards 1–3 |
| FSB Implementation Standards (2009) | Variable compensation deferral, malus, clawback |
| Companies Act 71 of 2008 | s.30(4), s.66(8)–(9) (Director fees; shareholder approval); s.72 (Board committees) |
| Employment Equity Act 55 of 1998 | s.27 (Pay equity — activates at licence-day) |

### 1.3 Build-phase note

During the build phase, the bank has no human employees and pays no remuneration. AI agents are not natural persons and are not remunerated. This policy is authored for the licence dossier; it becomes operative at licence-day when the thin human layer is appointed. All provisions in this policy are LICENCE-BIND.

### 1.4 Policy status hierarchy

In the event of inconsistency between this policy and a lower-order instrument (an individual employment contract, a RemCo resolution, or an administrative procedure), this policy prevails. In the event of inconsistency between this policy and applicable law or regulation, applicable law or regulation prevails, and Owen (Company Secretary, governance) initiates a policy amendment within 30 days.

## 2. Scope

### 2.1 Covered persons

This policy applies to the following categories of natural person:

**Non-executive directors (NEDs)**  
Members of the Board who do not hold executive office. NEDs receive director fees only; they do not participate in variable remuneration, short-term incentive (STI), or long-term incentive (LTI) schemes. Director fees are subject to shareholder approval under Companies Act s.66(8).

**Executive directors**  
Directors who hold an executive role (CEO, CFO, or such other positions as the Board designates). Executive directors receive fixed remuneration, may receive variable remuneration, and are subject to MRT requirements if designated as such.

**Key individuals (as defined under FAIS)**  
Persons who manage or control the rendering of financial services under a licence or who are responsible for supervising such rendering. Key individuals are identified by Zara (Chief Compliance Officer, governance) in the FAIS key-individual register and are subject to enhanced fit-and-proper monitoring under the Fit-and-Proper Policy.

**Material Risk Takers (MRTs)**  
Natural persons whose professional activities have a material impact on the bank's risk profile. MRT designation criteria and deferral requirements are set out in Section 5.

**Other employees**  
All other natural persons employed under a contract of employment at licence-day or subsequently.

### 2.2 Out of scope

**AI agents** are not natural persons. They do not receive remuneration, benefits, equity, or any other form of compensation. Agent operating costs (API compute, infrastructure) are budgeted and governed separately as operational expenditure under Camille (Chief Financial Officer, governance)'s mandate.

**Contractors and service providers** engaged under commercial contracts are not employees and are not governed by this policy; they are governed by the Procurement Policy and the relevant contract terms.

### 2.3 Thin-human-layer composition

At licence-day, the statutory minimum human layer is expected to comprise approximately 6–10 individuals: the CEO (Marc, simultaneously acting as founding director), at minimum a CFO, CRO, MLRO/FIC Compliance Officer, and Information Officer, and the appointed external auditor engagement partner (who is not an employee). The precise list is determined by the pre-licence go-live readiness gate co-owned by Saskia (Head of Global Markets, governance), Helena (Chief Risk Officer, governance), and Devon (Chief Operating Officer, governance). This policy governs all natural persons in that set from licence-day.

## 3. Remuneration Committee (RemCo)

### 3.1 Establishment and obligation closure

The establishment of a Remuneration Committee is required by:
- King IV Principle 14 and recommended practice 57
- BCBS Sound Compensation Practice — Principle 1 (active oversight by the board)
- Obligation ORG-GV-08 (Remuneration Committee established)

RemCo is a committee of the Board. It is formally constituted at the inaugural Board meeting at or before licence-day.

### 3.2 Composition

RemCo shall consist of:

- A minimum of three members, all of whom are non-executive directors.
- A majority of independent non-executive directors.
- A chairperson who is an independent non-executive director (and not the Board chairperson, unless otherwise permissible under King IV).

The CEO and CFO attend RemCo meetings by standing invitation but are not members and withdraw during deliberations on their own remuneration.

Helena (Chief Risk Officer, governance) attends all RemCo meetings in an advisory capacity to provide risk-alignment input on variable remuneration proposals. Helena has no vote.

Zara (Chief Compliance Officer, governance) attends as required to provide regulatory-compliance input.

Owen (Company Secretary, governance) acts as secretary to RemCo and maintains the committee register, minutes, and action log.

### 3.3 Interim arrangement — Board acting as RemCo

Until RemCo is formally constituted (i.e., until sufficient independent NEDs are appointed to constitute the committee), the Board as a whole assumes RemCo's functions and may not delegate them to management. All resolutions that would ordinarily require RemCo approval require full Board approval during this interim period. Marc (CEO), acting simultaneously as executive director, declares a conflict and withdraws from Board deliberations on his own remuneration; decisions on CEO remuneration during the interim period are made by the Board excluding the CEO.

### 3.4 Mandate

RemCo's mandate includes:

- approving the Remuneration Policy and recommending material changes to the Board;
- approving the remuneration framework and structures for executive directors and MRTs;
- approving individual total compensation packages for executive directors and MRTs;
- approving the performance targets against which variable remuneration is assessed;
- approving the outcome of variable remuneration assessments, including the application of deferral, malus, and clawback;
- reviewing NED fee proposals for recommendation to shareholders (RemCo does not set its own fees);
- overseeing pay-equity reviews and remuneration disclosure in the annual report;
- reviewing this policy annually and recommending amendments to the Board.

RemCo may not delegate any of the above functions to management. RemCo may commission independent external remuneration advisers; any such adviser reports to RemCo and has no management relationship with the bank.

### 3.5 Cadence

RemCo meets at least twice per financial year:
- **Annual remuneration meeting** (H1 of each financial year): reviews policy; sets performance targets for the current year; approves fixed remuneration adjustments.
- **Performance outcome meeting** (year-end + grace period): assesses variable remuneration outcomes; approves awards, deferrals, malus adjustments, and clawbacks.

Ad-hoc meetings may be convened by the chairperson on matters requiring timely resolution (e.g., hiring package approval for an executive appointment).

### 3.6 Independence safeguards

No member of RemCo may:
- vote on their own remuneration (members withdraw on matters relating to their own fees);
- hold unvested variable awards that could be materially affected by a current agenda item;
- receive remuneration advice from an adviser who has a management-side relationship with the bank in the same period.

## 4. Remuneration Structure Principles

### 4.1 Total compensation framework

Total compensation for covered persons is composed of three elements:

| Element | Description | Applies to |
|---|---|---|
| **Fixed remuneration** | Base salary / director fees. Reflects role, seniority, market value, and individual capabilities. Set at appointment; reviewed annually. | All covered persons |
| **Variable remuneration** | Performance-linked award. Assessed annually against pre-agreed financial, risk, compliance, and strategic metrics. For MRTs: subject to mandatory deferral, malus, and clawback. | Executives, key individuals, MRTs, other employees (at RemCo discretion) |
| **Benefits** | Medical aid, retirement fund contributions, risk-cover benefits, and such other benefits as approved by RemCo from time to time. | Employees (not NEDs) |

NEDs receive director fees only and do not participate in variable remuneration or benefit schemes, consistent with King IV recommended practice and to preserve independence.

### 4.2 Risk alignment

The bank's remuneration structures must at all times be consistent with its RAS (Risk Appetite Statement and Framework, owned by Helena (Chief Risk Officer, governance)).

Specific requirements:
- Variable remuneration for MRTs must not reward outcomes achieved by exceeding the bank's risk appetite.
- Performance metrics for variable remuneration must include risk and compliance dimensions alongside financial dimensions (Section 7).
- The deferral, malus, and clawback framework (Section 5) is the primary structural mechanism for intertemporal risk alignment.
- Helena (Chief Risk Officer, governance) reviews all proposed variable remuneration outcomes before RemCo approval and provides a written risk-alignment attestation.

### 4.3 No excessive risk-taking incentives

Remuneration structures must not create incentives for covered persons to:
- take on risk beyond the bank's approved risk appetite;
- misrepresent the nature, magnitude, or timing of risk exposures;
- engage in conduct that prioritises short-term financial performance over the bank's long-term soundness or franchise value;
- circumvent regulatory requirements.

RemCo reviews proposed remuneration structures for compliance with this principle before adoption. Helena (Chief Risk Officer, governance) provides an independent risk-alignment assessment of any materially new remuneration structure before it is approved.

### 4.4 Pay-for-performance

Variable remuneration is pay-for-performance. Performance is assessed against:
- pre-agreed metrics set before the start of the relevant performance period;
- both quantitative and qualitative dimensions;
- outcomes that reflect risk-adjusted returns, not gross returns.

RemCo exercises informed discretion in approving variable remuneration outcomes. Mechanical formulae do not override RemCo's judgment where outcomes do not reflect genuine performance or where risk events have occurred.

### 4.5 Pay equity

Owen (Company Secretary, governance) and Sade (AgentOps, operations) conduct an annual pay-equity review at or after licence-day. The review assesses whether remuneration differentials reflect legitimate differentiators (role, seniority, performance, market benchmarking) and not impermissible grounds under the Employment Equity Act 55 of 1998. Material pay-equity gaps are reported to RemCo with a remediation plan.

## 5. Material Risk Takers

### 5.1 MRT identification

The following roles are designated as Material Risk Takers by default at licence-day:

| Role | Rationale |
|---|---|
| **CEO** | Ultimate executive authority; sets strategic risk appetite; decisions affect the entire risk profile. |
| **CFO** | Oversight of capital, liquidity, and financial reporting; approval authority for significant financial commitments. |
| **CRO** | Direct oversight of the risk management framework; risk appetite custodian; independent risk function accountability. |
| **Head of Trading / Head of Global Markets** | Direct P&L and market-risk authority; single largest source of market risk and trading-conduct risk in the bank's operating model. |

Additional persons may be designated as MRTs by RemCo if they:
- have authority to commit the bank to transactions with a notional value exceeding the MRT threshold (set by RemCo annually, minimum ZAR 50 million per transaction or ZAR 200 million aggregate per quarter);
- manage material operational, credit, or market-risk functions independently; or
- are identified by Helena (Chief Risk Officer, governance) as having material impact on the risk profile through the annual MRT identification process.

The MRT register is maintained by Sade (AgentOps, operations) and reviewed by Helena (Chief Risk Officer, governance) annually, with changes approved by RemCo.

### 5.2 Deferral requirements

For MRTs, variable remuneration is subject to mandatory deferral as follows:

**Minimum deferral proportion:** at least **40%** of any variable remuneration award to an MRT must be deferred. For senior MRTs (CEO, CFO, CRO, Head of Trading), the minimum deferral proportion is **50%**.

**Minimum deferral period:** deferred awards may not vest earlier than **3 years** from the date of award. RemCo may impose longer deferral periods, particularly for awards linked to transactions or positions whose risk horizon extends beyond 3 years.

**Vesting schedule:** deferred awards vest on a pro-rata basis over the deferral period unless RemCo adopts a cliff-vesting schedule for specific award tranches. The vesting schedule for each award is approved by RemCo at the time of the award and documented in the individual remuneration letter.

**Form of deferral:** deferred awards may be held as:
- cash-denominated accounts (subject to conditions precedent until vesting);
- equity or phantom-equity instruments if and when the bank's ownership structure makes such instruments available.

RemCo approves the form of each deferred award tranche. The form must not create incentives that conflict with the risk-alignment principles in Section 4.

### 5.3 Malus

**Definition:** malus is the right of RemCo to reduce or eliminate unvested deferred awards (including prospective cash awards not yet paid) before vesting.

**Malus triggers** include, without limitation:

| Trigger category | Examples |
|---|---|
| Risk materialisation | A trading loss, credit event, or liquidity stress attributable in whole or in part to the MRT's risk-taking decisions |
| Regulatory finding | A finding by the Prudential Authority (PA), FSCA, SARB, or FIC that the MRT failed to comply with regulatory requirements |
| Conduct breach | A material breach of the Code of Conduct, Conflicts of Interest Policy, Insider Trading Policy, or ABC Policy |
| Financial restatement | A restatement of the bank's financial statements that materially affects the metrics on which the award was based |
| Fraud or dishonesty | Any act of fraud, wilful misconduct, or dishonesty by the MRT |
| Control failure | A significant failure of a control, process, or system within the MRT's area of accountability |
| Capital ratio breach | A breach (or material approach toward breach) of the bank's regulatory capital requirements |

Malus is applied by RemCo acting on a recommendation from Helena (Chief Risk Officer, governance), after a due-process review that provides the affected MRT with the opportunity to respond.

### 5.4 Clawback

**Definition:** clawback is the right (and, in certain circumstances, the obligation) of the bank to recover remuneration already paid or vested.

**Clawback period:** the bank may initiate clawback proceedings within **7 years** of the date of vesting of the relevant award, extended to **10 years** where the trigger involves fraud, wilful misconduct, or a regulatory investigation that was not reasonably discoverable within the standard period.

**Clawback triggers:** the same categories as malus triggers (Section 5.3) apply to clawback, with the additional condition that the relevant conduct, event, or restatement was not reasonably known at the time of vesting.

**Clawback procedure:**
1. Helena (Chief Risk Officer, governance) identifies a potential clawback trigger and notifies RemCo in writing.
2. RemCo commissions a due-process review, including independent assessment where relevant.
3. The affected MRT is provided written notice of the potential clawback and given a minimum of 20 business days to respond.
4. RemCo deliberates and decides, with reasons recorded in the minutes.
5. If clawback is approved, Sade (AgentOps, operations) initiates the recovery mechanism; Imani (Legal-as-code engineer, engineering) advises on the legal recovery pathway.
6. Unrecovered amounts are reported in the annual remuneration report.

The bank may pursue clawback through employment-law mechanisms, contractual deductions (subject to BCEA s.34 limitations), set-off against future payments, or civil proceedings where recovery requires it. All individual employment contracts for MRTs must include a contractual clawback provision enforceable under South African law.

### 5.5 Interaction with capital requirements

No variable remuneration for MRTs may be paid (whether deferred or non-deferred) if:
- the bank is in breach of its Pillar 1 minimum capital adequacy requirements at the time of payment; or
- the bank's capital adequacy ratio is below its Internal Capital Adequacy Assessment Process (ICAAP) target floor, unless RemCo (with Helena (Chief Risk Officer, governance) concurrence) determines that payment would not further impair the capital position and the Prudential Authority has been notified.

Helena (Chief Risk Officer, governance) provides a capital-adequacy certification to RemCo before each variable remuneration payment cycle.

## 6. Fixed Remuneration

### 6.1 Benchmarking methodology

Fixed remuneration is set with reference to:

- **Market benchmarking data** from recognised remuneration survey providers covering the South African banking sector. Benchmark comparator groups are agreed by RemCo annually before the annual remuneration review.
- **Role scope and complexity,** including the size of the bank's balance sheet, the nature of its activities, regulatory accountability, and the degree of autonomy in the role.
- **Individual factors,** including experience, qualifications, fit-and-proper standing, and demonstrated performance over time.

The bank's philosophy is to target fixed remuneration at the **median** of the relevant market benchmark, with RemCo discretion to approve above-median fixed pay where role or individual factors warrant it (subject to documented justification). RemCo approves the benchmark methodology annually and any departures from the median target on a case-by-case basis.

### 6.2 Approval authority

| Category | Approval authority |
|---|---|
| CEO fixed remuneration | Board (CEO withdraws from deliberation) |
| Executive directors (non-CEO) | RemCo |
| MRTs (non-executive directors) | RemCo |
| Key individuals | RemCo on appointment; delegated to CEO for annual adjustments within a RemCo-approved band |
| Other employees | CEO, within RemCo-approved salary bands |

NED fees are proposed by RemCo and approved by shareholders at the annual general meeting, consistent with Companies Act s.66(8). RemCo members may not vote on their own fee proposals.

### 6.3 Annual review cadence

Fixed remuneration is reviewed annually at the annual remuneration meeting (Section 3.5). Reviews consider:
- market movement in benchmark comparator groups;
- the bank's financial and capital position;
- individual performance assessment outcomes;
- pay-equity results.

RemCo may approve zero increases, below-inflation increases, or market-adjustment increases. Increases take effect from the first day of the new financial year unless RemCo specifies otherwise.

Mid-year adjustments to fixed remuneration require RemCo approval (for MRTs and executive directors) and are confined to role changes, promotions, or material market dislocation.

## 7. Variable Remuneration

### 7.1 Performance metrics framework

Variable remuneration for executives and MRTs is assessed against a balanced scorecard comprising the following metric categories. Each category's relative weight is set by RemCo annually before the start of the performance period.

| Category | Examples of metrics | Guidance weight range |
|---|---|---|
| **Financial performance** | Return on equity (adjusted); revenue growth; cost-to-income ratio; net interest margin | 25–35% |
| **Risk management** | Capital adequacy (CET1 vs. target); LCR and NSFR vs. limits; credit loss provisions vs. plan; risk findings remediated on time | 25–35% |
| **Compliance and conduct** | Regulatory findings (number and severity); suspicious-transaction reporting timeliness; policy-breach incidents; audit findings | 15–25% |
| **Strategic and operational** | Progress against licence-readiness milestones; platform build quality; talent and succession; stakeholder engagement | 15–25% |

The weight ranges above are guidance; actual weights for each performance period are approved by RemCo before the period commences and published in the annual remuneration report.

### 7.2 Capital ratio gate

No variable remuneration shall be paid to any covered person in respect of a performance period in which:

- the bank's CET1 ratio fell below the regulatory minimum (Banks Act Regulations Part III) at any point during the period; or
- the bank's Total Capital Adequacy Ratio fell below the ICAAP target floor at the end of the period.

This gate is absolute and not subject to RemCo discretion. Helena (Chief Risk Officer, governance) provides a capital-gate certification to RemCo at the performance outcome meeting. Where the capital gate is triggered, all variable remuneration for that period is forfeited; unvested deferred awards from prior periods remain on foot (subject to the malus provisions in Section 5.3).

### 7.3 Performance assessment and RemCo discretion

RemCo assesses variable remuneration outcomes annually at the performance outcome meeting. The assessment process:

1. Management (CEO, CFO) presents a scorecard out-turn against pre-agreed metrics.
2. Helena (Chief Risk Officer, governance) presents an independent risk-alignment assessment.
3. Zara (Chief Compliance Officer, governance) presents a conduct and compliance assessment.
4. RemCo deliberates, applying informed discretion. RemCo may:
   - **reduce** an award below the formulaic outcome where risk events, conduct issues, or capital pressures are present;
   - **increase** an award (within the approved maximum) where exceptional performance is underweighted by the scorecard;
   - **withhold** an award entirely where threshold performance has not been met.

5. RemCo documents its reasoning for any material discretionary adjustment in the minutes.

**Board override discretion:** The Board (on RemCo recommendation) may reduce or eliminate variable remuneration for the current year in circumstances of material financial stress, regulatory intervention, or systemic market disruption, irrespective of individual performance metrics. This override is a Board resolution requiring a simple majority.

### 7.4 Maximum variable remuneration

RemCo sets a maximum variable remuneration cap for each covered role. The cap is set as a multiple of fixed remuneration and is reviewed annually. Caps are calibrated to avoid excessive reward concentration and to maintain compliance with the risk-alignment principles in Section 4.

Proposed maximum caps for roles at licence-day are subject to RemCo approval at its inaugural meeting and will be disclosed in the first annual remuneration report.

### 7.5 Short-term and long-term incentive structure

Variable remuneration may be structured as:

- **Short-term incentive (STI):** assessed on a single financial-year performance period; minimum deferral rules apply to MRTs.
- **Long-term incentive (LTI):** assessed on a multi-year (3+ year) performance period; designed to align with the bank's long-term strategic objectives and risk horizon. LTI awards for MRTs are wholly deferred and subject to full malus and clawback.

RemCo determines the STI/LTI split for each covered role. For MRTs, a minimum of 25% of total variable remuneration (non-deferred and deferred combined) must be structured as LTI.

## 8. Governance and Disclosure

### 8.1 Annual remuneration report

The bank publishes an annual remuneration report as part of its integrated or annual report. The report is prepared by Sade (AgentOps, operations) under the oversight of Owen (Company Secretary, governance), reviewed by RemCo, and approved by the Board. The report includes:

- a summary of the Remuneration Policy;
- a summary of the RemCo composition and key decisions taken during the year;
- total fixed remuneration paid to each executive director and MRT (individually disclosed, named);
- total variable remuneration awarded, deferred, vested, and forfeited for each executive director and MRT (individually disclosed, named);
- total NED fees paid (individually disclosed, named);
- a description of any malus or clawback applied during the year (anonymised unless disclosure is required by law or regulation);
- the outcome of the pay-equity review;
- the MRT register (roles, not necessarily names, unless regulatory disclosure requires it).

### 8.2 Prudential Authority disclosure

The bank complies with disclosure requirements under the Banks Act s.60B and any PA guidance issued under the Regulations Relating to Banks regarding remuneration disclosure. Owen (Company Secretary, governance) is responsible for ensuring that:

- required remuneration data is included in the bank's annual report and any specific PA remuneration reporting templates;
- material changes to the Remuneration Policy are notified to the PA as required;
- the PA has access to RemCo minutes and remuneration data on request.

Zara (Chief Compliance Officer, governance) maintains a standing remuneration-disclosure checklist that cross-references PA requirements against the annual report, reviewed at the annual remuneration meeting.

### 8.3 Related-party transparency

Remuneration paid to persons who are related parties of the bank (as defined under IAS 24 and the bank's Related-Party Policy) is disclosed in the annual financial statements consistent with IAS 24 requirements. Owen (Company Secretary, governance) maintains the related-party register and flags any overlaps with the remuneration disclosure process.

Any remuneration arrangement that involves a related party of a director or MRT (e.g., a payment to a company controlled by a director's family member) requires pre-approval by the Board with the relevant director recused, and is disclosed as a related-party transaction.

### 8.4 Shareholder engagement

The annual remuneration report is presented at the annual general meeting for a non-binding advisory vote consistent with King IV recommended practice. A binding vote applies to NED fees (Companies Act s.66(8)). Where the remuneration report receives a dissenting advisory vote of 25% or more, RemCo engages with shareholders to understand the concerns and reports back to the following AGM on the response taken.

### 8.5 Policy review

This policy is reviewed annually by Owen (Company Secretary, governance) and Sade (AgentOps, operations), with input from Helena (Chief Risk Officer, governance) and Zara (Chief Compliance Officer, governance). Material amendments require RemCo approval and Board endorsement. The policy version register is maintained by Owen (Company Secretary, governance).

## Appendix B — Key definitions

**NED:** Non-executive director.  
**PA:** Prudential Authority (a division of the South African Reserve Bank).  
**RAS:** Risk Appetite Statement and Framework, owned by Helena (Chief Risk Officer, governance).  
**RemCo:** Remuneration Committee, established under Section 3.  
**STI:** Short-term incentive.  
**LTI:** Long-term incentive.
