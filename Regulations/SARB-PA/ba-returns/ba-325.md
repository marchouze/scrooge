## Purpose of BA 325

Form BA 325 is the prudential return through which the bank reports its **FRTB market-risk capital charge** to the Prudential Authority. Market risk is a **Pillar-1** risk: the bank must hold capital against the risk of loss in on- and off-balance-sheet positions arising from movements in market prices. The FRTB framework re-engineered the market-risk capital regime (replacing the Basel 2.5 standardised + VaR-based internal models) with (i) a recalibrated **Standardised Approach (SA)** built on the **sensitivities-based method (SBM)**, a **default risk charge (DRC)** and a **residual risk add-on (RRAO)**; and (ii) an **Internal Models Approach (IMA)** built on **expected shortfall** (replacing VaR) with desk-level eligibility (P&L attribution + back-testing) and a non-modellable-risk-factor (NMRF) capitalisation regime.

The headline capital identity (BCBS MAR `urn:reg:bcbs:mar:20.1`; Reg 28):

```
FRTB-SA market-risk capital = SBM (delta + vega + curvature)
                            + DRC (default risk charge)
                            + RRAO (residual risk add-on)
```

with **no diversification across the SBM / DRC / RRAO components** (FRTB SA aggregation rule). The total SA capital is converted to a market-RWA contribution (× 12.5, the reciprocal of the 8% minimum) and integrated into the BA 700 capital-adequacy aggregate (Reg 38, integrating Reg 28/29).

**The bank's actual market risk today.** In the build phase the trading book's live market risk is the **FX net open position (NOP)** — the bank runs spot and forward FX as its primary franchise, with bonds, IRS and equity entering as the book grows. Under FRTB SA, the FX NOP feeds the **FX risk class** (SBM delta; vega + curvature once FX options are traded); FX forwards/swaps additionally feed the **GIRR** (general interest-rate risk) class via the two discount legs (ZAR + counter-currency). The bank is on the **SA** — the **IMA is not elected** (no PA desk approvals sought; the IMA-related obligations `ORG-PR-56/57/58/59` are `NOT_APPLICABLE` until a desk election is made). The live measurement substrate computes a **99% 1-day historical-simulation VaR / 97.5% expected shortfall** suite (`var-engine.ts`) as the internal risk lens; the *capital* charge is the FRTB-SA figure.

## The FRTB Standardised Approach (SA)

The SA is the bank's regulatory capital basis. It has three additive components.

### 1. Sensitivities-based method (SBM) — delta, vega, curvature

The SBM computes capital from the position's **sensitivities** to a prescribed set of risk factors, aggregated by risk class, bucket and cross-bucket using prescribed risk weights and correlations. The risk classes (BCBS MAR / d457; SA risk-class taxonomy, per `frtb-sa-capital-computation.md` Step 3 `[citation: TBC — precise d457 paragraph indices]`):

| Risk class | Code | Scope for this bank |
|---|---|---|
| General interest rate risk | GIRR | FX-forward / -swap / -option discount legs (ZAR + counter-currency); bonds; OTC IRD |
| Foreign exchange risk | FX | FX spot, forward, swap, NDF, vanilla/exotic option (all pairs vs ZAR reporting currency) — **the live risk class today** |
| Credit spread risk — non-securitisation | CSR | Corporate-bond credit spread; OTC IRD counterparty CVA reference |
| Credit spread risk — securitisation (non-CTP / CTP) | CSR-S | Out of scope at v0 (bank does not trade securitisations) |
| Equity risk | EQ | JSE-listed equities held for client facilitation |
| Commodity risk | COM | Out of primary franchise scope; residual cross-currency only |

The SBM has **three limbs** — **delta**, **vega**, **curvature** — each computed across the risk classes:

- **Delta** — sensitivity to a small move in the risk factor (1bp for rate/spread tenors; 1% relative for FX pairs, equity prices, commodities).
- **Vega** — sensitivity to a 1% relative move in implied volatility (applies to options: FX vanilla/exotic, equity options).
- **Curvature** — the convexity not captured by delta (full revaluation under a stress shock minus the linear delta approximation; same option population as vega).

Aggregation is bucket → risk-class → cross-class, under **three correlation scenarios** (high / medium / low; the per-risk-class capital is the **max** across the three before summation — `frtb-sa-capital-computation.md` Step 7a, BCBS d457 `[citation: TBC]`). There is **zero correlation across the delta / vega / curvature limbs** within a risk class. The full SBM aggregation arithmetic (`Kb = sqrt(Σ WSi² + Σ Σ ρij·WSi·WSj)`; `Kclass = sqrt(Σ Kb² + Σ Σ γbc·Sb·Sc)`) is documented in [`Procedures/by-policy/frtb-sa-capital-computation.md`](../../../Procedures/by-policy/frtb-sa-capital-computation.md) §5.2 (Steps 4–9).

### 2. Default risk charge (DRC)

The DRC captures **jump-to-default** risk for instruments with credit-default exposure (corporate bonds, OTC counterparty default; securitisations out of v0 scope). It is built from gross jump-to-default losses → net JTD per obligor → bucket assignment with a **hedge-benefit ratio (HBR)** scaling → cross-bucket sum with **no diversification across DRC buckets**. The net-JTD / HBR mechanism (`frtb-sa-capital-computation.md` §5.3, Steps 10–11; BCBS d457 `[citation: TBC]`) recognises intra-bucket hedging so the DRC neither over-states (ignoring hedges) nor under-states (ignoring the long/short netting rules).

### 3. Residual risk add-on (RRAO)

The RRAO is a flat capital add-on for instruments with complex residual risks not captured by SBM or DRC — exotic FX options (barrier, digital, lookback, basket), Bermudan/American exotic IRD, and any instrument whose payoff is not a deterministic function of standard risk factors. The bank's RRAO perimeter (`frtb-sa-capital-computation.md` §5.4, Step 12; BCBS FRTB SA `[citation: TBC — RRAO scope paragraph]`): 1% of notional for exotic options / exotic IRD / securitisations; 0.1% of notional for the residual-residual category.

### CVA capital (adjacent)

CVA capital is computed under the **BCBS revised CVA framework (d507, July 2020)** — SA-CVA (default; its own SBM-like delta + vega structure per CVA risk class) and BA-CVA (basic-approach fallback for sub-materiality netting sets) — per Market Risk Policy v1 §5 and `frtb-sa-capital-computation.md` §5.5 (Step 13). CVA capital is **reported separately** (not on BA 325 itself — PC 18/2024 Appendix B carries the CVA roadmap as a parallel workstream); the daily `cvaComponent` is a continuous-monitoring estimate, the monthly figure is the reportable number.

## The Internal Models Approach (IMA) — not elected

The IMA replaces VaR with **expected shortfall (ES) at a 97.5% confidence level** (BCBS MAR `urn:reg:bcbs:mar:33.1`), with desk-level eligibility gated by the **P&L attribution (PLA) test** and **back-testing**, a **non-modellable-risk-factor (NMRF)** stressed-scenario capitalisation regime, and the **72.5% output floor** (IMA capital ≥ 72.5% × the SA-derived floor; BCBS FRTB Art 325 / `ORG-PR-60`). **The bank has not elected the IMA** for any desk: no PA desk-approval applications have been made, and the IMA-related obligations are marked `NOT_APPLICABLE` until an election is made:

- `ORG-PR-56` — FRTB IMA desk approval (PA approval before using IMA capital; Reg 28 IMA sub-section; FRTB Art 325az `[citation: TBC]`).
- `ORG-PR-57` — FRTB PLA test (daily P&L attribution per desk; FRTB Art 325bg `[citation: TBC]`).
- `ORG-PR-58` — FRTB back-testing (daily 1-day 99% VaR back-test over a rolling 250-day window; FRTB Art 325bf `[citation: TBC]`).
- `ORG-PR-59` — FRTB NMRF (identify and capitalise non-modellable risk factors via the stress-scenario method; FRTB Art 325bk `[citation: TBC]`).
- `ORG-PR-60` — FRTB SA/IMA dual capital reporting + 72.5% output floor (FRTB Art 325 `[citation: TBC]`).

These are documented here so the SA-vs-IMA scope is explicit; the SA limb is the live one (and would remain the floor under the output-floor rule even if the IMA were later elected).

## The trading-book / banking-book boundary

FRTB tightened the trading-book / banking-book boundary to prevent capital arbitrage between the two regimes (market-risk capital on the trading book; credit / IRRBB capital on the banking book). Reg 28(3) explicitly draws **FX positions** into market-risk scope whether held in the **banking book or the trading book** (Reg 28(3)(c): *"any foreign exchange instrument held in the reporting bank's banking book or trading book, that is, any foreign-currency position held by the bank"*) — i.e. the FX NOP is a market-risk position regardless of book. The same applies to commodity positions (Reg 28(3)(d)). The boundary, the presumptive-classification rules, and the re-designation gate are set in Market Risk Policy v1 §2 and operationalised at the New Product Approval gate; the precise BCBS boundary-paragraph references are counsel-gated.

## Material foreign-currency reporting

Reg 28(3)(c) brings every foreign-currency position into market-risk scope. Because the bank reports in ZAR but trades multi-currency positions, every foreign-currency sensitivity, risk weight and DRC notional is revalued into ZAR for the consolidated FRTB-SA capital figure — and the day-on-day FX-revaluation effect is isolated as its own attribution category (`frtb-sa-capital-computation.md` Step 15) so FX-translation noise does not contaminate the position / market-data attribution. This is the Principle-5 (multi-currency) hook on the market-risk return — ZAR is the presentation currency; the FX NOP is the live risk.
