## Purpose

This policy establishes Hoz Bank Limited's (the "Bank") zero-tolerance approach to bribery, corruption, and corrupt activities in any form. It sets out the framework through which the Bank prevents, detects, and responds to bribery and corruption, and the protected whistleblowing channel through which any person — agent, human principal, counterparty, or third party — may report suspected violations without fear of retaliation.

The policy implements:

- **PRECCA (Prevention and Combating of Corrupt Activities Act 12/2004)** — the primary South African anti-corruption statute, imposing criminal liability for bribery, corruption, and related offences. PRECCA s34 creates a mandatory obligation to report offences to law enforcement.
- **Protected Disclosures Act 26/2000 (as amended, "PDA")** — provides statutory protection for employees (and, under recent amendments, individuals in service relationships) who make protected disclosures of impropriety.
- **Companies Act 71/2008 s159** — provides a safe harbour for protected disclosures made in good faith to appropriate persons or bodies.
- **US Foreign Corrupt Practices Act (FCPA)** — applies where the Bank's activities involve US-nexus transactions, US-listed counterparties, or payments routed through US financial infrastructure. The Bank adopts FCPA-equivalent controls globally rather than apply a lower standard to non-US activity.

This policy sits at the *policy* layer of the Reg → Policy → Procedure → System Capability chain (Principle 2). Criminal statutes (PRECCA) sit above; procedures under this policy operationalise how the Bank investigates, reports, and disciplines; system capabilities (the whistleblowing ticket system, third-party screening database, training records system) execute those procedures.

## Principles

1. **Zero tolerance.** The Bank has zero tolerance for bribery, corruption, facilitation payments, and corrupt dealing in any form, in any jurisdiction, by any person acting for or on behalf of the Bank.
2. **PRECCA mandatory reporting.** Where a principal officer or agent of the Bank has reason to believe that a corruption offence has been committed, PRECCA s34 imposes a legal duty to report to the South African Police Service or the Directorate for Priority Crime Investigation (Hawks). This is not discretionary.
3. **Protected disclosures are encouraged.** The Bank actively encourages reporting of suspected bribery and corruption through its protected whistleblowing channel. Reporters are protected under PDA and Companies Act s159 and will not suffer retaliation.
4. **Proportionate response.** Investigations are proportionate, confidential, and concluded promptly. Unsubstantiated reports made in good faith are closed without adverse consequence to the reporter.
5. **Third-party integrity.** The Bank does not knowingly engage suppliers, agents, or intermediaries who engage in bribery or corruption. Third-party due diligence is a gate to engagement, not an afterthought.
6. **Political neutrality.** The Bank does not make political contributions or use corporate resources to influence political outcomes without the approvals required by this policy.

## 3. Standards and Limits

### 3.1 Zero-Tolerance Prohibition

The following are absolutely prohibited for any person acting for or on behalf of the Bank, in any jurisdiction, in any form:

- Offering, promising, giving, requesting, receiving, or accepting any bribe, corrupt payment, or corrupt advantage.
- Making facilitation payments — small payments made to government officials to expedite routine administrative functions — regardless of local custom or expectation.
- Using agents, intermediaries, or joint-venture partners to make payments that the Bank itself could not lawfully make.
- Entering into any arrangement, transaction, or dealing that constitutes a "corrupt activity" as defined in PRECCA s1.
- Falsifying records, accounts, or documentation to conceal a bribe or corrupt payment.
- Retaliating against any person who makes a protected disclosure in good faith under this policy or the PDA.

There are no de minimis thresholds for bribery. A R50 cash payment to a public official to expedite a permit is as prohibited as a R5,000,000 contract payment.

### 3.2 Gifts, Hospitality, and Entertainment

The general gifts and hospitality framework is set out in the Conflicts of Interest Policy (COI-POL-01) — individual limit R500, aggregate per relationship per year R2,000, register mandatory for all items above R200.

**Public officials — stricter standard.** Where the recipient is a public official (government employee, regulatory official, state-owned entity employee, or any person acting in a public capacity):

- No gifts, hospitality, or entertainment may be provided regardless of value, without the prior written approval of Zara.
- Requests from public officials for gifts or hospitality must be declined and reported to Zara immediately.
- Where declining a public official's request would create a compliance risk, Zara determines the approach in consultation with external counsel if required.

Any gift or entertainment involving a public official that is not pre-approved by Zara is treated as a potential bribery incident and investigated accordingly.

### 3.3 PRECCA s34 — Mandatory Reporting Obligation

Section 34 of PRECCA imposes a mandatory obligation on the Bank and its officers to report, as soon as reasonably practicable, any knowledge or reasonable grounds to suspect that a person has committed or intends to commit a corruption offence. The report must be made to the South African Police Service or the Hawks.

**Mandatory reporting process:**

1. Any agent or human principal who becomes aware of facts giving rise to a reasonable suspicion of a PRECCA offence reports immediately to Zara.
2. Zara assesses the report within 2 business days and, in consultation with external legal counsel (if time permits), determines whether a s34 obligation has been triggered.
3. Where a s34 obligation is triggered, Zara and the CEO co-sign the report to the SAPS or Hawks.
4. The report, supporting evidence, and the decision record are preserved in the investigation file.
5. Zara notifies the board (via Owen) of the report and its content within 1 business day of filing.

Failure to make a mandatory s34 report is a criminal offence under PRECCA. No commercial, reputational, or counterparty-relationship consideration overrides the mandatory reporting obligation.

### 3.4 Third-Party Due Diligence

The Bank does not engage suppliers, agents, or intermediaries without a corruption-risk screening that is proportionate to the relationship's risk profile.

**Screening requirements by risk tier:**

| Tier | Description | Minimum screening |
|---|---|---|
| High risk | Agents, intermediaries, or brokers with government-sector client exposure; jurisdictions with elevated corruption risk (FATF grey-listed; CPI score < 40) | Enhanced due diligence — ownership structure, sanctions check, adverse-media search, anti-corruption attestation, contractual anti-corruption covenants |
| Medium risk | Professional services providers; technology vendors with data access | Standard due diligence — ownership, sanctions check, adverse media |
| Low risk | Commodity suppliers; standard IT procurement | Proportionate screening — sanctions check; self-certification |

All third-party anti-corruption due diligence is recorded in the supplier register (Outsourcing Policy cross-reference) and reviewed annually or on material change to the relationship.

### 3.5 Political Contributions

The Bank does not make political contributions — direct or indirect, monetary or in-kind — to any political party, candidate, or political organisation without:

- Prior written approval of the CEO and the board.
- A determination that the contribution is lawful under applicable law.
- Full disclosure in the Bank's annual report.

In practice, the Bank does not anticipate making political contributions in the build phase or at licence-day. Any approach from a political party or affiliated entity requesting support is reported to Zara and the CEO immediately.

### 3.6 Extra-Territorial Anti-Bribery Statutes — UK Bribery Act 2010 (and FCPA)

**Citation:** UK Bribery Act 2010 ss.1, 6, 7 + Ministry of Justice adequate-procedures guidance (2011); US FCPA 1977; register obligation `ORG-FC-22`.

Where the Bank has UK touch-points that bring it within the extra-territorial reach of the UK Bribery Act 2010 — UK-incorporated counterparties, business conducted in the UK, GBP clearing or correspondent arrangements, or persons performing services for the Bank in the UK — the Act's offences apply, including the s.7 corporate offence of failing to prevent bribery by an associated person. The Bank's defence to the s.7 offence is the statutory "adequate procedures" defence, which this policy operationalises:

1. **Proportionate procedures** — the zero-tolerance prohibition (§3.1), gifts and hospitality pre-approval framework (§3.2), and political-contributions bar (§3.5) apply to all activity with a UK nexus without dilution.
2. **Top-level commitment** — CEO and board accountability per §2 (Governance); the zero-tolerance posture is set at the top of the house.
3. **Risk assessment** — the third-party risk-tiering in §3.4 treats UK-nexus intermediaries and agents on the same risk-tiered due-diligence basis as all others; jurisdictional bribery risk is an explicit tiering input.
4. **Due diligence** — third-party anti-corruption due diligence per §3.4, including contractual anti-corruption covenants for high-risk engagements.
5. **Communication and training** — ABC training per §4.4 covers the UK Bribery Act's s.6 foreign-public-official offence and the s.7 corporate offence for all agents and statutory humans whose mandates touch UK-nexus activity.
6. **Monitoring and review** — surveillance and register reviews per §4.5; Vera (internal audit engineer, engineering — third-line) tests UK-nexus coverage in the annual ABC audit.

The same global-standard approach applies to the US FCPA (§8 citations): the Bank adopts the strictest applicable standard globally rather than maintaining jurisdiction-specific lower standards. **Build-phase note:** the Bank currently has no UK establishment and no UK-nexus intermediaries; the controls above bind from the moment any UK touch-point arises, and the risk assessment at counterparty onboarding (Counterparty Onboarding Policy) is the detection gate.

## 4. Controls and Monitoring

### 4.1 Whistleblowing Channel

The Bank maintains an anonymous whistleblowing channel through which any person — internal or external — may report suspected bribery, corruption, fraud, financial crime, or other serious impropriety.

**Channel details:**
- **Mechanism:** dedicated email alias (internal and external-facing) plus a logged-ticket system that strips metadata preserving anonymity.
- **Custodian:** Zara (Chief Compliance Officer, governance).
- **Backup custodian:** Owen (Company Secretary, governance) — receives reports where Zara is implicated or unavailable.
- **Availability:** continuous (24/7); reports received outside business hours are triaged on the next business day.
- **Acknowledgement:** all reports acknowledged within 2 business days; anonymous reports receive a ticket reference for follow-up.

The channel address, reporting instructions, and PDA protections are communicated to all agents and human principals at onboarding and in annual mandatory training. They are also published on the Bank's intranet and, at licence-day, on the Bank's external website.

### 4.2 Protected Disclosures and Non-Retaliation

Any person who makes a disclosure in good faith through the whistleblowing channel, or who provides information in the course of an investigation under this policy, is protected:

- **PDA protection.** The Protected Disclosures Act 26/2000 (as amended) protects employees and persons in a service relationship from occupational detriment for making protected disclosures.
- **Companies Act s159 safe harbour.** Good-faith disclosures made to appropriate persons or bodies attract the statutory safe harbour.
- **Bank non-retaliation policy.** No person who makes a good-faith report under this policy will be disciplined, dismissed, demoted, disadvantaged, or subjected to any adverse action as a consequence of the report. Retaliation is a serious conduct breach and grounds for disciplinary action against the retaliator.

Where a report is subsequently found to be malicious or made in bad faith, it may be treated as a conduct breach by the reporter. The burden is on the Bank to demonstrate bad faith — good-faith reporters are not penalised for imprecision or for raising concerns that are not upheld by investigation.

### 4.3 Investigation Process

All reports received through the whistleblowing channel or through direct escalation are investigated promptly and confidentially.

**Investigation procedure:**

1. **Receipt and triage (day 1–2).** Zara receives the report, assigns a reference number, and conducts an initial triage to determine: (a) whether the matter is within scope; (b) immediate risk to the Bank; (c) whether law enforcement notification is required (PRECCA s34 or AML/financial crime parallel).
2. **Investigation appointment (day 3–5).** Zara appoints the investigation lead. Where the allegation implicates Zara directly, Owen or Helena leads the investigation and Vera provides audit support.
3. **Investigation conduct (variable; target 30 calendar days for standard matters).** Evidence is gathered, preserved, and reviewed. Relevant parties are interviewed where appropriate. Confidentiality is maintained throughout; only those with a need to know are informed of the allegation.
4. **Findings and determination.** The investigation lead produces a written findings report including: summary of evidence, determination (substantiated / unsubstantiated / inconclusive), recommended remedial action, and any law-enforcement referral.
5. **Escalation of material findings.** Findings involving criminal conduct, regulatory breach, or conduct of a director or key individual are reported to Helena (Chief Risk Officer, governance) and Owen (Company Secretary, governance) within 2 business days of determination. Owen notifies the board (or interim CEO/board) for significant findings.
6. **Closure and feedback.** The reporter is notified (to the extent anonymity permits) that the matter has been concluded and whether action was taken. The investigation file is retained for 7 years.

### 4.4 Training and Awareness

- **Annual mandatory training.** All agents and human principals complete mandatory anti-bribery and corruption training each year. Content includes: PRECCA obligations; FCPA equivalence; the Bank's prohibitions; the whistleblowing channel; how to identify and report red flags.
- **Training records.** Sade (AgentOps) maintains training completion records. Zara reviews completion rates quarterly; non-completion is escalated to the relevant function head.
- **Onboarding.** Anti-bribery and corruption obligations, including acknowledgement of this policy, form part of the onboarding process for all agents and human principals.

### 4.5 Monitoring and Surveillance

- Zara monitors the whistleblowing channel continuously and reviews all reports at receipt.
- Zara reviews the gifts register (maintained under COI-POL-01) monthly for public-official interaction breaches.
- Third-party due diligence records are reviewed annually by Zara and summarised in the annual compliance report.
- Vera conducts an annual independent audit of the ABC framework covering: channel integrity; investigation quality; training completion; third-party screening adequacy; PRECCA s34 record-keeping.

## 6. Exceptions and Escalation

### 6.1 No Exceptions to Core Prohibitions

There are no exceptions to:

- The prohibition on bribery and facilitation payments.
- The mandatory PRECCA s34 reporting obligation.
- The prohibition on retaliation against protected disclosures.

### 6.2 Escalation Triggers

The following events trigger immediate escalation to Zara:

- A bribe, facilitation payment, or corrupt advantage has been offered, promised, given, received, or requested.
- A public official has requested a gift or other advantage.
- A third-party due diligence screen has returned a high-risk corruption indicator.
- A whistleblowing report has been received.
- A person believes they have suffered retaliation for making a protected disclosure.

Where Zara is the subject of the escalation, reports are directed to Owen (Company Secretary, governance) or Helena (Chief Risk Officer, governance).

### 6.3 Board Notification

The following trigger board notification (via Owen):

- Filing of a PRECCA s34 mandatory report.
- Any finding of substantiated bribery or corruption.
- Any regulatory investigation or enforcement action related to anti-corruption.
- A significant finding in Vera's annual audit.

### 6.4 Code of Conduct Cross-Reference

This policy is given effect in the Bank's Code of Conduct, which every agent and human principal is bound by at onboarding and annually. The Code of Conduct is the day-to-day expression of the principles in this policy; this policy is the governing instrument. In case of conflict, this policy prevails.

Where a conduct breach under this policy constitutes a criminal offence under PRECCA or any other statute, criminal referral to law enforcement is not precluded by any internal disciplinary process. Internal discipline and criminal referral may proceed in parallel.
