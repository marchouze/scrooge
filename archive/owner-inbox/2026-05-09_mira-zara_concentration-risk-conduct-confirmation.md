## 3. Specific proposed change

One change to Owen + Imani's composition. The other six questions confirm.

### 3.1 Accelerate human-CRO appointment to before licence-day lodgment

Owen + Imani's §3 ("What Marc retains interim") names Marc as carrying "Banks Act CRO and CFO interim accountability — formally retained while Helena and Camille engineer the function. At licence-day SARB will challenge this; see §4. The recommendation is that *named human CRO and CFO appointments* are the next two hires after the five above."

Mira + Zara's read: the CRO half of that retention is a **Joint Standard 1 of 2024 finding** (Q5 above) at licence-day, not just a SARB-precedent challenge that can be answered by a post-licence hiring commitment. We are not proposing the CFO half changes — that is genuinely a SARB-precedent question and Owen + Imani's framing is right. We *are* proposing the CRO seat is filled **before lodgment**.

**Mechanics.** This is one additional hire moved into the licence-day window — total roster becomes **six separate humans + Marc + external audit firm** (was five + Marc + audit firm). This is still inside the "5–10 humans total" envelope `CLAUDE.md`'s operating model contemplates; we are at the lower middle of that range, not outside it.

**If Marc disagrees with the tightening,** the alternative is a documented Joint-Standard-1-of-2024 derogation lodged with the SARB Prudential Authority during pre-application engagement, with an expansion commitment under §4.1 of Owen + Imani's paper. The decision is therefore: tighten the composition (six humans), or take the derogation path (five humans + explicit JS-1 carve-out). The paper's existing recommendation does neither explicitly.

This is the only proposed change. Everything else confirms.

## 4. Concentration-risk amplifications — conduct-side angles on Owen + Imani §4

Owen + Imani flag three caveats. Brief conduct-side amplifications follow.

### 4.1 CEO + interim-CRO + interim-CFO concentration (their §4.1)

**Conduct-side angle.** The CEO-MLRO bar (Q3) is binding because the second-line independent check on STR escalation must exist before reports leave the institution. The same logic — second-line independence — is what binds the CRO seat under Joint Standard 1 of 2024. Owen + Imani frame the CRO challenge as three-lines-of-defence; we add: it is also a **Joint Standard 1 of 2024 finding** (§3.1 above). The amplification is that the CRO and the MLRO concentrations are the same architectural defect viewed from two regulators; resolving the MLRO concentration (which Owen + Imani already do — separate human, triple-hatted) does not by itself resolve the CRO concentration, because the second-line independence for *prudential* risk is a separate seat from second-line independence for *AML / CFT* risk.

### 4.2 Triple-hatted MLRO + FIC CO + IO (their §4.2)

**Conduct-side angle.** Owen + Imani's deputy-IO + MLRO-alternate framing (CoSec carries both alternates) is workable but heavy on the CoSec — see Q2 caveat. Practical recommendation: **deputy-IO is CoSec; MLRO-alternate is one of the independent NEDs** (the AC Chair, by preference, on the basis that AC oversight already touches AML/CFT controls). This splits the alternate load and avoids a single-point-of-failure at the CoSec seat. Mira will draft the alternate-designation form for Owen's secretariat substrate when the human roster is named.

### 4.3 NED-independence depth (their §4.3)

**Conduct-side angle.** No additional conduct-side challenge. Owen + Imani's framing (three-NED floor with named expansion commitment) is the right answer. Note for the licence-application narrative: if the AC Chair is also the MLRO-alternate (per §4.2 amplification above), that should be declared in the fit-and-proper file as an overlap that the Board has explicitly considered — not hidden.
