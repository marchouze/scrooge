The Conflict of Interest (CoI) Code (Board Notice 58 of 2010) — "Notice on
Determination under Section 3A of the Financial Advisory and Intermediary Services Act,
2002 (Act No. 37 of 2002) — Conflict of Interest" sets requirements for FSPs and their
representatives on managing conflicts of interest.

Key provisions:

1. DEFINITIONS — "Conflict of interest" means any situation in which a provider or
   a representative has an actual or potential interest that may, in rendering a financial
   service to a client, influence the objective exercise of that provider's or
   representative's obligations to that client or prevent the provider or representative
   from rendering an unbiased and fair financial service to that client, or that may
   reasonably be perceived to have such effect.

2. CONFLICT OF INTEREST MANAGEMENT POLICY — Every FSP must have and
   maintain a written conflict-of-interest management policy. The policy must set out
   how conflicts of interest are identified, assessed, monitored, managed, and disclosed.

3. DISCLOSURE — Where a conflict of interest exists or may exist, the FSP or
   representative must disclose the conflict of interest to the client before providing the
   financial service, and the client must be afforded a reasonable opportunity to decide
   whether to continue with the transaction.

4. CONSEQUENCES — An FSP may not act in a manner which conflicts with the
   client's interests and with its obligations under the Act.

5. RECORD-KEEPING — Records of identified conflicts, disclosures made to clients,
   and measures taken to manage conflicts must be maintained.

6. REGISTER OF FINANCIAL INTERESTS — FSPs and representatives must maintain
   a register of financial interests received from or given to other FSPs, product
   suppliers, or representatives.

Relevance for Hoz Securities Limited:
- As a Category I/II FSP, Hoz Securities Limited is subject to the CoI Code.
- The bank's Conflicts of Interest Policy (Domain L, ORG-WB-04) is the fulfilment
  policy for this obligation.
- Institutional counterparty trading creates inherent conflict of interest situations
  (principal vs agent; proprietary vs customer flow) — the CoI Code requires these
  to be systematically identified, managed, and disclosed.
