## 1. Role purpose + regulatory anchor

### 1.1 Purpose

NED #2 is one of the three Non-Executive Directors comprising Hoz Bank Limited's Audit Committee at licence-day. Together with the Independent Chair (NED #1, who also chairs the AC) and NED #3 (non-executive, not strictly independent), NED #2 satisfies the three-NED AC composition floor under Companies Act 71 of 2008 s.94(2) and the substantive-independence overlay under s.94(4)–(6).

Per Owen's composition paper §7A.1, the same human serves as a director on the **shared board** across Hoz Group Limited, Hoz Bank Limited, and Hoz Securities Limited under D-LEGAL-ENTITY-TREE-V0; the seat-shape is bank-led for fit-and-proper purposes (Banks Act s.60 + SARB PA fit-and-proper) and additionally clears the Companies Act director-fit-and-proper baseline at the group entity and the FAIS / JSE fit-and-proper at the securities entity per §7A.2.

### 1.2 Regulatory anchor — strict-independent shape

NED #2 is the **strict-independent** seat among NED #2 and NED #3. The third NED slot (NED #3) is permitted to be non-executive but not strictly independent under Companies Act s.94 (per Owen+Imani §3 item 4 and Nolan NED-3 scope §1). NED #2 must therefore clear the *strict* substantive-independence test, the same test that applies to the Independent Chair.

### 1.3 Why this shape, not the others

The "strict-independent" shape on NED #2 (versus the relaxed test on NED #3) is the load-bearing element of how the bank answers SARB PA's NED-independence-depth challenge under Owen §4.3. The licence-application narrative says: *of the three NEDs, two are strictly independent under Companies Act s.94(4)–(6) and King IV substantive testing; the third widens the candidate pool for banking-regulatory or technical depth that the strict-independent pool cannot supply at floor scale.* Without a credibly strict-independent NED #2, the entire two-of-three independence floor collapses to one (the Chair), which SARB has not historically accepted even with an expansion commitment.

### 3.2 Conditional mandatory — financial-expertise contingency

If the Independent Chair search **does not** land on a candidate carrying Companies Act s.94(4)(b) financial-expertise (CA(SA) / former bank-CFO / financial-reporting fluency), then **NED #2 must carry it instead**. Practically:

- This is a contingent must-have decided after the Chair shortlist narrows.
- The s.94(4)(b) requirement is per-AC, not per-member: only one AC member must hold financial-expertise. The Chair is the dominant candidate.
- PAX recommends the Chair search be biased *toward* financial-expertise-strong candidates so this seat is freed to take a different skills-axis. This is the steady-state SA Tier-2-bank shape and is the recommended sequencing in §7.

### 3.3 Recommended skills-axis — primary recommendation: **technology / cyber**

The bank's defining institutional posture is **AI-driven** (CLAUDE.md operating model; Principle 7 — autonomous by default). The labour force is autonomous AI agents. The thin human layer is the regulator-facing residual. This shape places extreme weight on the AC's ability to oversee cyber-resilience, model-risk (both the AI agent fleet *as* a model risk and the conventional credit/market-risk models), and the technology substrate generally.

The Joint Standard 1 of 2024 §6–§7 binds Board-level cyber-resilience oversight at licence-day. SARB PA + FSCA jointly read the Standard's responsible-person and Board-oversight provisions as substantive — boards without credible technology / cyber depth at the NED level will draw a finding.

PAX's primary recommendation is therefore that NED #2 be sourced primarily on a **technology / cyber depth** axis. The candidate shape is:

- Former CIO / CTO / CISO of a major SA bank or Tier-1 financial services firm; **or**
- Former technology-NED or technology-committee-Chair at a JSE-listed financial; **or**
- Former senior-partner technology / cyber assurance practice at a Big-Four firm; **or**
- Former senior official at SARB / FSCA on the technology / cyber-resilience side (subject to the post-employment cooling-off window — typically 12 months).

The *strict-independence test* on this axis is harder to clear than on the financial-expertise axis (the SA technology / cyber ex-bank-executive pool is small, and many candidates carry residual relationships with their former employer that may or may not clear King IV substantive testing). PAX's view is that the test is clearable with diligence — see §7.

### 3.4 Substitutable second-best axes

If the technology / cyber axis cannot deliver a strict-independent shortlist within a reasonable search window, the substitutable second-best axes are (in order):

1. **Risk-modelling / model-risk depth.** Former senior risk officer or model-validation officer at a Tier-1 SA bank; former PA / SARB risk supervisor (cooling-off window). This axis is closely related to the technology axis — model-risk discipline overlaps with the AI-agent-risk discipline — and clears the AC's oversight of Rohan (Risk engineer) and Nadia (Independent-validation engineer).
2. **Transformation / B-BBEE / S&E expertise.** Former CHRO or transformation officer at a Tier-1 SA financial; former B-BBEE Commission senior; former senior S&E NED. This axis directly addresses the Companies Reg. 43 S&E pillar and the licence-day Board's transformation profile, which is one of the four NED-depth axes Owen §4.3 names.
3. **Treasury / liquidity / institutional-wholesale depth.** Former treasurer of a Tier-1 SA bank; former senior fixed-income trader-manager; former senior PA prudential-supervisor. This is a NED-#3 candidate-shape (banking-regulatory) but if NED #3 lands on transformation, NED #2 may take treasury depth instead.

### 3.5 Excluded axes

- **Audit-firm partner currently active.** A current audit-firm partner clears Companies Act s.94 substantive-independence only if the firm is not (and will not be) the bank's external auditor; in practice the SA Big-Four pool is small enough that this constraint disqualifies most candidates.
- **Currently-serving executive of any Tier-1 SA bank or material counterparty.** Concurrent-directorship disqualifier per Owen+Imani §4 hard-rules.
- **Family or close personal relationship with Marc (CEO)**, with the Compliance Lead (seat #5), with the human CRO (seat #6), or with the Independent Chair (seat #1).

## 4. Fit-and-proper considerations

### 4.1 Companies Act 71 of 2008 ss.69, 71

Standard director-disqualification fit-and-proper layer: not an unrehabilitated insolvent; not under court order of delinquency or probation; not removed from a position of trust; not convicted of certain offences. Applies at the Hoz Group Limited director seat and is the base layer at Hoz Bank Limited and Hoz Securities Limited as well.

### 4.2 Banks Act 94 of 1990 s.60 + SARB PA fit-and-proper

PA fit-and-proper sub-instruments apply to NEDs of a SARB-licensed bank. Relevant tests: integrity, competence, financial soundness, and the absence of disqualifying conduct. Documented in the licence-day fit-and-proper file under the Sade-owned (AgentOps) substrate template **PROC-FAIS-KI-FAP-01** (PR #69, merged) — extended per Owen §7A.2 and §7A.5 to capture entity-specific declarations covering all three regimes inside one director-file per human.

The licence-application file submits the fit-and-proper file to the PA at lodgment.

### 4.3 Companies Act s.94(4)–(6) substantive-independence — **strict test**

The seat's defining test. The candidate must demonstrate:

- **No material business relationship** with the bank or any group entity in the three years immediately preceding appointment (Companies Reg. 88(2) reading; King IV Principle 7 substantive).
- **No material financial relationship** beyond the directorship itself.
- **No close family relationship** with any executive of the bank, its group, or its material counterparties.
- **No prior employment or remuneration** by the bank or its group within three years of appointment that would compromise independence judged on a fact-based reading.
- **No common directorship** at a competitor SA bank or material counterparty.

The substantive-independence file is part of the licence-application package; the AC's separate s.94(7) report to shareholders will recur to it annually.

### 4.4 King IV Principle 7

Substantive-independence test layered on top of the s.94 formal test. King IV reads independence as fact-based, not box-ticked. The AC's annual s.94(7) report attests to the substantive independence of each AC member. Common-law and BCBS substantive-independence reading apply.

### 4.5 JSE / FSCA fit-and-proper (Hoz Securities Limited shared-board seat)

Per Owen §7A.2: FAIS Determination of Fit and Proper Requirements 2017 + JSE Membership Rules + JSE Listings Requirements (for the securities entity). The shared-board director clears each entity-regime; the PROC-FAIS-KI-FAP-01 substrate template captures the triangulation as a typed structure rather than three separate files.

### 4.6 POPIA / FIC fit-and-proper (incidental)

Owen §5.2 specifies the MLRO-alternate is the Independent Chair NED, not NED #2. NED #2 therefore does not carry MLRO / FIC CO / IO designations and is not subject to FIC's published RMCP-officer fit-and-proper standards in this seat. (NED #2 may be considered for the MLRO-alternate role only as a fallback if the Independent Chair NED cannot or will not carry it; this is a deliberate non-default per Owen §5.2.)

### 4.7 Joint Standard 1 of 2024 §6–§7

If NED #2 lands on the recommended technology / cyber axis, the candidate file will be reviewed against the §6 responsible-person reading at the Board level. The expectation is technology / cyber fluency demonstrable through prior senior role, not formal certification. This is a substantive — not formal — fit-and-proper expectation.

## 5. Independence test — strict s.94 substantive-independence required

This section is doctrinally important and worth restating clearly.

**The seat is strict-independent.** The substantive-independence test of Companies Act s.94(4)–(6) read with King IV Principle 7 and BCBS Corporate Governance Principles for Banks (2015) Principle 3 applies in full. **NED #2 must clear the strict test.**

**NED #3 does not have to.** The NED #3 scope (Nolan, 2026-05-09) explicitly relaxes this requirement: "need not be independent in the strict s.94 sense, but must be non-executive". This was a deliberate Owen+Imani §3 item 4 design choice to widen the third-NED candidate pool while preserving the two-of-three independent floor SARB will accept under an expansion commitment.

The line between NED #2 (strict-independent) and NED #3 (relaxed) is therefore the load-bearing structural element of how the bank's three-NED AC composition answers SARB's NED-independence-depth challenge under Owen §4.3. Without strict-independent NED #2, the floor falls below SARB's acceptable threshold.

**Implications for search:**

- The strict-independence test narrows the candidate pool meaningfully (perhaps by half) versus the NED #3 search.
- The technology / cyber axis sits in a smaller candidate pool than the financial-expertise axis (the dominant Chair-axis), which is part of why PAX recommends biasing the Chair search toward financial-expertise to free this seat for technology / cyber.
- Substantive-independence diligence on this seat must be completed before Board-pack appointment, with the substantive file forming part of the licence-application package.

**Substrate gap:** The substantive-independence-test register (per-candidate fact-based assessment) is not yet built. Owner: Owen (governance secretariat). Target: pre-shortlist. Surfaces as a `governance-recruitment` substrate task.

### 6.2 Director-fee structure

- **Base annual NED fee** — fixed, paid in cash quarterly. No equity (per Companies Act + King IV independence preservation). No variable.
- **Per-meeting fee** — Board, AC, S&E, standing-attendee Risk; differential per committee with AC + Risk attracting the higher per-meeting rate.

Convention varies; PAX's recommendation is single-roster comp with explicit per-entity allocation in management accounts (Hoz Group / Hoz Bank / Hoz Securities) for arm's-length pricing under IFRS 10 + IAS 24.

### 6.3 Indicative ceiling

PAX's view is that the comp must clear the SA Tier-2-bank AC-member benchmark **plus a small premium** for two reasons: (a) the AI-driven institution shape attracts a candidate-pool risk premium (some otherwise-credible candidates may decline on AI-substrate-novelty grounds); (b) the strict-independence-on-technology-axis combination is the structurally-narrowest pool of the three NED searches, so demand-side pricing is harder. The premium should be modest (≤15% over benchmark base) so the comp does not itself become a substantive-independence concern under King IV.

## 7. Search strategy

### 7.1 Channels

**Primary channels (concurrent):**

- **Specialist NED-search firms** — Spencer Stuart SA, Heidrick & Struggles SA, Korn Ferry SA. NED searches in this sub-pool typically run 6–12 months. Firms have proprietary independent-NED databases and conduct substantive-independence diligence as part of their standard process.

- **Technology / cyber-specialist** — for the primary-axis (§3.3): direct sourcing through SA technology-leadership networks (SA CIO Magazine network; cyber-resilience-specialist communities; ISACA SA chapter senior membership).

**Secondary channels:**

- **Former PA / SARB technology supervisors** — outside the post-employment cooling-off window (typically 12 months).
- **Big-Four technology / cyber advisory partners** — provided no current audit-firm-engagement conflict with Hoz.

**Channels explicitly excluded:**

- LinkedIn-only sourcing (insufficient diligence depth for strict-independence testing).
- Existing Marc / Hoz network (independence-compromise risk; substantive-independence test would be harder to defend).

### 7.2 Skills-axis emphasis

PAX recommends the search funnel be biased from day one as follows:

| Axis | Funnel weight | Rationale |
|---|---|---|
| Technology / cyber depth | **60%** | Primary recommendation per §3.3. AI-driven institution + Joint Standard 1 of 2024 §6–§7. |
| Risk-modelling depth | 20% | Substitutable second-best per §3.4 #1. Hedge against thin technology-axis pool. |

Final axis confirmed once Chair shortlist narrows and the AC's collective skills profile is testable.

### 7.3 Complementarity to Chair shortlist

Run NED #2 funnel concurrently with Chair search but with a 4–6 week phase-lag on shortlist convergence. When the Chair shortlist narrows to 2–3 candidates, the NED #2 axis is locked. If the Chair search lands on a CA(SA)-strong candidate (the dominant SA shape), NED #2 axis = technology / cyber. If the Chair search lands instead on a banking-regulatory-strong candidate, NED #2 axis = financial-expertise (per §3.2 contingent must-have), and NED #3 takes the technology axis instead.

### 7.4 Substantive-independence diligence

Each shortlisted candidate undergoes a structured substantive-independence diligence: prior-employment-history review (3 years); related-party-transactions review (counterparties of Hoz); family-relationships disclosure; concurrent-directorship review; SARB / FSCA / JSE engagement-history review. The diligence is a defined Owen-substrate task (substrate gap §5 above).

### 7.5 Multi-entity fit-and-proper sequencing

Under Owen §7A.2 the candidate clears three fit-and-proper regimes simultaneously: bank, securities, group. PAX recommends the shortlist diligence run all three concurrently rather than sequentially — sequential diligence stretches the timeline by ~30% and risks losing candidates to other roles. The Sade-owned PROC-FAIS-KI-FAP-01 substrate (per-entity extension, surfaced in Owen §7A.5 as a v1 substrate task) is the correct substrate to capture this; PAX flags the substrate dependency.
