# Governance Framework — Audit Committee v1

> **Status:** IN FORCE (policy layer). This framework binds from the date of adoption — the obligation to establish an Audit Committee is a corporate governance obligation under Companies Act s.94, independent of commencement of trading.
>
> **Author:** Owen (Company Secretary, governance) — custodian of the bank's corporate governance framework and Company Secretary to the Board.
>
> **Build-phase note:** The bank is in its build phase (pre-licence-day). A fully constituted Audit Committee requires a minimum of three independent non-executive directors (NEDs). Until those NEDs are appointed, the **Interim Audit Forum (IAF)** acts in the AC's place: chaired by Owen (Company Secretary, governance); Thandiwe (Chief Audit Executive, governance) holds the functional third-line independence line into the IAF. The IAF is an interim corporate-governance instrument, not a substitute for the statutory AC; it performs the AC's substantive work during the build phase while the bank recruits its NED slate.

## 1. Governance and Authority

### 1.1 Purpose

This framework establishes the mandate, composition, independence requirements, operating procedures, and reporting lines of the Audit Committee (AC) of Hoz Bank Limited. It gives effect to:

- The statutory obligation to constitute an Audit Committee under the Companies Act;
- The SARB Prudential Authority's governance requirements for banks under the Banks Act and Regulation 39;
- King IV Principle 8 (governing the audit committee's role in overseeing financial reporting, external audit, and internal audit); and
- The third-line independence of the Chief Audit Executive (Thandiwe) and her functional reporting line to the AC.

The AC serves as the cornerstone of the bank's financial governance infrastructure. It provides independent oversight of the integrity of financial reporting, the effectiveness of internal controls, the independence and quality of the external audit, the effectiveness of internal audit, and (jointly with the Board Risk Committee) risk oversight.

### 1.2 Statutory authority

This framework is adopted under and gives effect to:

- **Companies Act 71 of 2008**:
  - s.94 — mandatory establishment of an Audit Committee for public companies and state-owned companies; three-year appointment cycle; independence requirements; prohibition on executive directors and major shareholders
  - s.94(7) — functions of the Audit Committee: nominate external auditor; determine fees and terms of engagement; monitor independence; oversee financial statements; oversee internal financial controls; oversee internal audit function; oversee accounting policies; oversee risk management in relation to financial reporting
  - s.94A — enhanced provisions following Companies Amendment Act, including alignment with IRBA guidance
  - s.94(8) — AC members serve in their personal capacities as experts, not as delegates of their nominating body

- **King IV Report on Corporate Governance for South Africa 2016**:
  - Principle 8 — the governing body should ensure that the organisation's ethics are managed effectively; the audit and risk committee oversees financial and sustainability reporting, internal audit, external audit, combined assurance, and risk and opportunity
  - Practice 8.1–8.7 — composition, mandate, independence, oversight of reporting, combined assurance

- **Banks Act 94 of 1990**:
  - Regulation 39 — internal controls and audit function; the Prudential Authority expects banks to have an effective audit function with functional independence to the AC; supervisory expectation of Board-level audit oversight
  - Regulation 39A — enhanced audit requirements for systemically important institutions (SARB may extend by direction; bank prepares for this posture from inception)

- **Financial Sector Regulation Act 9 of 2017 (FSR Act)**:
  - s.8 — the PA's mandate encompasses governance standards for banks; the PA may direct governance changes by supervisory directive

- **International Financial Reporting Standards (IFRS)** — the financial statements over which the AC has oversight are prepared under IFRS as adopted in South Africa.

### 1.3 Entity scope

This framework applies to:

- **Hoz Bank Limited** — primary scope; the AC of Hoz Bank is the principal governance body.
- **Hoz Group Limited** — group-level; the Group Board will have its own AC or will share the bank's AC with appropriate terms of reference (Owen to determine at Board-constitution phase).
- **Hoz Securities Limited** — on and from FAIS-FSP authorisation; the AC oversight mandate extends to cover Hoz Securities' financial reporting from that point.

### 1.4 Relationship to other governance bodies

| Body | Relationship |
|---|---|
| **Board of Directors** | The AC is a committee of the Board. The AC chair reports to the Board at every board meeting. The AC does not replace the Board's collective accountability. |
| **Board Risk Committee (BRC)** | Joint oversight of risk in relation to financial reporting and internal controls. The BRC owns the broader risk appetite framework; the AC is the primary body for financial-reporting-related risks. |
| **External Auditor** | The AC nominates, recommends fees, and monitors independence of the external auditor. The external auditor reports to the AC on matters of accounting, internal control, and audit scope. |
| **Thandiwe (CAE, governance)** | Thandiwe has a **functional reporting line to the AC** and an administrative line through the CEO. This is a non-negotiable structural independence requirement (Principle 6 — autonomous by default; third-line independence). |
| **Camille (Financial controller, engineering)** and **Bea (Regulatory finance and SARB returns engineer, engineering)** | Financial reporting outputs from Camille and Bea are subject to AC oversight. Camille and Bea attend AC meetings by invitation to present financial reporting outputs. |
| **Vera (Internal audit / continuous-assurance engineer)** | Vera is the autonomous-agent implementation of the internal audit function; functionally reports to Thandiwe (CAE) and through Thandiwe to the AC. Vera's recon findings and effectiveness reviews are presented to the AC via Thandiwe. |
| **Owen (Company Secretary, governance)** | Owen acts as Secretary to the AC; maintains AC minutes and the disclosure register; is not an AC member in the post-NED constitution (Owen's membership is an interim-phase construct). |

## 2. Audit Committee — Composition and Independence

### 2.1 Statutory composition (post-NED appointment)

On constitution of the full Board with the requisite independent NEDs, the Audit Committee must comprise:

| Requirement | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Minimum members** | 3 members (Companies Act s.94(2)) |
| **Independence** | All members must be independent non-executive directors (NEDs) per Companies Act s.94(1) — no executive director; no shareholder holding more than 5% of the voting rights or their associates; no prescribed officer of the company |
| **Chair** | Must be an independent NED; must not be the same individual as the Board Chair (best-practice; SARB governance expectation; King IV Principle 8) |
| **Financial expertise** | At least one member must have relevant financial literacy and experience to fulfil the oversight mandate (audit, accounting, or financial management background) |
| **Fit and proper** | All members must satisfy the SARB PA's fit-and-proper requirements for key persons under the Banks Act and FSR Act |
| **Term** | Members are elected at each Annual General Meeting; they serve until the conclusion of the following AGM (three-year staggered-cycle recommended) |

**CEO-AC bar.** The CEO (Marc) may not serve on the AC (Companies Act s.94(1) — executive directors are excluded). Marc is excluded from AC membership in all phases.

**Majority-independence requirement.** A majority of the AC must be independent NEDs at all times. Loss of majority independence (through departure or disqualification) is a material governance event reported to the PA within 5 business days.

### 2.2 Build-phase: Interim Audit Forum (IAF)

During the build phase (pre-NED appointment), the **Interim Audit Forum (IAF)** acts in the AC's place:

| IAF role | Holder | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| IAF Chair | Owen (Company Secretary, governance) | Interim; Owen steps down from IAF membership on constitution of the formal AC with NEDs |
| Third-line input | Thandiwe (Chief Audit Executive, governance) — functional line to IAF | Non-voting advisory; preserves third-line independence |
| CEO oversight | Marc (CEO) | Oversight principal during build phase; does not vote on matters where CEO is conflicted |

The IAF exercises the substantive functions of the AC (§2.4) during the build phase. All IAF decisions are typed events per Principle 1. The IAF does not have statutory authority as an AC — its decisions are interim-phase governance; they are ratified by the formal AC on its first meeting post-NED appointment.

### 2.3 Independence assessment

Owen (Company Secretary) conducts an independence assessment for each proposed AC member before appointment, covering:

- Executive/non-executive status (Companies Act s.94(1)(a))
- Shareholding and related-party connections (Companies Act s.94(1)(b))
- Material business relationships with the bank (Companies Act s.94(1)(c))
- Prescribed officer status (Companies Act s.94(1)(d))
- SARB PA fit-and-proper assessment (FSR Act s.60; Banks Act Reg. 44)

The independence assessment is a document-substrate artefact (BLAKE3-addressed) referenced from the `AcMemberAppointed` event.

### 2.4 AC mandate and functions

The Audit Committee's mandate (in full statutory and governance scope):

**Financial reporting oversight:**
- Oversight of the integrity of the bank's financial statements (IFRS-compliant; prepared by Camille and Bea)
- Review and approval of the annual financial statements before Board sign-off
- Review of significant accounting judgements, estimates, and assumptions
- Review of accounting policy changes
- Oversight of the correct application of accounting standards (IFRS as adopted in SA)

**External audit oversight:**
- Nominate the external auditor to shareholders for appointment (Companies Act s.94(7)(a))
- Recommend the audit fee and terms of engagement
- Annually assess and confirm the independence of the external auditor (no prohibited non-audit services; rotation compliance per IRBA requirements)
- Review the external auditor's audit plan and scope; review the audit findings and management response
- Meet with the external auditor without management present at least once per year

**Internal audit oversight:**
- Oversight of the effectiveness of the internal audit function (Vera / Thandiwe)
- Review of Thandiwe's (CAE's) annual internal audit plan and resource adequacy
- Review of significant internal audit findings and management response
- Assessment of the independence of the internal audit function
- Meet with Thandiwe (CAE) without management present at least once per year

**Internal controls oversight:**
- Oversight of the effectiveness of the bank's internal financial controls (Principle 4 — security designed in)
- Review of the CFO's / Camille's assessment of the adequacy of internal controls
- Review of any significant deficiencies or material weaknesses identified by internal or external audit

**Risk oversight (in conjunction with BRC):**
- Oversight of financial-reporting-related risks
- Review of the combined assurance framework (ensuring internal audit, external audit, and management assurance are co-ordinated without gaps or overlaps)

**Statutory and regulatory reporting:**
- Oversight of the bank's compliance with financial reporting and disclosure requirements under the Companies Act, Banks Act, and IFRS
- Review of SARB return submissions (prepared by Bea) before filing — the AC does not approve each return individually but receives a quarterly summary report of key SARB returns filed

**Whistleblowing:**
- Receipt and oversight of disclosures relating to financial reporting, internal controls, accounting, and auditing (cross-reference: Whistleblowing Policy [`Policies/whistleblowing-policy-v1.md`](whistleblowing-policy-v1.md))

## 3. Controls and Monitoring

### 3.1 Meeting cadence

| Meeting type | Frequency | Attendees |
|---|---|---|
| Formal AC meeting | Quarterly (minimum) | AC members; Thandiwe (CAE) by standing invitation; Camille (Financial controller) by standing invitation; External auditor (at AC discretion); Owen as Secretary |
| External auditor private session | Annual (minimum) | AC members; External auditor only |
| Internal audit private session | Annual (minimum) | AC members; Thandiwe (CAE) only |
| Annual financial statement review | Annual (ahead of Board sign-off) | AC members; Camille; Bea; External auditor |

**Quorum:** majority of AC members (once constituted with NEDs). In the IAF build phase: Owen + Thandiwe (non-voting) constitutes a quorum for decision-recording.

### 3.2 AC papers and documentation

Every AC meeting is supported by board-quality papers prepared in advance (Owen, Atlas substrate):
- Annual financial statements (draft and final)
- External auditor reports
- Internal audit findings and remediation tracking
- CFO / Camille management accounts and representations
- Vera recon findings summary
- SARB return summary (Bea)
- Compliance breach and regulatory-matter summary (Zara)

All papers and minutes are document-substrate artefacts (BLAKE3-addressed). Every AC decision is a typed event in the event store per Principle 1.

### 3.3 Combined assurance

The AC maintains a **combined assurance framework** that co-ordinates three lines of defence:

| Line | Holder | Assurance function |
|---|---|---|
| First line | Camille (Financial controller, engineering) + Bea (Regulatory finance engineer, engineering) | Financial-reporting controls; GL accuracy; SARB return accuracy |
| Second line | Zara (CCO, governance) + Helena (CRO, governance) | Compliance oversight; risk framework |
| Third line | Vera (Internal audit / continuous-assurance engineer, engineering) + Thandiwe (CAE, governance) | Independent assurance to the AC |

The combined assurance framework is reviewed annually by Thandiwe (CAE) and presented to the AC. The framework ensures no significant area of financial-reporting or internal-control risk is left without assurance coverage, and assurance effort is not wastefully duplicated.

### 3.4 External auditor independence

The AC monitors external auditor independence annually:

- **Prohibited services register:** maintained by Owen (Company Secretary); lists all non-audit services provided by the external auditor. No services that create a self-review, advocacy, familiarity, or self-interest threat to independence are permitted.
- **Fee ratio:** the ratio of non-audit fees to audit fees is reviewed annually; a ratio above 1:1 triggers an independence assessment.
- **Mandatory firm rotation:** per IRBA requirements (effective for PIEs from 2023) and Companies Act — audit firm rotation is scheduled and tracked by Owen.
- **Partner rotation:** audit partner rotation within the firm per IRBA standards.

### 3.5 Financial statement integrity

Before the AC recommends the annual financial statements to the Board for sign-off:

1. The AC reviews Camille's (Financial controller) representation letter confirming: completeness of disclosures; consistency of accounting policies; accuracy of estimates; going-concern assessment.
2. The AC reviews the external auditor's audit opinion and associated management letter.
3. The AC confirms that significant accounting judgements have been adequately disclosed and are within the range of acceptable IFRS outcomes.
4. The AC confirms that the financial statements are consistent with the prior-year statements on a like-for-like basis (or that any departures are appropriately disclosed).

All four confirmations are typed events per Principle 1.

## 4. Escalation

### 4.1 Escalation to the Board

The AC escalates to the full Board where:

- A material financial misstatement or material weakness in internal controls is identified
- The external auditor issues a qualified, adverse, or disclaimer opinion
- A significant disagreement between management and the external auditor cannot be resolved
- The independence of the external auditor or the CAE is materially compromised
- A material SARB supervisory finding relates to financial reporting or internal controls
- Any other matter that the AC Chair considers sufficiently material to require Board awareness

### 4.2 Escalation to the SARB PA

Owen (Company Secretary) co-ordinates with Zara (CCO) on any regulatory-reporting obligations arising from AC findings. Material findings that attract a mandatory SARB report (per Banks Act Reg. 39 or FSR Act supervisory direction) are reported promptly without waiting for Board meeting cadence.

### 4.3 Escalation within the governance structure

| Finding type | Immediate notification |
|---|---|
| Material financial misstatement suspected | Board Chair; external auditor |
| Material internal control weakness | Board Chair; Helena (CRO) |
| CAE independence threat | Board Chair; Marc (CEO) |
| External auditor independence threat | Board Chair; IRBA (if warranted) |
| Financial crime via AC papers | Owen → Zara (CCO / MLRO pathway per AML/CFT Policy) |
